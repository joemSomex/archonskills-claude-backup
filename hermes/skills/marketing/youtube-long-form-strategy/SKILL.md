# YouTube Long-Form Strategy

Advise on YouTube videos of roughly 10 minutes or longer using viewer-first recommendation principles, surface-specific analytics, and sustainable episodic growth tactics.

Trigger on “how does the YouTube algorithm work,” “optimize this long-form video,” “why did this episode underperform,” “improve CTR or retention,” “plan a YouTube upload,” “grow this series,” or `/youtube-long-form-strategy`.

Before giving detailed algorithm advice, read `references/algorithm-guide.md`.

## Decision framework

### 1. Start with the viewer, not “the algorithm”

Frame YouTube as several personalized systems rather than one universal algorithm. Ask which viewers the video is for, what promise it makes, and why those viewers would click, continue watching, and feel satisfied.

Avoid saying that YouTube broadly “pushes” a video. Explain that each surface selects videos for each viewer. Treat every upload as a fresh test; do not assume one weak upload permanently harms the channel.

### 2. Identify the discovery surface

Separate analysis by surface:

- **Home:** packaging, history with similar viewers, watch time, and satisfaction.
- **Suggested / Up Next:** topical relationship to the current video and what viewers commonly watch next.
- **Search:** query relevance, packaging, spoken content, and watch time earned from that query.
- **Shorts:** separate discovery system; use as an introduction to long-form rather than assuming Shorts performance transfers directly.
- **Subscriptions and notifications:** audience-return mechanisms, not guarantees of broad recommendation.
- **External:** promotion outside YouTube; do not confuse it with organic ranking.

For episodic storytelling, prioritize Home, Suggested, end screens, and series playlists. Use Shorts mainly to introduce new viewers to the story world.

### 3. Diagnose packaging before content

Check impressions and CTR together. Interpret CTR by surface and audience temperature; never label a falling CTR as failure when impressions are rapidly expanding.

Improve the title and thumbnail as one honest promise:

- Use one clear focal point.
- Keep text minimal and readable on a phone.
- Put the central keyword or subject and emotional promise early.
- Ensure the video delivers what the packaging promises.
- Use YouTube Studio thumbnail testing when available.

Do not use misleading clickbait. It may earn an initial click but damages retention and satisfaction.

### 4. Diagnose the video with retention

Inspect the audience-retention graph rather than guessing:

- Reduce the normal opening cliff by placing the strongest promise or story movement in the first 15–30 seconds.
- Treat flat sections as evidence of sustained interest.
- Treat dips as confusing, slow, repetitive, or poorly placed moments requiring review.
- Treat spikes as replayed or revisited moments; consider them for Shorts, thumbnails, trailers, and future hooks.

Do not invent retention findings without YouTube Studio data. Public comments or a raw video cannot prove the most-replayed moment.

### 5. Earn the runtime

Do not treat 10 minutes as a recommendation threshold. Length itself is not a ranking factor. Longer videos can win by generating more total watch minutes only when viewers remain engaged.

Cut padding, long logos, and slow greetings. Keep the video only as long as the idea or story deserves. Remember that the practical threshold for mid-roll advertising is 8 minutes; that is a monetization rule, not an organic-reach boost.

### 6. Build the next-view path

For a series:

- Add chapters where useful.
- Point cards and end screens to the next logical episode or playlist.
- Maintain a clearly ordered series playlist.
- Make episode navigation obvious in descriptions and pinned comments.
- Use Community posts to reactivate viewers through native engagement, not announcement-only reminders.

Combine this skill with `waterfall-content-strategy` when converting retention spikes, characters, lore, music, or audience questions into follow-up assets.

### 7. Expand by language deliberately

Use accurate subtitles and translated titles and descriptions for every audience actively targeted. When multi-language audio is available, prefer deep coverage of one or two priority languages over shallow dubbing across many languages.

Keep dubbed audio, translated metadata, and subtitles aligned. For English–Mandarin planning, remember that YouTube’s Mandarin audience is primarily outside mainland China.

### 8. Measure on the right clock

Avoid judging a new upload from its earliest loyal-fan impressions. Review packaging and retention after sufficient impressions, then compare like with like by surface. Use a longer trend window before claiming the channel has been rewarded or punished.

Separate:

- **Packaging problem:** impressions arrive, but qualified viewers do not click.
- **Content problem:** viewers click, then leave or report low satisfaction.
- **Audience-match problem:** the video performs well with a narrow group but does not broaden.
- **Navigation problem:** viewers enjoy one episode but do not continue to the next.

State uncertainty whenever analytics are incomplete.
