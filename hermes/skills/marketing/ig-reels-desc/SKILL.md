# Instagram Reels descriptions - working draft

Draft and revise Instagram Reel captions/descriptions for Somex Studios' Quantum 13. This is an initial baseline, not a finalized house format. Incorporate further user examples, performance data, and preferences before approval. Do not promote to approved without explicit user instruction.

## When to Use

Use for an Instagram Reel description, caption, caption variations, or a review of existing Reel copy. Do not apply this skill to YouTube metadata, Stories, carousels, publishing, or scheduling. Leave calendar placement to `somex-content-calendar`.

## Inputs and source checks

- Identify the Reel's actual subject, target viewer, and purpose: new-viewer discovery, character connection, discussion, or a path to the released series. Reuse information already provided; ask only for missing facts that affect the copy.
- For episode clips, obtain episode and cut identifier, scene summary and start/end anchors or a transcript. Accept these instead of requiring a video upload. Check only the matching produced-episode summary and subtitle span. Do not infer the full cut from an opening screenshot. If visual verification matters, request the relevant frame or clip and disclose coverage limits.
- Use `quantum-13-canon` to verify continuity when required. Public copy must stay within released material or explicitly cleared previews. Avoid spoilers beyond the actual Reel, including later events from the same episode. Treat theories as theories and never mine unreleased character-bible details for public revelations.
- Read `references/buffer-baseline.md` when using the article's strategy guidance. Distinguish article advice, user requirements, actual channel results, and experiments. Never claim a caption caused reach, retention, or sends without suitable evidence.

## Draft the caption

1. Lead with a concrete, understandable scene hook or observation. Use relevant character names and natural topic words so a newcomer can understand the Reel. Do not keyword-stuff, invent stakes, or substitute generic hype for what the footage shows.
2. Write natural, scannable copy that gives the viewer a reason to care. Match length to the idea and user feedback; no fixed word count or mandatory short/long format has been approved. Use ASCII-safe punctuation. Use `Quantum 13` by default for new Instagram copy unless the user supplies a different exact brand treatment.
3. Aim for genuine conversation or share-worthiness where appropriate, not engagement bait. Never append automatic 'save this' or 'send this' commands. The user selected this episode-discovery CTA; preserve its wording when using it: "Want to see what happens next? Find the latest episode through our bio link." Use it for episode-highlight captions where appropriate, without adding a competing comment prompt by default. The planned bio destination is a Carrd containing all social-media links and the newest episode link. Its live placement has not been verified: drafts may use the CTA with a separate readiness note, but confirm the bio link and latest-episode destination before treating the copy as publish-ready. Do not invent a Carrd URL or copy YouTube link blocks or its fixed title suffix into Instagram captions.
4. Use only relevant hashtags if requested or useful; no fixed hashtag set or count has been approved for this skill. Check current official platform limits before presenting a numerical limit as a rule. Do not transfer YouTube's six-tag line automatically.
5. Preserve user-selected wording unless revision is requested. For a revision, change the requested component rather than restarting ideation. Return one caption by default; provide meaningfully different options when asked, respecting explicit counts. These defaults remain adjustable during development.

## Humanizer editing pass

Before delivering or revising a caption, load `humanizer` and apply its editing principles to the copy. Keep the Buffer strategy baseline; humanization changes the writing, not the evidence or platform rules.

- Match the user's supplied Kara sample: plain, empathetic prose, concrete character details, and naturally varied sentences. Preserve exact wording the user asks to keep; do not rewrite a protected passage just to satisfy a style heuristic.
- Remove redundant summaries, inflated stakes, generic hype, forced metaphors, mechanical three-point takeaways, and repetitive labeled bullets. Do not repeat a paragraph's meaning in bullets and again in the closing. A requested list/layout can remain, but each point must add something distinct. Treat three points as optional unless explicitly requested.
- Do not copy emojis from a layout example or add decorative emojis by default. Keep straight quotes/apostrophes and ASCII-safe punctuation.
- Keep questions and CTAs specific and relevant. Do not append 'Let me know in the comments!' automatically; retain it when explicitly requested or protected.
- Preserve character facts, emotional nuance, spoiler boundaries, names, relevant natural keywords, and requested format. Do not invent personal opinions, studio experiences, audience reactions, dialogue, or extra story details to add 'soul'. Humanizer patterns are editing cues, not forbidden-word rules or proof of AI authorship.
- Draft, audit for remaining formulaic patterns, and revise internally. For this caption workflow return only the finished caption unless the user asks to see the audit or alternatives. Do not force the humanizer's standalone draft/audit/final presentation into every caption response.

## Efficient review and output

Keep retrieval narrow. Prefer supplied boundaries, concise transcripts and cached verified scene notes over full videos or large canon dumps. If a video is supplied, inspect a small timestamped sample and expand only where uncertain; do not claim full visual coverage from sparse sampling. Do not send audio externally without user approval. Return the requested copy in chat without an analysis log or unsolicited file. Flag only material missing evidence, spoilers, permissions, or destination uncertainty.

Before delivery check: clip fidelity, names, spoiler scope, natural readability, exact user wording, absence of invented studio/release claims, and any requested hashtag or length constraint. Output remains a draft; do not schedule or publish.

## Learn from results

Use Somex's own Reel data when provided: non-follower reach for discovery, watch time for viewing, and likes/shares relative to reach for response. Compare comparable formats, topics, and evaluation windows. Do not assume caption text alone improves video retention or that all shares equal private DM sends. Record hypotheses separately from observations. Keep Sunday off and existing calendar rules intact; an article does not authorize cadence changes.
