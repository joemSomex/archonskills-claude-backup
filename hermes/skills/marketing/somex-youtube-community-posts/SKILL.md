## Compose

1. Briefly critique a user-supplied idea before rewriting it: identify its primary community purpose, intended audience (newcomers or current viewers), why a fan would care, what participation it invites, and whether the question is specific and interesting. Without a named target, propose a mix of newcomer-friendly and current-viewer ideas over time, labeling each idea's audience and context needs. Explain a weak idea plainly and propose a better one, rather than agreeing by reflex.
2. Choose a native format by purpose, not a quota: a poll is a low-friction vote with explicit answer choices; an approved still can anchor a scene observation; text can carry a self-contained discussion, verified studio observation, announcement, or update **without ending in a question**. When asked for a week or set of Community ideas, map each idea's purpose and audience first, then vary formats when they suit those ideas and approved assets. Do not default every Community post to a question, copy a generic Monday/Wednesday/Sunday template, or invent a studio milestone. Poll votes reflect participating viewers, not the entire audience or guaranteed future video views. An image or behind-the-scenes claim needs an actually approved public asset. Read `references/community-craft.md` for format and comment checks.
3. Keep general posts spoiler-light. Label episode-specific discussions and avoid major reveals until the user clears them even if the episode has been released. An unreleased glimpse requires explicit public-release approval. Apply spoiler and canon checks to poll options, images, and implicit wording, not just the main copy.
4. Write substantive but scannable Community copy: give the viewer enough released-episode context or a human observation to feel invited into the discussion, then ask one natural question. Do not mistake 'concise' for a two-line prompt; length should be earned by the idea, not fixed. The user's draft-copy samples show fuller, paragraph-broken, energetic *announcements* when there is verified news, not a mandate for every reflective post to use all caps or emoji. The memory-choice test resonated because it posed a relatable dilemma; a strained close reading of a single quoted word felt forced, so reject that style unless the moment genuinely supports it. Let a post breathe without padding it with lore exposition, invented emotion, or multiple repetitive questions. Match the register to the purpose: playful excitement for actual milestones, curious warmth for discussion. Avoid generic marketing hype, fake controversy, generic calls to comment, and pretending the account is an ordinary fan. Use ASCII-safe straight quotes and apostrophes in new copy. Do not append a CTA if the prompt already invites a response.
5. Separate observed comments from inference and studio replies from audience sentiment. Do not turn one comment into 'the community thinks'. Obtain the user's approval **and the creator's permission** before featuring identifiable comments, usernames, theories, art, or edits. Suggest a follow-up only when actual replies warrant one.

## Two-stage ideation and drafting

1. **Concept selection first.** When the user asks for Community posts on specified days, provide **ten distinct, concise content concepts per requested day** (e.g., ten Tuesday ideas and ten Friday ideas), each with a topic/angle, purpose, suggested native format, and any important spoiler or asset caveat. Do not write full post copy, poll choices, or wording variants yet. Mix formats where appropriate; do not make every concept a question or disguise a trivia quiz as a poll. Respect Friday 23:00 GMT+8 and Sunday off. Invite the user to choose one concept for each day or refine the shortlist.
2. **Variation only after selection.** Once the user picks a concept for a day, draft **five complete, substantively different content variations for that selected concept**, in the agreed native format. If the user selects concepts for both Monday and Friday, produce five variations for each selected post. Preserve its subject and verified release/spoiler scope; vary entry point, emotional stake, participation mechanic, or treatment rather than making superficial paraphrases. If a poll is selected, each variation includes a publishable poll setup and distinct voting choices, not an open-ended text question. Wait for the user's selection before this stage; never auto-advance from concept suggestions to full drafts in the same response. An explicit request for a different count or to skip a stage overrides this default process.
3. If the user instead supplies a single chosen idea or asks directly for copy, treat the concept as already selected and provide five variations without re-running discovery. A request to assess an existing draft calls for critique first, not unsolicited replacement copy. Preserve exact user-provided wording unless asked to revise.

## Schedule and deliver

- For a dated suggestion, respect the current calendar: at least one Community post every Friday at **23:00 GMT+8**, Sunday off; optional additional posts may be useful. Vary Friday formats according to purpose - no default poll or open-question quota. Test any additional timing against Somex's own results rather than importing an article's ideal days or hours. Community posts do not count as videos for the video-spacing rule. A copy-only request need not include dates.
- When drafting full copy in stage two, keep a short shared assessment if useful, then present the five ready-to-review variants; for copy-only requests, omit padded strategy notes. Quiz-style polls must have one unambiguous defensible answer if framed as factual trivia; opinion polls must not imply false facts. Label every proposal **draft**; never claim it was approved, queued, or published.
- Link to a verified released episode only when it directly helps a viewer follow the specific discussion or find the scene; never require a video link in every post. Before returning, check every factual claim, public-release boundary, terminology, quote, implied theory confirmation, visual/permission need, and whether the prompt is worth answering. Label all copy **draft**. Never claim a post was approved, queued, or published.
- When the user supplies post results, review each idea against its stated purpose and format: poll participation, substance of replies, returning discussion, and relevant episode traffic when measurable. Compare like with like and, if a useful run of data exists, review the format mix over roughly a month rather than declaring a winner from one post. Watch for repetitive shallow responses; vary the next question or format if needed. Do not infer that votes caused video views, present vote totals as audience consensus, or claim an algorithmic lift without evidence. This is post-review guidance, not a reply-drafting workflow or a required analytics report for every copy request.
