## What the examples show

- These are **full Community post drafts**, not one-line prompts: an attention-grabbing opener, a concrete point or announcement, a beat that acknowledges fan anticipation or support, and a closing thought or invitation. Paragraph breaks make longer copy scannable. Do not apply every component as a formula; make the length earn its place.
- Somex's suggested announcement voice can be loud and playful when there is **real confirmed news**: selective caps, rhythm, occasional emoji, direct fan address, and 'we hear you' phrasing. The more restrained versions and emphatic versions are alternatives, not a requirement to choose the loudest. A reflective story question should not inherit the same all-caps announcement voice by default.
- The merchandise drafts ask for concrete fan ideas, naming example product forms and character/world inspiration. `Stickers` appears as one *hypothetical merch idea* in an unused draft, **not** as an Instagram Story format or an existing product. Do not infer merch will launch, product types are approved, or that this question needs to be repeated.
- The milestone draft celebrates a specific stated achievement and thanks watchers, sharers, commenters, and theorists. It demonstrates genuine gratitude when a milestone is verified, but old numbers and future-release promises are not evergreen facts.

## Guardrails when reusing patterns

- The 'next episode will be ONE HOUR LONG' line is a **draft claim**, not a verified current runtime or release commitment. Do not use it without fresh explicit confirmation. Likewise, 'more details coming soon', unseen behind-the-scenes work, monetization, member perks, or merchandise must be verified for the proposed post.
- Avoid padding an engaging dilemma with repeated declarations, generic hype, and stacked CTAs just to match announcement length. Instead add a natural context beat, a real choice/tension, and one meaningful invitation. Do not force literary close reading of a line that did not naturally provoke discussion.
- When the user shares a candidate, preserve their chosen wording and emotional register unless asked to revise it. Compare only like with like: announcement drafts guide announcements; discussion drafts need their own natural voice. The source document explicitly notes that other topics were discussed but their exact wording was unavailable; never reconstruct or attribute those drafts as if archived here.
