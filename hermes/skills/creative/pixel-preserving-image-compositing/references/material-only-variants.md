# Material-only variants of an immutable emblem

1. Resolve the actual master and every assigned material source by visible content, not message order. If a chat mirror omits AVIF or other attachments, fetch the original message's attachment manifest and retrieve the missing file before rendering; partial attachment paths are not evidence that the reference is absent. Convert AVIF to PNG only for inspection, without altering the master.
2. Read the master's exact dimensions, RGBA alpha, and pixel geometry. Use the supplied master itself, not a visually similar earlier design or regenerated rendition. Keep one fixed canvas, placement, crop, viewing angle and light-direction structure. If the master is opaque, define the foreground mask carefully; if it is RGBA, preserve its alpha array byte-for-byte, including antialiased edge values.
3. For each material reference, derive a dark-to-highlight RGB ramp and roughness/microtexture from its surface, excluding the reference object's silhouette and broad sphere shading. Apply the ramp as `out_rgb = interp(master_luminance, knots, material_rgb_ramp)` to the SAME master for every rank. Preserve the master luminance topology so bevels, gaps, central motif, bolts and scratches stay registered; add only low-amplitude high-pass material texture (e.g. reference grayscale minus a Gaussian blur) inside the existing metal. Do not paste a sphere, its background, or its whole illumination gradient over the badge. For repeated masters with the same four material sources, parameterize the master path and per-run output directory in one reusable render script rather than hand-editing hard-coded paths each time; otherwise a stale path can silently recolor the previous badge or overwrite its outputs.
   For transparent metallic avatar-frame recolors with several existing materials, inspect opaque-pixel HSV samples from each rail, inset, light strip, and recess before choosing a recipe. Derive smooth material weights from the *source* hue/saturation (and a brightness floor), render each requested metal separately at the source pixel's original HSV value, then interpolate the candidate RGB colors by those weights and renormalize each mixed pixel to its original value. Do not interpolate gold and silver hue numerically in HSV: the path crosses green/cyan, producing off-theme seams. Keep deep recesses near black, desaturate specular glints, preserve original RGB where alpha is zero, and assert output alpha and HSV value equal the source arrays; this preserves the original light/shadow and surface relief while recoloring red/violet/cyan details without a flat overlay. Check off-palette hues only on visible, adequately lit pixels, then inspect every final frame on dark and light backings.
4. Treat a multichrome/iridescent reference as reflective spectral coloration confined to the master's exposed planes; use smooth spatial hue modulation while retaining source shadows and relief. Compare the result with EACH assigned sample at thumbnail size. If a ramp still reads as paint or metal instead of refractive crystal, generate a reference-driven surface candidate with the master as sole geometry authority and the material sample as the exclusive appearance source. Inspect its color separation and reflections, then uniformly register it to the original canvas and use its RGB as texture only, never its silhouette. For centered, same-view badges, obtain the source foreground box from alpha and the candidate box from its background contrast; fit one isotropic scale and a center translation between those boxes before sampling candidate RGB. Before compositing a multi-badge batch, compare each candidate with its own source at thumbnail and native scale; reject or regenerate a candidate that changes the central symbol, page/loop count, speech-bubble shape, or any seam, even if its outer badge looks right. Check those same inner landmarks after isotropic registration: the original alpha can match exactly while a generator's altered symbol leaks through an opaque inner disk, and independent width/height stretching can conceal the shift. For a specifically nonmetal crystal target, replace the old metal RGB on exposed surfaces rather than blending the candidate over the original silver ramp: derive candidate chroma from `candidate_rgb/max(candidate_luma,12)`, compute a target luminance from softly blurred source relief mixed with candidate luminance, multiply chroma by that target, then protect navy/dark structural wells with a feathered source-relief mask. Tune the luminance mix per badge and inspect at full size; source scratches in a recessed well can otherwise become bright faceted shards. Preserve source alpha and transparent RGB exactly. For a metallic hologram target instead, a masked blend with the original derived ramp may remain appropriate. Restrict strong transfer to actual exposed components with a feathered source-structure mask, while allowing shadowed outer plates that are still exposed to inherit the crystal material. Reject hard masking seams at native size, and assert source alpha and fully transparent RGB are byte-identical. Reject pasted reference-object silhouettes, duplicate contours, external glow, rainbow halos, and geometry drift; a generator's persuasive surface does not establish pixel-exact construction.
   For an approved finish applied across many different emblems, calibrate once against the approved appearance and reuse ONE color recipe for the entire batch. For a one-badge review of a selected finish on a new emblem, use the new unmodified badge as the only geometry source and the selected example as appearance evidence only; when the central symbol or cutouts differ, generate a separate surface candidate from the new badge rather than copying RGB or icon topology from the approved example. Register that candidate by isotropic foreground-box fit, re-shade its colors through the new badge's relief and exact alpha, and inspect every loop, bolt, symbol and negative-space opening at native size on light and dark mattes; alpha equality alone cannot detect a redrawn inner emblem. Derive a luminance-to-color ramp from the original/approved pair; obtain a low-frequency chroma field by Gaussian-blurring the approved-minus-ramp residual through a lit-material mask, normalized by the blurred mask. Apply that field to each new master according to its own luminance, not the example's edges or symbol. If the contact sheet still reads as uniform blue metal, add broad cyan/mint and pink/lilac reflection regions at fixed canvas coordinates, normalize each hue field to the ramp luminance, and blend it only onto mid-lit planes; taper color out of deep recesses and near-white glints. This preserves each source's relief while making the approved iridescence visible and consistent across different icons. Process a bounded batch at a time, append each source-to-output record to a manifest, then verify its count, labels, file hashes, dimensions, alpha equality and legibility against every supplied original before delivery; filename numbering need not be sequential.
5. For material-only variants, reuse exactly the original alpha for each PNG and keep transparent pixels untouched. When the user explicitly requests an external glow or cosmic atmosphere instead, follow `localized-effects.md`: preserve the opaque emblem's placement and geometry, put mist/rays in a separate low-alpha layer outside its silhouette, and verify the new alpha on both dark and light preview mattes. A white color ramp alone reads as silver metal, not a radiant halo; conversely, flooding a dark central glyph with a generic elliptical white patch reads pasted on and destroys its depth. Programmatically assert same dimensions, alpha-array equality, unchanged transparent pixels, independent output count, and distinct file hashes. Inspect a matched-scale contact sheet and each questionable badge at native size and website-thumbnail scale for material differentiation, central symbol legibility, and accidental flattening or new marks.
6. Describe this as a controlled material translation, not a physically exact reconstruction of a 3D shader: a rendered sphere supplies appearance cues but not the badge's true BRDF or underlying mesh. When the user asks to review finishes before updating a reusable skill, deliver only the separate standalone candidates, keep any contact sheet internal unless explicitly requested, and wait for explicit approval before modifying the skill; otherwise the skill can freeze an unapproved look. When packaging an approved finish for reuse, store the approved appearance and raw material as appearance-only assets, parameterize source/candidate/output in the compositor, and replay it on at least two badges with different central geometry. Require output hashes to match accepted previews and verify the packaged archive contains the exact assets and script; a generic skill is not proven by one emblem or an untested ZIP. Encode a general material-transfer workflow, never a one-off badge or transient output paths.
