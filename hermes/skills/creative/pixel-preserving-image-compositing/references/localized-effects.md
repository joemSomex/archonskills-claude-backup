# Localized additive effects

Use this recipe when the user wants particles, glow, haze, sparks, grain, or another additive effect in one region while the source face, framing, crop, lighting, and existing design must remain unchanged.

## Decision rule

Use deterministic raster editing rather than image-to-image generation whenever unchanged content is a hard requirement. Generative editors can redraw identity, pose, crop, and lighting across the full frame even when prompted not to.

## Procedure

1. Decode the source once with Pillow and keep that decoded RGB/RGBA image as the comparison baseline.
2. Measure the permitted edit region in original pixel coordinates. Prefer a hard rectangle for negative-space additions; use a grayscale mask for irregular regions.
3. Create a same-size transparent RGBA overlay. Draw only the new effect on this overlay.
4. Match the nearby source effect rather than inventing a new style:
   - sample its dominant colors;
   - reproduce particle-size and opacity ranges;
   - follow its flow direction and density gradient;
   - taper alpha near path ends and mask boundaries;
   - use a faint blurred under-layer beneath crisp particles so the effect feels embedded.
5. Hard-mask the overlay to the allowed region before compositing. Keep particles clear of faces, eyes, product marks, and other locked elements.
6. Alpha-composite overlay over the decoded source and save as PNG at the original dimensions.
7. For a second reference used as a faint background texture, cover-resize and center-crop it to the target canvas, then use a low-opacity screen blend through a combined mask: `allowed spatial region × dark-source luminance`. This keeps luminous strands and bokeh visible in black negative space without repainting the subject.
8. For broad colored glow, render the radial or elliptical glow on its own RGBA layer, blur that layer, and then multiply its final alpha by the same combined background mask. Mask after blur because blur expands beyond the original bounds and can relight the face or hair. For a transparent emblem, keep the original opaque subject fully opaque and alpha-composite the badge over a separate translucent glow/mist layer; compare both dark and light mattes, since a pale halo can disappear on white or look like a flat ring on black. If the reference is an orb of light but the target contains a dark central symbol, prioritize that symbol's thumbnail legibility rather than painting an unrelated white ellipse into its recess.
9. Verify zero changed pixels outside the allowed region with `scripts/verify_region_preservation.py`. Also inspect the full diff bounding box when a subject-protection contour is stricter than the rectangular edit region.
10. Apply the review mode in SKILL.md: skip unsolicited contact sheets and exhaustive visual review for this user; when a specific defect check is requested, inspect that feature on each final variant. When broader comparative QA is requested, inspect a temporary contact sheet. Compare density, balance, negative space, accidental rings/wings/symbols, texture visibility, glow spill, and whether every variation still satisfies the same locked framing. For a large effect slate, reserve visibly different light topologies (orbit, axial ray, asymmetric mist, filament field, sparse particles) and compare the illuminated subject as well as its surrounding halo; different hashes, labels, or tiny shifts in corona intensity are not distinct variations. Group near-duplicates on the contact sheet and replace each failed slot from the original references with a different image-space light direction and intensity distribution. Reject any slot that still reads as the source metal, or whose overexposed core erases small anchors such as a cart grid, tag hole, or central symbol at website-thumbnail size; correct that slot alone, then rebuild and recheck the full sheet. Never deliver the contact sheet.

## Release effects and thin-string artifact checks

1. Use the user's selected corrected image as the next batch's common base. Measure the actual prop contact point on that image; do not reuse coordinates from another crop or trust a prompt's coordinate guesses. For archery, locate the arrow rest immediately above the bow-holding glove, both bow tips, and the released drawing hand.
2. Separate the old projectile/string cleanup, changed release-hand/string geometry, and new effect into distinct masks. Remove the entire obsolete arrowhead, shaft, wake, rings and bloom before moving the trajectory; relighting the old path leaves a second apparent shot. Keep preserved face, hair, armor and background outside those masks.
3. State the action phase explicitly: after release, the drawing fingers no longer pinch a shaft and the string rebounds toward the bow plane. For a pure photon streak, omit solid shafts, triangular heads and fins. By default, make the light originate at the measured rest and continue forward, rather than below the hand or backward through the torso. If the user explicitly asks for a chest-start trail, extend that visual wake backward to the chest while keeping chest, measured arrow rest above the glove, and forward beam on one coherent straight axis; do not move the firing point below the grip. Place shockwaves and particle flow around that same axis. Specify the bowstring's occlusion at the extended bow-holding forearm separately: when the user requires the near-side string, it must remain visibly in front of that forearm at the crossing, not disappear behind it or pass through the armor.
4. Depict one physical bowstring as one continuous translucent motion-blur band attached to the bow tips. Avoid requesting several discrete string afterimages when the user forbids extra strings; the generator can interpret those as additional taut strings.
5. Inspect each requested final variant specifically for stray bright AND dark strings, including both attachment tips, the space beside the blur, face/hair crossings, and the lower span behind the arm. A luminous blurred string can conceal a separate dark taut line against the city. Prompt exclusions and file hashes do not establish its absence.
6. If local repair is necessary, trace the complete unwanted line separately on each aligned image, include its antialiased edge, and protect bow hardware and foreground hands. Black-hat filtering can help locate dark slender features but must not replace tracing: thresholds can miss low-contrast spans and select legitimate background rails. Test localized `cv2.inpaint` only through the measured line mask; reject smoothed architecture or severed hardware. Do not apply guessed broad rectangles or one unregistered polyline across a whole batch.
7. Reopen every repaired final export and inspect the named defect after the LAST write, then rerun count, format, dimensions and applicable outside-mask pixel checks. Describe only verified corrections; an attempted cleanup is not proof that all extra strings disappeared.

## Standalone streak assets and local arc edits

1. Assign an action-scene reference to effect appearance only when the user requests just its streak on white. Exclude the character, prop, strings, environment, annotations and presentation borders. Keep the requested streak direction, glow color and pulse-ring rhythm; use a white-hot core with saturated colored edges so it remains legible on white. A white-background asset is not a transparent cutout.
2. Treat an arrowhead-free streak as pure light: remove the complete solid or outlined head, triangular planes, fins and shaft, and terminate the light with a natural soft taper. Do not replace a removed arrowhead with a smaller geometric tip.
3. Inventory the main straight beam, small elliptical pulse rings, large crescent, sparks and bloom as separate components before changing one. A circle around the crescent identifies that component, not permission to erase every small ring or beam segment inside the annotation.
4. For an in-place crescent reversal, state its current and desired opening explicitly, for example `(` opening right becomes `)` opening left. Lock the crescent envelope/center and beam trajectory independently; reverse only the crescent, not the entire canvas or the diagonal streak. For removal, include both horns, the full curved backbone, curved wisps and residual bloom in the cleanup mask while protecting the beam and small rings.
5. Recover an unmarked base by comparing decoded candidate pixels outside a dilated annotation-color mask. Search the relevant original asset outputs as well as the latest variants; the upload may annotate an older version. A low global error is only a candidate match: check protected features and local differences before replacing the upload. Use the unmarked asset as the edit source and never include the colored annotation in generation.
6. When reconstruction is needed where an arc crosses the beam, use an aligned generated cleanup as donor pixels only inside the complete arc envelope. Composite onto the untouched base through a narrow inward-feathered mask and verify outside-mask preservation. Do not infer that a whole-side rectangular transfer preserves a small overlapping pulse ring; protect it separately or disclose that it was reconstructed.
7. For a requested local correction, inspect the final export for the named defect: no crescent ghosts or annotation remnants, unbroken diagonal beam, retained small-ring count, and the intended arc opening when reversed. For ordinary variation batches, retain the direct-delivery/count/format checks without adding unsolicited contact sheets or broad review loops.

## Front-mounted translucent energy barriers

1. Read the machine's actual forward direction from its body, muzzle, or travel geometry, then mark the intended shield plane **ahead of** that geometry. Sample the original pixels at a proposed emitter contact: the projector must land on real chassis hardware, not stop in white negative space. Reserve a visible connector from chassis to shield edge when the field would otherwise float.
2. Keep a locked original-sized canvas. Build each barrier on a transparent RGBA overlay at several times the source resolution: a low-alpha colored pane, structural outer edges and nodes, then a clipped wire lattice **only if the effect reference calls for one**. Render bloom on a separate layer. A white studio backdrop needs enough local blue/cyan contrast to reveal the plane, while the original machinery must still show through it.
3. Blur the glow layer, bound it to the allowed forward-effect envelope, and downsample **the overlay alone** before compositing over the decoded original. Resizing the full composite can change protected pixels even when the effect itself is localized. Never copy annotation strokes or circles from a placement reference into the design.
4. Export lossless PNG at the source dimensions, compare decoded RGB against the original for zero changed pixels beyond the allowed shield/connector/glow region, and inspect the pane, actual machine contact, transparency, and any edge clipping at native size. For multiple requested designs, vary the barrier's overall silhouette and folding/curvature rather than merely its grid spacing or color.

## Small illuminated openings in locked artwork

For a new slit, triangular eye port, or tiny illuminated recess on an otherwise unchanged character/product, inspect a native-resolution crop and measure each opening independently against the actual surface contours. Render at several times the original resolution: first a dark inset silhouette, then a narrow material-colored bevel, then a low-intensity colored core clipped to a smaller interior polygon. Blur a separate faint halo, downsample, and **hard-mask the entire overlay after blur/downsampling** to the measured edit region; otherwise glow alters protected pixels outside the aperture. For a slender inverted triangle, keep the top edge short and the downward tip distinctly longer, rather than using a broad equilateral triangle. Build each requested variant from the same untouched source, varying only permitted port geometry or illumination. Compare the decoded source/output pixels for zero difference outside the edit region, then inspect both the magnified apertures and full-body image: numerical preservation does not prove the ports align with the implied eyes or that the red light remains dim.

## Luminous material transfer versus additive glow

When the target is a metallic emblem but the requested effect makes the emblem itself translucent light, treat it as a material conversion, not just an additive halo. Calibrate one core at thumbnail size before making a slate; a luminance tint retains the old specular relief, while a hard dark/light threshold can turn scratches into false black markings. If the core still reads as metal, use an OpenAI reference-driven edit with the original emblem as the design anchor and the light/orb reference explicitly limited to material and atmosphere. State that **every visible plane** must become diffuse internally lit opal/glass, and forbid metal, chrome, and copied orb geometry. This can produce the desired appearance, but a full-frame render is not pixel-exact: register and mask its light texture through the original silhouette and cutouts before claiming exact geometry, or disclose that geometry is approximate. Inspect the core, seams, outer silhouette, and central negative space separately at native size and thumbnail scale. For a reference with intense electric-white edges and dense branching filaments, make filament density, saturated blue sparks, and crispness explicit acceptance gates; a soft blue mist is not equivalent. When masking a generated texture onto an exact emblem, retain unblurred candidate pixels inside the original alpha and use a softened candidate only for the outside aura, with the original cutouts protected. Inspect for doubled rims and an overly uniform white halo at native size; matching the silhouette mask alone does not validate every seam.

For a transparent energy emblem, inspect the **actual alpha** of both badge and effect reference before editing: a black-looking reference may already have useful translucent wisps, while a generator's visible checkerboard may be baked into opaque RGB. Use the original badge alpha and luminance as the geometry authority. When a collection needs matching clean edges and palette, render independent OpenAI texture candidates against a working black backdrop, then clip their luminance to each original source mask rather than accepting generated silhouettes. Normalize candidate luminance using percentiles of source-identified lit faces to a shared cool-white response curve; use one fixed shadow/mid/highlight palette across all badges. Uniformly scale a naturally smaller source and its aligned candidate together only when collection-scale consistency is requested, keeping its proportions. Bound a narrow low-alpha glow by distance from the original alpha silhouette and assert zero alpha beyond that limit; do not layer reference smoke or particles when the user forbids them. Preview an actual alpha composite on dark and light mattes at native size: a raw RGBA viewer may display unpremultiplied pale RGB in nearly transparent pixels as a falsely solid blue outline. For each source, obtain an independently generated nonmetallic light rendition as a **texture candidate**, ideally on a uniform black working background for reliable exclusion; resize/register it to the untouched source, but never use its silhouette or background as final alpha. A luminance recolor plus a halo is not enough when the target itself must become pearlescent energy: at thumbnail size it still reads as metal. Transfer the candidate's bright opal/filament texture substantially onto the source's structural surfaces while retaining an aligned contribution from the original relief to keep seams, cube facets and symbols readable; map dark recessed planes separately to deep blue shadow. Where a candidate has an opaque baked checkerboard, reject its near-neutral periodic pixels before transfer and keep the source-mask color there; never extract those candidate background pixels as atmosphere. Extract exterior wisps from the master only outside the source silhouette and away from the master's own subject icon, or derive a softly blurred halo from the source shape; attenuate every exterior alpha to zero at the canvas edges. Put sparse hairline trails and sparks behind the original badge, not across defining symbols. Preserve every originally opaque source pixel as opaque, allow only translucent new glow in original transparent space, and keep true structural cutouts distinguishable from mist. Export RGBA PNG at the exact requested dimensions; assert alpha contains zero, partial, and opaque pixels and all four edges are zero-alpha. Inspect the final files on dark and light mattes at native and thumbnail sizes for matching core brightness/palette across a collection, readable source-specific details, no doubled rims, dirty fringes, opaque rectangles or clipped trails. Source alpha preservation proves outer geometry only; if inner seams came from a generative candidate rather than the original, disclose that they are approximate.

## Particle-arc guidance

For a subtle half-enclosure, use one or more open cubic Bézier paths outside the subject contour. Scatter narrow particle lanes around each path normal, fade density at both ends, and leave the arc visibly open. Vary density emphasis by region rather than changing the base image: top-heavy, middle-weighted, bottom-heavy, or uniformly sparse.

## Pitfalls

- Do not call a full-frame image generator for a one-region additive effect — the model can silently alter identity and composition.
- Do not blur the final composite globally — blur only a duplicate of the overlay, or locked source pixels will change.
- Do not alpha-composite a blurred glow directly over the full frame — its feathered edge can cross the intended boundary; re-mask the blurred alpha against the protected background before compositing.
- Do not place a semi-transparent effect reference uniformly over the target — use screen blend plus spatial and luminance masks so dark background receives the effect while skin, hair, and existing bright particles remain locked.
- Do not verify against the compressed source file bytes — compare decoded pixel arrays in the same color mode.
- Do not let procedural particles form letters, numbers, logos, complete halos, wings, or other semantic shapes when the user asked for abstract effects.
