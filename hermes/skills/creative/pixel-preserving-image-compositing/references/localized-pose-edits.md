# Localized pose edits

Use this recipe when the user says to change only an arm, hand, or pose while keeping the supplied image otherwise unchanged.

## Procedure

1. Decode the untouched base and record its exact dimensions. Create every requested variant from this same base.
2. Describe the requested pose as traceable geometry before generation: shoulder/root → elbow → forearm → wrist → palm plane → finger spacing, plus which limb is in front at every crossing. For foreshortened hands, state palm orientation separately from finger spread. For a hanging arm, compare upper-arm and forearm lengths with the torso: elbow near waist/upper hip, wrist below hip, and relaxed fist by upper thigh; do not mistake a fist by the waist for an anatomically complete arm. For legs, map hip → knee → ankle → boot for each side, specify which leg supports and which bends/overlaps, and allow a planted foot to leave the unchanged crop rather than scaling the whole subject down.
3. Generate one aligned candidate per variant from the untouched base. Lock identity, hair, costume materials, lighting, background, camera, crop, and canvas. Treat each candidate as restoration pixels, never as the deliverable.
4. Uniformly resize the candidate to the exact base canvas. Compare aligned original and candidate side by side at native resolution around the anatomical root and every crossing. Reject candidates whose requested limb orientation, joint length, digit count, attachment point, or camera/subject scale fails; a correct isolated leg pose is unusable when its pelvis no longer aligns with the locked torso.
5. Build an edit mask that covers the union of the complete old limb footprint and complete new limb path. For a raised arm moving behind the body, trace the old silhouette from hand through wrist, elbow, and shoulder, then trace the new shoulder-to-hand route; use a wide stroke along each joint centerline as well as contour polygons, because disconnected mask polygons can leave a floating old forearm. Include shoulders and occlusion boundaries only where geometry must change; a mask that covers only the new hand leaves old-arm remnants.
6. Build separate hard protection masks for face, locked hair, unrelated torso armor, the other hand, and background regions that overlap the broad limb envelope. Apply a narrow feather to the edit mask, then reapply hard protection after feathering so blur cannot spill candidate pixels into protected areas. Shape face protection to the actual skin/hair contour rather than ending it in a straight horizontal line: a broad rectangular cutoff can preserve face pixels while leaving a visible neck-to-shoulder seam. If a pose changes the shoulder under the chin, transition only the narrow non-face boundary with a controlled feather and verify the face core is pixel-identical.
7. Composite candidate pixels only through the final mask. Preserve correct depth order: foreground hair/clothing that should cover the new limb is copied back above it, while a new foreground hand may legitimately cover hair or torso only along its measured silhouette. For a cross-body hand resting at the opposite waist, mask the entire shoulder-to-elbow-to-forearm-to-hand route and its contact shadow; protecting the whole torso can clip the hand or make it float even when the pose candidate is correct.
8. Verify equal dimensions, `outside_changed_pixels=0`, and zero changes in every protected-region mask. Repeat these checks after any mask adjustment.
9. Inspect each final image at native-resolution crops. Count exactly two arms/two hands and two legs/two feet unless the request specifies extras; count five fingers per visible hand; trace every limb continuously from root to terminal and compare its length with the unchanged body. Reject old-limb ghosts, fused crossings, hard seams, duplicate joints, shortened segments, blurred foreshortened hands, or palm/boot orientation that reads differently from the request. Inspect the negative-space gap around the removed limb - especially beside hair, above a protected foreground fist, along the torso, and around a moved leg - because a reduced contact sheet can hide a floating dark fragment or an old thigh remnant.
10. If a small old-limb fragment survives beside a protected foreground limb, isolate that fragment as a bounded connected component using color/luminance plus spatial limits, dilate only enough to include its anti-aliased edge, and fill it from the aligned candidate. Then reapply the original protected limb pixels and rerun both `outside_changed_pixels=0` and protected-region difference counts; cleanup feathering can otherwise alter the hand that was supposed to remain unchanged.

## Extra-finger repair in a locked scene

1. Resolve anatomical hand versus viewer side before cropping; the archer's right release hand can be on viewer-left while the other hand grips the bow.
2. Crop the malformed hand with unchanged wrist, shoulder, face-edge and background anchors. Use a square crop when practical; generate each requested repair independently from that same original crop, not another repair. Specify replacement of the COMPLETE malformed hand, including redundant palm branches and old fingertips, because merely subtracting tips can preserve the wrong anatomy.
3. Describe the count spatially: one separated thumb PLUS four separately visible fingers in a descending fan, naming index, middle, ring and little finger. For an open release gesture, require five distinct tips; a generic 'five fingers' prompt can produce three fingers plus a thumb or hide the missing digit. Preserve glove material, wrist connection, hand scale and compact gesture.
4. Resize the donor uniformly to the crop, then register unchanged anchors using SIFT matches outside a dilated hand mask and RANSAC partial affine alignment. Build an inward-feathered mask around the union of old and new hand silhouettes, including exposed-background restoration beneath removed digits. Transfer only this region onto the untouched full image and hard-restore everything outside it.
5. For an explicitly requested digit correction, inspect enlarged crops of EVERY final hand and count thumb plus four fingers after compositing. Replace only failed slots independently from the original crop; a successful mask diff cannot prove finger count, and a four-tip result is not a completed five-digit repair. Check wrist continuity and absence of old fingertip ghosts as separate gates.
6. Verify full-resolution PNG dimensions, requested count, distinct hashes and zero decoded changes outside the hand-repair mask. Label the outputs localized hand-repair variations, not new scene compositions; uniqueness comes from the accepted local articulation while the scene stays fixed.

## Multi-arm and torso-yaw rule

For rear mechanical arms, derive the root location from the clothing reference rather than from the pose reference. If mounted at mid-back, keep both roots below the shoulder line and show the path extending outward before the elbow bends upward. Rotate the entire back rig with the torso: the near side becomes slightly larger/forward and the far side slightly smaller/back. Do not leave the rig in a flat frontal plane when the torso turns.
