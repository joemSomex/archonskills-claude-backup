# Marathon Aesthetic — universal prompt template

Replace every bracketed field. Never retain placeholders in final output.

```text
Create an original [DELIVERABLE TYPE] for [SUBJECT / PROJECT] using a speculative diagnostic editorial design system.

The visual language combines precise information design, physically believable rendering, technical illustration, and controlled digital imperfection. It should feel like an unfamiliar advanced system examining, indexing, assembling, or decoding the subject, but it must not resemble any existing game, film, brand, interface, logo, or fictional technology.

PURPOSE AND OUTPUT:
[Audience, use, dimensions, orientation, required copy, accessibility, technical constraints]

COMPOSITION:
Use a near-black or project-specific dark neutral field with generous negative space. Build asymmetric islands of dense information around one dominant focal subject. Permit selected type, diagrams, and subject edges to crop beyond the frame. Separate foreground annotation, midground subject, and background grid/signal planes. Alternate sparse idle areas with concentrated processing zones.

SUBJECT SYSTEM:
Base every graphic element on the real components, functions, states, materials, measurements, relationships, and transformations of [SUBJECT]. Show only useful states selected from silhouette, physically believable render/photo, structural wireframe, point cloud, exploded assembly, cross-section, and material detail.

GRAPHIC LANGUAGE:
Create new project-specific partial circles, segmented arcs, coordinate lines, modular rails, point clusters, cross-sections, status bars, registration marks, and thin leader lines. Every component communicates hierarchy, state, measurement, relationship, assembly, or change. Avoid decorative HUD clutter.

PALETTE:
Choose one dark base, one dominant saturated hue, one contrasting technical accent, pale neutral typography, and one warning color used in less than ten percent of the design. Derive the hues from [PROJECT IDENTITY / MATERIAL / STORY]. Each color represents a different operational state. Do not reproduce a recognizable entertainment-brand palette.

TYPOGRAPHY:
Use an original condensed technical sans serif for major labels and a restrained monospaced face for data. Combine oversized cropped typography with small annotations. Use vertical or rotated labels sparingly. Align type to modules and subject structure. Use only original project-specific terminology, codes, measurements, and required copy.

TEXTURE:
Apply fine scanlines, controlled dithering, localized raster breakup, subtle channel misregistration, small pixel blocks, restrained phosphor bloom, and occasional compression artifacts. Corruption appears only at transitions or unstable regions. Keep most of the design crisp.

LIGHT AND MATERIAL:
Use deep blacks, narrow structural rim light, matte materials, subdued reflections, small specular highlights, translucent technical layers, and restrained active-state glow. Lighting reveals construction and material behavior rather than adding generic glamour.

DEPTH:
Layer sharp foreground labels, a readable central subject, and softer background grids or traces. Attach data selectively to specific subject components.

ORIGINALITY LOCK:
Create a new visual grammar specific to [PROJECT]. Do not imitate or reproduce any identifiable logo, wordmark, bug/insect, mascot, running figure, character shell, equipment silhouette, circular emblem, interface glyph, boot/reboot screen, status mark, icon set, terminology, code, tagline, typography, palette balance, composition, or transition from existing media.

[OPTIONAL MOTION BLOCK]
Reveal the design through an original staged progression based on the subject’s story. Assemble graphics in layers; let labels appear after associated objects become readable; move selectively between silhouette, scan, wireframe, exploded, and finished states; reserve raster disturbance for state changes; contrast rapid diagnostic bursts with longer inspection holds. Keep underlying motion physically continuous. Do not copy a source sequence or timing.
```

## Negative prompt

```text
copied logo, copied wordmark, copied bug or insect, mascot, running figure, recognizable character shell, recognizable equipment, copied circular emblem, copied interface glyph, copied status mark, branded terminology, copied typography, copied palette arrangement, shot-for-shot recreation, generic sci-fi HUD clutter, random full-frame glitch, illegible required copy, decorative fake data, crowded negative space
```

## Short style suffix

```text
Apply an original graphic retro-futurist diagnostic aesthetic: dark negative-space composition, asymmetric information islands, project-specific technical typography, segmented geometry, wireframe and exploded views, physically believable materials, subject-driven annotations, restrained scanlines and dithering, localized raster corruption, and a new two-accent palette. Treat the subject as something being measured, assembled, or decoded. Avoid every recognizable logo, word, bug/insect, running figure, character shell, interface symbol, palette arrangement, and composition from existing media.
```
