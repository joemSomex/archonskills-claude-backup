# Marathon Aesthetic / Marathon 美学

“Marathon Aesthetic” is the user’s shorthand for an original graphic retro-futurist diagnostic design system: dark negative space, asymmetric information islands, technical typography, subject-driven diagrams, physical 3D materials, wireframes, and controlled raster imperfection. It is not permission to reproduce the source work’s intellectual property.

## When to Use

Load this skill whenever a request says:

- “Use the Marathon Aesthetic.”
- “Make this in Marathon style/aesthetic.”
- “Apply the Marathon look to this design, image, video, prompt, website, merchandise, episode, presentation, interface, or campaign.”
- “使用 Marathon 美学 / 马拉松美学。”

Use it as a visual-system layer around the user’s own subject and requirements. Do not use it when the user asks for a literal recreation, recognizable fan asset, copied interface, or branded derivative.

## Language Handling

- English request → respond in English.
- Chinese request → respond in Chinese while preserving filenames, technical terms, and user-locked copy.
- Never put the source property’s names, slogans, labels, or fictional terminology into a generated prompt merely because this skill was invoked.

## Non-Negotiable Originality Boundary

Never reproduce or closely approximate:

- Logos, wordmarks, brand bugs, insects, mascots, or identifying symbols.
- Running figures, signature character shells, equipment, silhouettes, anatomy, or poses from the source.
- Exact circular emblems, interface glyphs, boot/reboot screens, status marks, icon sets, diagrams, or screen layouts.
- Original words, codes, faction names, terminology, taglines, or typography.
- Exact palette proportions or recognizable combinations.
- Shot-for-shot transitions, sequence order, or composition.

Translate only high-level design principles. Build every symbol, diagram, label, palette, subject treatment, and animation from the new project’s purpose.

Read `references/originality-guardrails.md` before a high-visibility commercial deliverable or whenever the user asks to get “closer” to the source.

## Core Design System

### Composition

- Use a very dark neutral field with generous negative space.
- Concentrate detail in asymmetric information islands rather than filling the canvas evenly.
- Establish one dominant focal subject.
- Allow selected type, geometry, and subject edges to crop beyond the frame.
- Separate foreground annotation, midground subject, and background grid/signal planes.
- Alternate sparse idle states with dense processing states.

### Palette Architecture

Choose a new palette for each project:

1. One near-black or very dark neutral base.
2. One dominant saturated hue.
3. One contrasting technical accent.
4. Pale neutral typography.
5. One warning color occupying less than ten percent of the frame.

Every color should represent a state or function. Do not automatically reuse the source’s familiar blue/acid-green balance. Tie colors to the new subject’s identity, industry, material, or story.

### Typography

- Use an original condensed technical sans serif for major labels.
- Use a restrained monospaced face for data and annotation.
- Contrast oversized cropped type with very small operational labels.
- Use vertical or rotated text only where it strengthens hierarchy.
- Align type to modules, rails, and subject anatomy rather than centering everything.
- Generate new project-specific codes, measurements, and labels with real meaning.

### Graphic Language

Use only elements that communicate hierarchy, state, measurement, relationship, assembly, or change:

- Partial circles and segmented arcs.
- Coordinate lines and modular rails.
- Point clouds and wireframes.
- Exploded or cross-section views.
- Status bars and registration marks.
- Thin leader lines tied to specific subject components.
- Project-specific technical symbols derived from the subject’s function.

Avoid decorative sci-fi HUD clutter. The graphics should explain the subject.

### Subject Treatment

Translate the subject through compatible states:

- Clean silhouette.
- Physically believable rendered or photographic form.
- Wireframe or structural scan.
- Point-cloud reconstruction.
- Exploded assembly or cross-section.
- Localized material details.

The design should feel as if an unfamiliar advanced system is examining, indexing, assembling, or decoding the user’s subject.

### Texture

Use controlled digital imperfection:

- Fine scanlines.
- Dithering.
- Localized raster breakup.
- Slight channel misregistration.
- Small pixel blocks.
- Restrained phosphor bloom.
- Occasional compression artifacts.
- Imperfect edge interpolation.

Glitch is punctuation for transitions, unstable regions, or state changes. Most of the design remains crisp and intentional.

### Lighting and Materials

- Deep blacks and controlled contrast.
- Narrow structural rim light.
- Matte industrial surfaces.
- Subdued reflections with small specular highlights.
- Transparent or translucent technical layers.
- Restrained glow around active components.
- Lighting that reveals construction and material behavior instead of generic glamour.

## Procedure

1. **Identify the deliverable.** Record medium, size, orientation, audience, subject, required copy, interaction/motion, and practical use. Completion: every fixed requirement is listed before styling.

2. **Extract the subject’s own system.** Identify real components, functions, states, materials, measurements, relationships, and transformations. Completion: every proposed graphic element can be traced to the new subject rather than the source video.

3. **Choose an original palette.** Build the five-part palette architecture from the project’s identity. Completion: the palette does not recreate the source’s recognizable balance.

4. **Build the composition.** Place one focal subject, asymmetric information islands, and clear depth planes. Protect substantial negative space. Completion: the layout remains legible without texture or glitch.

5. **Create a project-specific visual lexicon.** Design new arcs, grids, labels, codes, diagrams, and symbols from the subject’s function. Completion: no symbol or screen could be mistaken for an extracted source asset.

6. **Choose subject states.** Decide which silhouette, scan, wireframe, point-cloud, exploded, material, or final-render states are useful. Do not include every state by default. Completion: each state advances understanding or narrative.

7. **Apply typography.** Use strong scale contrast and operational hierarchy. Required text stays readable; generated filler never replaces user copy. Completion: labels describe the new project accurately.

8. **Add texture last.** Apply controlled raster imperfection only after the clean design works. Completion: turning texture off still leaves a coherent design.

9. **For motion, stage a reveal.** Use the progression `signal → module → mechanism → subject structure → recognizable subject → original status mark` when appropriate. Vary or reorder it to suit the new narrative. Completion: no transition copies the source shot-for-shot.

10. **Run originality QA.** Compare the result against `references/originality-guardrails.md`. Replace anything dependent on a logo, insect/bug, running figure, exact glyph, signature ring, source word, character shell, interface layout, or recognizable palette arrangement.

## Required Output

Unless the user requests otherwise, provide:

1. **Design direction** — a concise explanation of how the aesthetic maps to the subject.
2. **Palette architecture** — functional roles, not copied values.
3. **Final generation/design prompt** — one copy-ready block.
4. **Negative constraints** — source-IP and generic-HUD safeguards.
5. **Originality note** — one sentence confirming what was deliberately changed.

For production work, load `templates/master-prompt.md`. For medium-specific guidance, load `references/medium-adaptations.md`.

## Motion Rules

- Assemble modules in layers rather than fading everything in together.
- Let labels appear after their associated object becomes readable.
- Use hard modular cuts and brief black intervals sparingly.
- Rotate segmented structures at distinct speeds.
- Move between silhouette, scan, wireframe, exploded view, and finished subject.
- Reserve glitches for state changes.
- Keep underlying motion physically continuous.
- Contrast rapid diagnostic bursts with longer inspection holds.
- Avoid generic corporate morphs, random glitch overlays, and copied transition timing.

## Pitfalls

- Copying the source palette while changing only the subject.
- Reusing exact rings, icons, labels, or layout proportions.
- Treating “retro-futurism” as an excuse for random HUD clutter.
- Filling all negative space with data.
- Using meaningless codes and fake measurements.
- Applying glitch before composition and hierarchy work.
- Repeating one visual trick across every medium.
- Leaving the project’s real identity subordinate to the reference aesthetic.

## Verification

Confirm before delivery:

- The subject, symbols, copy, labels, and measurements are project-specific.
- No source logo, bug/insect, word, running figure, character shell, interface glyph, or iconic composition appears.
- The palette follows the architecture but uses a new project-specific balance.
- Negative space and information islands remain intentional.
- Typography has a clear hierarchy and required copy is readable.
- Every technical graphic has a purpose tied to the subject.
- Texture is localized and does not obscure the design.
- Motion changes state rather than adding arbitrary noise.
- The result is recognizably its own system when the source reference is removed.
