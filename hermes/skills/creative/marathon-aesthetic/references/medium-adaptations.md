# Medium adaptations

## Static image, poster, or key art

- Prioritize one focal subject and one information cluster.
- Use oversized cropped type as structure, not decoration.
- Keep raster texture localized around state changes or edges.
- Ensure the composition works without motion.

## Video and motion graphics

- Establish a state progression tied to the subject’s story.
- Use layered assembly, hard modular cuts, brief black intervals, and palette inversions selectively.
- Keep labels synchronized with the object or state they describe.
- Alternate quick diagnostic bursts with readable holds.
- Preserve physical continuity beneath graphic interruptions.

## Website and interface

- Translate the aesthetic into a restrained component system rather than animating every element.
- Use dark negative space, modular cards, technical hierarchy, and one active accent.
- Keep navigation conventional and accessible.
- Reserve glitch and scan effects for loading, state changes, or hero moments.
- Do not reduce usability to mimic a fictional interface.

## Merchandise and packaging

- Build graphics from product construction, materials, sizing, edition data, or manufacturing details.
- Use print-safe dithering and vector geometry.
- Keep legal, care, and product information readable.
- Avoid placing copied symbols or fictional UI on the object.

## Episode or campaign system

- Define a stable grid, typography hierarchy, palette roles, and annotation logic.
- Give each episode/campaign item a new subject-specific diagnostic motif.
- Preserve system consistency without repeating one hero symbol.

## Presentation or document

- Use sparse section dividers, technical labels, cropped numerals, diagrams, and dark/light palette inversions.
- Keep body copy readable and reduce texture behind long text.
- Use data visualization honestly; do not add meaningless measurements.

## 3D object or character presentation

- Use exploded views, material callouts, wireframes, and cross-sections derived from the actual model.
- Keep identity and anatomy original.
- Use lighting to explain volume and assembly.

## Audio cover or music visualizer

- Derive geometry from waveform, frequency, rhythm, instrumentation, or lyrical structure.
- Do not use random equalizer bars as filler.
- Let the palette and texture respond to meaningful audio states.
