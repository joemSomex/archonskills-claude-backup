# Character: Card Conversion

## When to Use

When the user writes `Character: Card Conversion` with two attached images, immediately perform the five-card workflow. Treat capitalization-only differences as equivalent. If either image is missing, locate it only when an unambiguous earlier user message assigned its role; otherwise ask for the missing reference. If a third image is explicitly assigned as an action reference, keep the roles separate: image 1 determines character identity and costume, image 2 environment, and image 3 pose/activity only. When a fourth attachment is assigned as a weapon reference, include it as its own role in every generation, retaining its distinctive shape/material rather than copying the pose image's weapon or energy color. If the user's ordinal reference conflicts with the number of attached images (e.g. 'image 5' but four arrived), ask which attachment they mean before generating; never silently renumber it. Include every role-specific original independently in each generation; do not transfer the pose image's face, hair, clothing, palette, or energy/VFX to the character unless explicitly requested. If the user removes an effect from a previous batch, regenerate all five directly from the originals with that effect prohibited on each relevant surface; inspect every result at native size, distinguishing permitted ambient rain/reflections from an attached glow, arc, or halo. Never substitute an unrelated image simply because it was the latest attachment. A quoted trigger in discussion is not a new generation request.

Before generation read `references/authoritative-brief.md` in full. Its role and composition requirements override generic `card-design` defaults (which use different ratios, counts, and references). Inspect both original images and classify by visible content: image 1 controls character identity, face, hair, outfit, proportions and details; image 2 contributes architecture, atmosphere, lighting, scale and environmental language only. Do not copy its landscape layout. User-provided reference assignments win over attachment ordering.

Generate five independent OpenAI/ChatGPT images from **both originals on every call**, never from a prior generated variation. No Claude; no Higgsfield unless explicitly requested. Reserve five distinct action silhouettes, camera/framing compositions and reimagined landscape arrangements before generation. Keep the character's readable face as the strongest focal point in the upper/middle card, facing away from direct viewer eye contact and moving naturally through combat by default. If the current user explicitly asks for a front-facing/direct-to-camera attack, that user instruction overrides the default gaze rule: make the visor/face orient squarely to the lens and use unmistakable active strike/reach/kick body mechanics rather than a static frontal portrait. For a genuinely sealed helmet, make its visor/head the recognizable 'face' focal point without inventing a human face; check its orientation at native size, since a symmetrical frontal visor can still violate the default no-direct-viewer-gaze rule. If the user explicitly identifies a support/repair role, preserve that function over the generic combat-pose examples: depict urgent repair under threat rather than adding a firearm, and inventory source tools (e.g. a tethered tablet and exact count of shoulder manipulators) in every variation. When an additional action reference shows hands-on repair of a seated robot, maintain the technician's source outfit and face while varying camera, setting layout, and repair interaction; use repair implements or a scanner drone only when supported by that action reference, never copy its differently dressed technician. Treat an iconic central tower/halo in the environment reference as inspiration only, not a background template repeated across cards. Preserve a calm, dark, low-contrast bottom third for future text, but place no text or graphic content anywhere unless the current user explicitly requires a source marking (for example, chest `N.C.F.` or bike-side `SKPD`). An explicitly retained marking belongs only on its specified physical surface, not as floating title; inspect its exact spelling, orientation, visibility and punctuation at native size in every card. Keep other card graphics and words absent. Render sculptural, painterly, animated depth rather than photography, anime or generic CGI. No frame, UI, or extra marks.

For face-covering masks, inspect a native-size source eye crop before generation: a circular veined mask cell is not an exposed red iris, human eyeball, glowing lens, or open eye socket. Preserve the source's actual eye-cover construction when the user requests direct gaze; direct gaze can come from the exposed eye without inventing a second visible eye. When correcting this defect across a batch, repair every affected original independently and verify unchanged pixels outside each localized eye-mask region.

For marked two-handle props with explicit left/right assignments, identify which grip is forward and rear on the original; state the anatomical shoulder-to-elbow-to-glove path for each hand and choose a camera/weapon orientation that lets both real handle contacts remain visible. At native size, reject a newly invented grip on the rail or an unheld original handle even if both hands touch something. A near-frontal torso with the item front toward viewer-right often makes anatomical left on viewer-right reach the forward grip and anatomical right on viewer-left reach the rear grip; reverse this mapping for rear views. For asymmetric costumes, inventory anatomical sides before generation and check their relationships under the actual pose: a right-hip attachment must remain on the same body side as a right-shoulder feature, regardless of screen position. Repeating anatomical instructions in the prompt does not prove compliance. Inspect profile gaze, hip attachment, and the exact lower-third boundary independently; a repaired side can still fail gaze or text-space composition. Never overwrite an existing slot with a candidate that has not improved it; retain failed candidates as drafts and report unresolved criteria honestly.

For medical-support scenes, keep every patient head, care contact, scanner and bright armor mass in the upper 58–60%, with seated laps and kneeling legs naturally entering shadow by that boundary. Explicitly prohibit reflective pavement highlights and architectural lines in the reserved bottom: asking only for a dark bottom can still produce detailed reflections or a second-city double exposure. Anchor diagnostic illumination to an actual instrument and prohibit projected crosses/holographic symbols when the brief permits physical garment crosses only. Strongly specify broad chunky painted planes if source references push results toward photorealistic tactical renders; inspect the actual style rather than assuming 'painterly' in the prompt achieved it.

For paired enemy hit-reaction corrections, assign contrasting torso tilts, head directions, arm paths and knee states to the two identities in every original-reference generation; inspect actual contact flashes as well as pose contrast. Opposed poses can succeed while feet, bracing palms and wet-ground reflections still breach y=2H/3, so record the lower-third result separately rather than certifying the whole card from successful poses. Respect a replacement authorization limited to identical-pose failures; do not silently expand it into unrelated regeneration cycles.

For airborne figures whose limbs or bright architecture repeatedly intrude into the reserved lower third, regenerate from the originals with the complete figure constrained to the upper 60% and a seamless dark-haze lower 40% safety margin. Treat this as a prompting aid, not evidence of compliance: inspect the actual y=2H/3 boundary, and never accept a replacement merely because its bottom is darker. Strong foreshortening can also collapse distinct camera requests into similar frontal views; judge actual silhouette and perspective across the contact sheet, not prompt labels.

When an explicit correction audit authorizes targeted review, treat a glossy action reference as a strong style-leak risk even when the primary painted identity reference is listed first. Inspect the woman herself for planar paint, nose/mouth/jaw geometry and hair-lock construction; matching pose and palette does not establish fidelity. A stronger negative prompt can still reproduce the glossy action source, so stop at the authorized bounded replacement limit and report unresolved style/identity and lower-third failures honestly rather than certifying compliance. Keep an earlier slot when its replacement worsens the image, and retain round/selection provenance in the manifest.

For explicitly audited electric-cage corrections, establish depth through actual foreground horizontal and vertical wires crossing opaque torso/limbs, rear wires occluded by the creature, joined side/roof planes, and weightbearing feet inside the street-level base quadrilateral. A visible cube outline alone is not proof of containment. Judge roof clearance against the receding roof plane, not the highest front corner; nominal 30% headroom in prompts may render as a narrow gap. If the character source is a multiview sheet, specify one creature with one head because it can otherwise produce duplicated muzzles. Respect the authorized per-slot replacement limit and mark unresolved candidates partial rather than certifying the whole batch.

The user has opted out of visual review for image generation. Generate and send directly without contact sheets, crop inspections or unsolicited replacement cycles unless the user explicitly asks for review; retain lightweight file/count/format/uniqueness checks and never claim visual QA. Inspect source references as needed to ground the prompt. Reject image-wide shared defects before batching replacements when visual QA is enabled; replace individual failures directly from the two originals. Export five standalone PNGs at an **exact 2:3 pixel ratio**, without stretching or unsafe crop. Check PNG encoding, count, distinct hashes and visually meaningful differences; distinguish a rendered likeness from exact pixel reproduction. Deliver all five individual files in chat, not a collage.
