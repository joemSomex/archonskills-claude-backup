# Authoritative Item: Card Conversion brief

Create 5 separate, unique 2:3 digital item-card images using @image1 as the primary item reference and @image2 only as environmental/location inspiration.
The final image must read as a premium digital playing card featuring a single item in an unmistakable Arcane-style animated aesthetic: painterly, sculptural, cinematic, textured, dramatic, and clearly animated. Emphasize dimensional, stylized forms; expressive hand-painted textures; visible but refined brushwork; graphic shapes of light and shadow; rich colored shadows; controlled edge highlights; and subtle surface wear. The final result must look like an item rendered within a high-end Arcane-style animated production—not photorealistic, anime, or ordinary product photography. Cinematic lighting and believable materials must support the painterly animated finish.
@image1 is the clear main subject and defines the item’s design. Preserve its identity, silhouette, proportions, construction, materials, color palette, functional components, and important non-textual details. Translate the same item into the Arcane-style animation language without redesigning its function, changing its proportions, adding unrelated components, or removing defining structural features. Omit any text, lettering, numbers, logos, or symbols present on the reference item. The item’s most recognizable feature must remain the sharpest, most prominent, and most detailed focal point, framed strongly in the upper and middle portions of the card. Keep enough of its form visible for the item to remain immediately recognizable.
Present the item in a dramatic, dynamic cinematic composition, with the energy of a moment captured within an action sequence. Vary its orientation, camera perspective, placement, and relationship to the surrounding environment—for example, a strong diagonal composition, a low-angle view, a close three-quarter angle, or a compelling close-up emphasizing its defining construction. Create energy through perspective, lighting, asymmetry, and subtle environmental movement. Any physical motion must suit the item and remain believable; otherwise, ground it naturally on or against an appropriate surface. Preserve clear, readable item details. Show the item alone: no characters, people, faces, hands, wearers, operators, creatures, or competing hero objects.
Use @image2 only to inspire the surrounding architecture, terrain, materials, atmosphere, lighting, scale, and environmental design language. Create an original location with a different layout, arrangement, and composition from the reference. Do not reproduce the exact landscape or reuse its framing. Keep the environment supportive, atmospheric, and cinematic, with controlled detail and visual depth. The item must remain more visually dominant than its surroundings. Render both the item and environment in the same cohesive Arcane-style animated aesthetic.
Reserve the bottom one-third of the image as intentional space for text to be added later. This area must be darker, calmer, less detailed, and lower contrast, containing only subtle atmospheric texture and softly simplified environmental forms. Concentrate the item and its essential identifying details within the upper two-thirds. Keep strong highlights, sharp edges, busy debris, and prominent objects out of the lower third. Let this darker area emerge naturally through lighting, shadow, and atmosphere, with a soft transition into the rest of the artwork. Do not create a visible panel, box, banner, overlay, or hard dividing line.
Keep the final composition clean and image-only: no text, letters, numbers, symbols, stats, logos, icons, UI elements, labels, borders, decorative graphics, or watermarks anywhere on the card. At this stage, it must be purely a clean 2:3 illustrated item-card image.
Generate 5 genuinely different individual files, each with a unique item orientation, camera angle, framing, composition, lighting arrangement, and environmental staging, while preserving the exact same item identity and consistent Arcane-style visual language. Each variation must feel like a distinct cinematic composition; changing only the crop or color is insufficient. One complete card image per file.
