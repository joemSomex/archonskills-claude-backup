# 道具卡牌转换 (Item: Card Conversion)

## When to Use

When the user writes `Item: Card Conversion` with an item and an environment image, immediately generate five independent cards. Match capitalization-only differences. If the trigger is invoked without images, recover only references explicitly and unambiguously assigned in the conversation; otherwise ask for the missing source(s). Never reuse a prior vehicle, character or setting merely because it is the latest attachment. The user's full brief itself also invokes this workflow when both sources are available.

Read `references/authoritative-brief.md` in full before generation; it overrides the generic card-design template, ratio, count and role map. Inspect both originals at useful resolution and assign image 1 **item identity/design only**, image 2 **environment language only**. Record the item's silhouette, proportions, material and color hierarchy, function, connection graph and defining non-textual marks. Omit text, logos and symbols even if present on the item. When the item has precise mechanical topology, also load the complex-prop-fidelity reference bundled with `creative:reference-driven-image-generation`.

Use OpenAI/ChatGPT image generation only for this user. Generate five independent calls directly from both original references; never use a prior generated card as input. Reserve five materially different camera orientations and environmental staging arrangements before generating, unless the current user correction locks item orientation or placement. When the source is a multi-view design board, inspect and isolate one canonical complete front view for the image base while retaining the full original board as a topology reference; do not reproduce several board panels in a card. If the user forbids deformation and requires upper-middle centering, lock the canonical silhouette, front orientation, grip/hub positions and the full object's horizontal bounding-box midpoint near 50% (not merely the handle or visual mass); vary the original environment and lighting instead of rotating or redesigning the item. For this user's weapon cards, ALWAYS redraw the weapon and setting together as one coherent illustration with shared key light, metal reflection, contact shadow and atmospheric depth. Never deliver a source-weapon cutout over a new background as a generated card, even when the user also requires the weapon's original shape and length. Keep the measured item above the lower text zone by changing camera distance or whole-object orientation, not shortening a shaft or blade. Preserve defining topology and component ratios by reference-driven generation and native-size QA; if the generator cannot guarantee pixel-exact geometry, disclose that limitation rather than quietly switching to compositing. Each card contains just the one item, no people, hands, creatures or competing objects, with a believable pose/support and clearly readable identifying structure in the upper two-thirds. Render both subject and setting in the same dimensional hand-painted animated-series language, not photography, anime or generic CGI. Reserve a naturally dark, calm, low-detail lower third with a soft transition and **no** visible text panel. Exclude all text, letters, numbers, logos, symbols, UI, border or decorative marks across the entire image.

Do not carry an earlier source-cutout workaround into a later card-conversion or revision request merely because the same item image recurs. If the current user explicitly requests weapon and setting to be redrawn together or asks for integrated environmental light, override the earlier pixel-lock method: generate every final card directly from the original item and environment references as a whole-scene repaint, inspect shared key light, contact/occlusion shadow, local emitter spill and atmospheric depth, and never label a pasted cutout as a redraw. Preserve the item's defining proportions and full-length blade as closely as possible, but explain that a model redraw cannot guarantee pixel-identical geometry. When a geometry constraint seems to conflict with full repaint, retain the full-repaint requirement and explain that exact pixel identity cannot be guaranteed; seek clarification only if the user actually requests mutually exclusive pixel identity and new painted surfaces.

When an otherwise correctly framed source cutout looks studio-lit against a colored environment, relight the temporary composition guide before regenerating: use the environment's actual key side to cool only exposed metal planes, keep the item's intrinsic emitter color separate, and preserve the original silhouette/alpha. Then request matching key-side highlights, opposing occlusion, near-field emitter spill and shared haze on both prop and setting. The guide is not final proof: compare generated native-size blade and shaft against the environment for consistent highlight direction, shadow depth and cutout halos. Inspect a contact sheet, then each native-size output for identity/topology, actual style transfer, meaningful composition variation, empty lower-third text space, and stray lettering. Reject and regenerate failed slots directly from the originals when feasible. When a correction forbids changes to a thin mechanical item's shape or length and redraws distort it, build an exact-geometry source guide solely to condition a fresh full-scene redraw; never use a literal source cutout in the final five cards. Reject generated variants that change the silhouette, connection graph or component ratios. If no variant meets the geometry and full-repaint gates, report the shortfall plainly rather than delivering a background-only substitution. Normalize by safe crop to an **exact 2:3 aspect ratio**, never stretch or cut the defining item. Verify five individual PNG files and distinct hashes; hashes alone do not prove visual uniqueness. Deliver the five individual files in chat, with candid caveats for inferred surfaces or imperfect details.

## Preserve the lower-third text reserve during revisions

Treat the authoritative brief's bottom one-third text reserve as persistent across battle-scene redesigns and cinematic revisions. Epic shots, foreground debris, rain, smoke, a central enemy, and an upper-background warship do NOT waive this reserve. Unless the user explicitly places a subject in the lower third or explicitly removes the reserve, keep essential battle action, enemy, ship, muzzle flashes, projectile paths, impact rings, sharp debris and bright water reflections within the upper two-thirds. In a 1024x1536 card, keep y=1024 through 1535 dark, calm, low-contrast and low-detail, with only soft atmosphere and simplified shadows; transition naturally without a panel, banner or dividing line. Never insert prompt language that sacrifices this zone for cinematic staging. Reassert the reserve in every independent generation prompt and correction; a scale or action revision is not permission to discard it. When visual review is requested, inspect the lower third itself rather than accepting the prompt or dimensions as proof.

## Correcting mirrored gauntlet anatomy

When the user flags duplicated handedness, treat their targeted visual check as an override of any standing no-review preference. For dorsal, fingers-down paired fists, explicitly assign the screen-right foreground LEFT hand's radial thumb root to its inward screen-left edge and the screen-left rear RIGHT hand's root to its inward screen-right edge. Show four finger columns plus one distinct curled thumb on each and trace each wrist into its forearm; camera rotation alone does not change handedness. Inspect each output's two thumb roots and dorsal/palm planes at useful native crops, record per-slot findings rather than accepting the prompt or hashes as proof, and replace failed slots independently from the authorized originals. Keep a per-file manifest with all source roles, provider, visual findings, exact dimensions, file hashes and decoded-pixel hashes. Report 2D visual evidence and any remaining style/layout limitations candidly; do not claim a validated anatomical rig.
