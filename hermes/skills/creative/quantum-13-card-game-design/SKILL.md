# QUANTUM XIII Card Game Design

## Route art requests before rules research

When this skill is named for an image-composition request, generate the artwork rather than explaining game mechanics or inspecting the engine. Load `reference-driven-image-generation`; use `character-card-conversion` for the established painterly 2:3 character-card art workflow when applicable. Follow the current explicit count; if a plain composition request has no count or batch trigger, create one image rather than inheriting an earlier batch size. Recover the original character and environment for follow-ups, and add separately assigned pose and weapon references without letting their faces, outfits, weapons or sheet layouts leak into other roles. Preserve the latest requested shot size and gesture. Default to direct delivery after lightweight file checks, without unsolicited per-image review. Artwork generation does not modify card stats, game data or narrative canon.

## Design goal
Keep the core simple but nuanced; put expansion depth in card abilities and cross-character interactions. This is one card game for the **entire QUANTUM 13 universe**, with all characters eligible for inclusion; do not pitch separate faction games or treat faction-specific deck mechanics as the game's core concept. The user has now locked the exact card-game title **`QUANTUM 13: Convergence`**. Do not treat it as a proposal or substitute another subtitle; earlier title alternatives remain unselected. The Force/Domain version was too complicated; a pure GWENT-style score contest was too simple. Distinguish user decisions from assistant proposals. Game mechanics are not narrative canon.

## Historical user-confirmed design revisions (not the live ruleset)
These decisions document an earlier iteration. For current gameplay, use the implementation-first check below; do not brief a developer from this section as though it were the active ruleset.
- Online 1v1; 40-card decks; 50 Life Force per player.
- Five opening cards; with the approved alternating-action structure, both players draw one card at the beginning of each round, not each individual action.
- No Commander system or player/Commander skills.
- No Domain or Location cards.
- Characters use Attack and Health. Damage persists; zero Health sends a Character to the Graveyard (formerly Memory).
- Win by reducing the opponent's Life Force to zero, using Characters and card effects.
- All defending Characters protect their player: a Character cannot attack an opponent directly while that opponent controls any Characters, unless the attacking Character's special ability explicitly permits bypassing them. This protection is independent of readiness, attacking, supporting or deployment that round. Removing the final defender permits subsequent legal direct attacks; it does not grant an extra attack or automatic excess damage.
- This replaces the Guard-only protection rule; no separate Guard keyword is needed for basic player protection. The restriction concerns Character attacks, not direct damage from cards whose text explicitly permits targeting the player.
- Spell-like cards may represent powers such as Kara's R.A.S.F.; do not require all effects to be literal physical items.
- User requested a summoning/resource restriction comparable to mana. The earlier no-resource rule is superseded as a design constraint, but the specific replacement is not confirmed.

## Historical differentiation decisions
These preceded the implemented whole-turn ruleset; preserve them as provenance, not current tutorial instructions. Jason selected options 1, 3, 4, 6 and 8:
- Alternating single actions: play one card, attack with one eligible Character, use a printed support action, or pass. Passing ends participation for the round; the other player can continue until also passing. Both players receive resources and draw at round start. Newly deployed Characters normally wait until next round to attack; each Character attacks at most once per round.
- Selected Characters may use a printed support ability instead of attacking, consuming their attack opportunity. This is a card ability, not a player or Commander skill.
- Equipping an Item consumes the recipient's attack opportunity for the round. A Character that has already attacked or supported cannot be equipped that round. Equipping is one card-play action, not an additional support action.
- Character-linked Ability cards work alone and improve when their associated Character is present. Exact R.A.S.F. card text remains illustrative, not canon or finalized balance.
- Selected cards spend the Graveyard by removing specified cards from the match as a printed cost. This is optional card text, not a universal resurrection rule. Initially suited to IOSIS.
- Do not add unselected options: upgrades, dual-use cards, visible response subsystem or deck-building budget. All-Character protection was subsequently explicitly selected, but without the earlier proposed condition that attacking Characters stop protecting.
- Integration details need explicit specification: support eligibility for newly deployed Characters, Item placement on newly deployed Characters, first-action alternation and effect durations. Prefer shared attack/support readiness, no same-round support from newly deployed Characters, and legal deployment-round equipment that leaves the Character unavailable until next round; label these implementation clarifications rather than separately approved mechanics.

## Earlier assistant-proposed baseline (not the live ruleset)
- Automatic Energy growth/refill, capped at ten; pay printed costs to play multiple cards.
- Character, one-use Ability, and attached Item card types.
- Under the approved action structure, each established Character can attack OR use a printed support action once per round; newly deployed Characters normally cannot attack that round.
- Simultaneous Character combat; direct player attacks have no retaliation.
- Guard-only protection is superseded by the user-confirmed all-Character protection rule above.
- Five Character spaces, one Item per Character, ten-card hand limit.
- Rush/Charge and exact Energy timing remain proposals; avoid a first-turn resource increment ambiguity.

## Story-encounter developer handoff
1. When briefing the developer who built the game, lead with the requested player experience and smallest playable scope, not a recitation of mechanics they already implemented. Use other games as narrative/presentation references only; explicitly retain Convergence gameplay. Keep the tone warm, collaborative, and polite rather than issuing a rigid specification.
2. Agree on the story event and whether the encounter follows produced continuity or is a separate hypothetical. Read the canon archive README, the corresponding screenplay, produced-episode analysis, authoritative SRT/revised dialogue script, and alignment report before drafting lines. A dialogue-alignment package is not itself a merged adjusted screenplay: use screenplay for action and produced SRT for spoken wording, labeling adaptation differences.
3. Map the actual event into an opening, playable middle, reversal, and ending before assigning game triggers. If a story's apparent victory becomes an ambush, do not turn that beat into a clean defeat of the antagonist; propose a survival or transition objective and check the game's actual encounter hooks with the developer. Preserve produced ending geography and cliffhanger rather than importing a contradictory written ending.
4. Deliver dialogue as an event-driven packet: unique ID, trigger/state condition, speaker, exact approved text, presentation, once/repeat rule, ordering/priority, and fallback if the relevant card never appears. Put the short intro and endings on guaranteed states; tie optional lines to card or combat events only after effects resolve. Do not force draws or fixed turns to make a line fire. Specify loss/retry as non-canon unless the author approves an alternate story.
5. Send a trigger inventory to the developer for feasibility before finalizing the script; distinguish already exposed engine events from proposed phase transitions or scripted reinforcements. For a prototype, ask for one complete replayable encounter and data-editable dialogue, triggers, decks, and AI behavior, rather than a whole campaign.
6. Preserve the user's ASCII-safe copy-paste presentation in chat. If joining subtitle cue fragments or changing typographic punctuation for an implementation draft, label that as a presentation adaptation rather than claiming verbatim SRT punctuation.
7. Before revising an existing encounter, read its saved developer draft under the card-game archive and preserve the author's selected lines exactly. Scope a non-canon adaptation to the requested bounded scene; exclude characters and events outside it even when the source episode continues. Present exchanges through the existing board, portraits, and text UI unless the user asks for cinematics; narrating a location change does not require a cutscene. Give both sides optional upper-hand lines and a distinct player-loss line when requested, but make the confirmed result cancel pending taunts. Label new game-only dialogue separately from source dialogue.

## Playable story tutorials and dialogue variants
1. Ground the tutorial in the current implemented rules and the relevant screenplay/produced voice evidence before drafting. Use a pre-deployment training or assessment frame without claiming that a literal card match happened onscreen; Enji-type instructors test a knowledgeable character's decisions while teaching the new player. Keep the established relationship: precise, dry prompts from the instructor; brief, lightly cocky but likable reactions from the trainee, not a joke or a profession of ignorance after every rule.
2. Plan guided matches as playable states: opening hand/mulligan, Energy and deployment, end turn/readiness, simultaneous combat and persistent damage, defender protection, short Ability/Item/printed Support drills, and either a guided or free-choice finish according to the user's current scope. Expand both the trainee's and coach opponent's turns when teaching a full match. Demonstrate a rule at its point of consequence, then explain it; a prompted attempt at an unavailable direct attack is appropriate only if the UI gives feedback without consuming the attack or trapping the player. Let the player perform the legal actions rather than auto-playing them.
3. For every beat specify board/hand setup, whose turn it is, required or optional action, success condition, dialogue timing, and recovery from other legal choices. A Character normally cannot attack or Support on deployment; Rush permits an immediate attack but not Support. Under the active v4 rules, equipping does not consume the recipient's action. Split Ability, Item, and Support prompts. If the user asks for an extended all-mechanics tutorial, include legal hero Call/Protect/Awaken, Suppress and other feasible keywords in the match; route late-round, empty-deck, or faction-exclusive mechanics to later drills or glossary rather than claiming ten-turn mirror decks demonstrate them all. Use the live UI's card-type term, not an unverified synonym such as `Action`.
4. Keep all tutorial presentation in the normal board UI, make dialogue advanceable/skippable on replay, and offer a skip for returning players. Ask the developer which training-state/highlight hooks exist before treating the plan as an implementation spec.
5. When asked for multiple dialogue variations in individual `.txt` files, create one complete, self-contained file per variation with both speakers and the same ordered lesson labels, unless the user explicitly requests separate character files. Give variants meaningfully different banter density/cadence while keeping mechanics and voices consistent. Verify file count, stage coverage, and speaker presence programmatically before attaching each file; do not substitute one combined file or only a list of lines in chat.
6. For a fixed-length story tutorial, count both players' turns explicitly and choose starting seat so the intended player takes the final turn. Use the current starter deck as a base, make only lesson-driven swaps, and validate each complete list. Build a deterministic route with exact opening hands, guided mulligan and replacement, automatic draws, opponent commands, player card/target/action clicks, and End Turn clicks; use the live tutorial's prepared states and constrained-command hooks. Curating player actions must not auto-play them. Verify every card is in its validated deck, every command is legal, and the intended win happens on the promised turn by replaying the sequence against the engine; a seeded match alone may not enforce hand/draw order. Preserve dependencies such as signature Call before its hero leaves the deck and the intervening opponent turn before Awaken. When teaching a second-player +1 Energy option, make the player click it at a turn where a specific legal card becomes affordable, show Energy before and after, and check costs with effective ruleset definitions; a tooltip alone does not teach its use. Rework dialogue at the same beat so the instructor explains the action when it happens. Preserve the author's selected dialogue verbatim while adding implementation notes where flavor wording could imply unguided choices.

7. For an existing tutorial draft, locate the latest developer brief and any newly supplied author revision before editing; preserve selected dialogue verbatim rather than restoring an older variant. Present dialogue and gameplay in the same chronology: opening, opponent identification, pregame mulligan, first player's turn, then second player's first-turn instruction. The mulligan is not a match turn. Keep character lines separate from implementation notes. If a route was tested only with an engine probe, describe UI integration and deterministic draw controls as remaining work rather than calling the tutorial implemented. Author one sentence per portrait bubble without rewriting it; store explicit speaker/text/trigger/action metadata instead of splitting punctuation at runtime. Auto-advance reaction/story bubbles after `max(2.5, 1 + wordCount/3)` seconds, starting only when fully visible after animations; keep instructional bubbles until the required action or Continue, and allow manual advancement, pause, and slower accessibility reading. Skipping dialogue never skips required actions.

## Card-art naming and replacement proposals
1. Inventory every image across the referenced conversation turns, in arrival order, before assigning requested serial numbers. Count distinct artworks against the requested range; if fewer are available, number only the verified subset and ask for the remainder rather than inventing entries. Include a concise visual identifier for each mapping so the author can verify order.
2. When the author says to ignore card text, use only the artwork to name the depicted creature. Do not infer identity, powers, rarity, or continuity from placeholder names or ability text printed on the supplied mockups; the separate live catalog may be consulted only to choose a thematically suitable existing art slot.
3. For each artwork, propose a distinct name and a specific existing card ID as an **art replacement**, explaining the visual or gameplay-role fit. Check the current catalog and established Necroid roster first to avoid accidental name collisions; do not imply that replacing art also renames a card, changes its effects, or canonizes the creature. Do not update GBrain or project data when the request is proposals only, especially when the user explicitly forbids memory changes.

## Necroid codename proposals
- For myth-inspired Convergence Necroid names, use original, concise in-universe codenames that echo the requested mythic atmosphere; do not use actual Greek gods, the twelve Titans, or other mythological names verbatim when the author asks for names *inspired by* rather than copied from mythology. Favor plausible field designations over ornate fantasy-monster epithets.

Names remain proposals until selected; do not imply a visual alone proves powers, class, or canon status.

## Expansion and review discipline
- Favor a small number of core rules plus concise card-specific exceptions.
- The whole-series cast is the starting point. Faction identities can color individual cards, but they are not substitutes for a compelling universe-wide gameplay concept. Quantums can express teamwork; IOSIS can express creation, sacrifice and reconstruction; NuHumas can express adaptation. Treat specific abilities as design proposals, not established lore.
- When asked for alternative mechanics, present options rather than adopting all of them. Explain the decision each creates, complexity cost and balance risk.
- Do not claim thematic reskins or numerical changes alone distinguish the game mechanically.
- Compare combinations carefully: simultaneous combat and alternating single actions are different structures, not automatically compatible modules.
- Do not resurrect the old 25-card/Commander/one-card-one-attack design; the later implemented-game compendium's 20 Life Force is a distinct source, not that abandoned baseline.
- Qualify undecided duplicate limits, deck-building budgets, empty-deck rules, balance, and keywords as part of the earlier design discussion; the later compendium specifies several of them for its snapshot. When discussing the live game, check its current implementation or developer confirmation rather than presenting old open questions as current facts.
