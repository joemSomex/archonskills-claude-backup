# Extracting expressive avatar frames from chroma-key renders

Use when the outer frame may be asymmetrical, but the requested circular or rounded-square photo opening and surrounding canvas must be genuinely transparent. For an exact numeric corner radius, also follow `rounded-square-rendering.md`; a generated rounded square alone is only approximate.

1. Generate each concept independently from the relevant original style references. Specify one centered, fully visible frame and a flat saturated green key in **both** the entire requested photo aperture and the outside; for a square aperture, explicitly demand four straight sides and rounded corners rather than allowing a circular ring or landscape plaque. Forbid black fill, shadows, gradients, checkerboard, watermark, text and portraits. Reserve a different structural silhouette for each concept before prompting, because changing tiny seams on the same torus is not a new frame. If a provider rejects an oversized or alpha-bearing reference image, make a bounded RGB copy for the upload (for example, a 1200px-max thumbnail), leaving the original untouched, then retry; this preserves the visual guidance while avoiding invalid-input image uploads.
2. Inspect the encoded pixels before extraction: confirm green at the center and all corners, and that the frame occupies a plausible share of the canvas. Measure the green center opening *after the complete outer silhouette is scaled into its padded output canvas*. For circles, use radial rays; for rounded squares, label connected green components, select the component containing the image center, measure its bounds and the first opaque pixel along all four straight axes. Reject or regenerate when the opening is too small relative to the fitted outer silhouette; the center component can be smaller than an attractive-looking thumbnail suggests. Prompts about a 'large hole' are not reliable dimensional controls, and enlarging a too-small opening by deletion can cut through the designed collar. When generated backgrounds vary slightly around the requested green, classify by `dominance = G - max(R,B)` rather than exact RGB equality. Sample the actual center/corner key RGB first. For a near-`(4,249,7)` key, `alpha = clip((185 - dominance)/132, 0, 1)` preserved antialiased metal edges in a tested batch; tune both bounds to the current candidate rather than using them as universal thresholds. Recover edge foreground color before resizing with `foreground = clip((RGB - (1-alpha)*sampled_key)/max(alpha, 0.01), 0, 255)`; merely deleting green pixels leaves a colored fringe. Keying only the outside leaves the apparent central hole opaque.
3. Replace green-contaminated RGB on partially transparent antialiased edges with a Gaussian-weighted average of nearby **opaque frame pixels** (`blur(RGB * opaque) / blur(opaque)`), or solve the foreground color from the measured green key and soft alpha before resizing. After resizing to final dimensions, set RGB to zero for very low-alpha pixels (for an 8-bit image, alpha below roughly 8 is a useful starting point), then inpaint residual green-dominant pixels (`G > 1.10*max(R,B)`, with a brightness floor) from nearby metal. Recheck the inpainted RGB and neutralize any surviving key color. Inspect a native-size crop of both inner and outer rails: resizing unpremultiplied RGBA can leave vivid green RGB beneath alpha 1-7, which looks like neon hairlines in enlarged previews even though a full-size contact sheet appears clean. For a strictly gold/silver/black finish, identify adequately visible off-palette green/magenta chromatic-aberration by HSV hue and saturation, replace only those RGB values with neutral steel at their existing HSV Value, and keep alpha and valid gold reflections untouched; inspect again after any subsequent gold-hue grade because RGB rounding can reintroduce isolated off-palette pixels. Require clean dark- and light-matte composites after this final cleanup. Keep details that intentionally detach from the main collar only when clearly part of the frame.
4. If a generation nearly touches the canvas edge, crop to the nonzero-alpha bounding box and uniformly scale the frame into a padded transparent canvas; never stretch axes separately or clip swept tips. Do not rescale a correctly padded frame merely to homogenize an expressive set.
5. Verify each standalone PNG after decoding: requested dimensions and RGBA mode, alpha zero at center and all four canvas edges, sufficient radial clearance for a circle or centered connected transparent area plus four-axis clearance for a rounded square, plausible foreground bounding box, no large stray component, unique hash, and exact batch count. A generated corner that only looks close to a requested numeric radius is not an exact measurement; apply and validate the deterministic mask when exactness is required. For 'more' batches, persist each bounded generation/extraction batch to a manifest and verify the requested count and hashes against both the new files and the previously delivered slate; compare macro silhouettes at thumbnail size, since new filenames or unique alpha hashes do not establish genuinely new constructions. Inspect all results on dark/light mattes and zoom into suspicious edges or ornamental endpoints at native size; high file-count/valid alpha alone does not establish that the designs differ meaningfully. Do not deliver the internal contact sheet.
