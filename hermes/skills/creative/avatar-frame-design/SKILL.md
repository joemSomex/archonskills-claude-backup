# Avatar frame design

## When to Use

Use when creating standalone circular or rounded-square profile/avatar borders, tiered frame collections, or reference-driven decorative frames that must keep a transparent profile aperture.

## Workflow

1. Inspect every supplied reference by visible content and assign roles: palette, material finish, frame construction, and hierarchy. When the user replies to or explicitly approves a particular delivered slate, treat **that slate** as the primary construction, thickness, palette and finish reference even if newer slates exist; vary details rather than importing unrelated circular badge or later thin-frame geometry. For a follow-up asking for "more" without repeating technical specifications, retain the approved slate's dimensions, separate-file delivery, transparent aperture and transparent surrounding canvas unless the new brief overrides them; style continuity alone does not make an opaque or differently sized asset usable. If a single repaired frame is approved and the user asks for more, make the requested count of new distinct frames in that repaired family instead of silently converting older unapproved candidates. Choose a cohesive palette from the **current brief and references**, not an unrelated Quantum XIII slate: a tightly specified brand-color tier set may use gold/navy/cyan, while a reference-driven industrial set may instead use nickel/gunmetal with restrained red and selective warm gold. For Cadet rank recolors, treat silver as the dominant metal and restrained gold as the secondary material, with graphite supporting recesses; provide reusable primary/secondary/accent HEX values, but do not freeze an unapproved exact swatch as a rank standard. Exclude reference-sheet text, portraits, logos, watermarks, and unrelated shapes.
2. Choose the construction branch before production. For a rounded-square avatar brief, keep the exterior and aperture square with rounded corners. If a numerical radius must be exact, construct complete plates within a deterministic geometric mask from the outset; see `references/rounded-square-rendering.md`. For expressive generated frames where the user accepts visual corner rounding, prioritize the requested output size and intact designed details over forcing an exact radius with a post-generation mask, and disclose the radius as approximate. Design complete plates and corner ornaments to taper into the side rails **before** export; cropping generated motifs afterward leaves visibly amputated details even when alpha checks pass. Match the border weight of the brief or specifically approved reference; do not apply a universal thin-frame default to a request for more designs in an approved medium-border family. Reserve macro constructions before generation and compare a calibration slate at avatar size; color-only swaps and a common annulus with tiny different fittings do not count as distinct designs. If a deterministic trial reads as the same narrow outline ten times, switch to independently generated topology candidates before completing the batch, because unique alpha hashes and ornament details cannot supply visible structural variety. When asked to mix complexity, deliberately interleave minimal, medium and intricate concepts instead of making one uniform template or a monotonic tier ramp, while keeping each opening usable. For an intricate concept, verify that the final thumbnail actually shows layered, connected construction; regenerate a plain rail decorated only with cuts or micro-grooves, because prompt detail and a unique hash do not create visible complexity. For a strict request for *perfectly circular outer silhouettes* and ranked progression, reserve distinct annular topologies with visibly changing width, plates and light architecture; tiny changed ticks on one repeated ring are not distinct. Use deterministic supersampled rendering, clipping every decoration to a mathematical annulus; see `references/circular-rendering.md`. For an expressive brief that prioritizes visual variety over rigid uniformity, reserve different **macro silhouettes** (swept crescents, sculpted chrome, segmented gate, scarred collar, intersecting orbit, circuit inlay, angular anchors) and generate independently from the assigned original references. Do not force expressive concepts back onto one common annulus or copy a reference frame literally.
3. Make the photo aperture genuinely open and match the requested geometry (circular or rounded square) in every branch. When the frame must be a thin accent, measure the clear opening against the **complete padded silhouette**, including protrusions: aim for an opening span of at least roughly 70% of the outer span, not merely a narrow metal band near the hole. Long compass tips can make a nominally thin ring dominate the portrait; shorten the tips and regenerate rather than deleting the inner rim. For generative expressive frames, request perfectly flat vivid chroma green across the entire opening and outside canvas, then key it to RGBA; a model can render fake checkerboard or black in the hole despite a transparency prompt. Follow `references/chroma-keyed-frames.md` for extraction and validation. Before keying, decode the candidate's actual pixels and reject or regenerate a non-square frame when the brief requires square geometry, even if the generator request asked for a square; stretching or padding a rectangular design to square changes its proportions. Also reject an opening that becomes too small after fitting the whole silhouette with padding; cutting a wider hole later can destroy the designed inner rim. Do not include text, watermarks, portraits or symbols inside the aperture.
4. Export independent PNGs at the requested dimensions. For a continuation of an approved slate, compare each new frame with its corresponding approved design for outer scale, aperture size, palette and opaque-pixel coverage (`alpha > 127`); if generated borders are thicker, do **not** match coverage by blindly dilating the transparent aperture: that cuts off inward plates, rails and filigree. Regenerate with the correct opening or redesign those details to fit; compare full-resolution source and export at every inner edge, not just a thumbnail contact sheet. Inspect every secondary rail, plate and decorative arc endpoint even when no mask was used; image generation can leave a dangling or sharply unfinished termination that looks like a crop. Regenerate that candidate with an explicit capped joint or smooth taper before delivery. When the brief supplies exact metallic HEX anchors, measure hue/saturation only on adequately lit opaque pixels after extraction: a generator can drift from specified gold toward orange. Gently shift existing warm-metal chroma toward the requested gold, taper saturation in bright glints and deep recesses, and preserve Value, alpha and geometry; fixed HEX colors are material anchors, not a flat tint to paste across reflections. Recheck the graded output on both mattes. Verify exact requested count, nonidentical hashes, centered complete silhouette, alpha-zero aperture and canvas edges. When the user specifies a numerical corner radius, do not call a generated approximation exact: apply the output-coordinate rounded-square mask and verify the radius, or explicitly disclose that it was only a visual target. Require a common outer size **only** for the strict tier branch; for expressive frames preserve proportional variety and uniformly scale only oversized candidates to restore padding. At thumbnail scale compare macro silhouettes and interior clarity, and use dark/light mattes to spot stray backgrounds or fringes. Deliver each standalone image in chat; keep QA sheets internal.
