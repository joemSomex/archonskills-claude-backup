# Story synopsis localization

Translate English story summaries into natural, cinematic Chinese that reads like original Chinese promotional copy rather than a sentence-by-sentence translation.

## Procedure

1. **Lock names and terminology first.** Preserve English character names exactly and load any project glossary before drafting. For QUANTUM XIII material, read `references/quantum-xiii-glossary.md`.
2. **Map the narrative beats.** Identify setup, destination, prior relationship, condition, danger, and final hook. Preserve every beat while allowing Chinese sentence boundaries to differ from the English.
3. **Draft for Chinese narrative flow.** Use explicit subjects and causal transitions where Chinese needs them. Prefer idiomatic phrases such as “旧识”“提出一个条件”“不速之客” over literal English syntax.
4. **Clarify pronouns naturally.** Replace ambiguous English pronouns with names when needed, especially across paragraph breaks.
5. **Shape the escalation.** Let each paragraph advance one stage: arrival, bargain, danger or reversal. End on the suspense beat without explaining beyond the source.
6. **Run a translationese pass.** Remove calqued clauses, overloaded relative constructions, unnatural passive voice, and stiff phrases such as “被建造用于进入”. Read the result as Chinese genre copy, not as a bilingual alignment exercise.
7. **Return only the polished translation unless analysis is requested.** Do not add commentary, alternate versions, or terminology notes when the user asks only for the translation.

## Always-on rules

- Preserve English character names in Latin letters exactly as supplied.
- Prefer natural Chinese storytelling over word-for-word correspondence while preserving plot facts and suspense.
- Keep established project terms consistent; never improvise a new translation for a term already in the glossary.
- Do not over-compress relationship or motivation beats that make the synopsis easy to follow.
- Avoid translationese even when the literal version is grammatically correct.
