# Specialist equipment in reference-driven character batches

Use this when adapting a character's palette/materials to a different medical, research or engineering job, when a compact tool's silhouette could read as a weapon, or when attaching giant wearable arms and projected defenses.

1. **Assign reference roles.** Inventory every held object, belt canister, shoulder attachment, marking and hand contact in the source. Keep only the requested identity or style features; exclude props tied to the source's old job. A color-matched tube rack can silently make an engineer read as a medic.
2. **Reserve distinct functional silhouettes before rendering.** For a medical/researcher character, choose sealed sample vessels, shallow instrument trays, flat diagnostic displays or rounded manipulators. For an engineer, choose a blunt short torque wrench with visible open/hex head, hinged rubber-lined clamp connected to a pack, parts tray, junction box, cable loop or panel tester. Vary headgear, shoulder/chest/waist connections and the tool's physical mounting across concepts, not just its red color or surface markings.
3. **Bind tools to the body.** State which hand grips the part and where its wrist, cable, hinge or pack mount connects. Keep tools short and blunt unless a longer silhouette is expressly required; parallel protruding cylinders, needle clusters and slender barrel-like projections can read as firearms regardless of their label. A backpack manipulator must trace from pack through each joint to its working jaws, not float behind the shoulder.
4. **Inspect each candidate twice.** At contact-sheet size, ask whether the role reads immediately from silhouette rather than from the prompt. At native size, check hand-to-tool contact, open jaws/caps, cable endpoints, visible contents of trays/cases, and any copied source vial or weapon. Replace the candidate if the tool communicates another role, resembles a gun, floats, or leaves the full-body frame. Unique hashes do not establish distinct work functions.

## Wearable machine arms and energy shields

1. **Resolve the limb topology before rendering.** Distinguish two exoskeleton arms enclosing the character's own arms from two additional back-mounted manipulators; state the total visible arm count and shoulder-to-elbow-to-fist load path in every prompt. Otherwise oversized gauntlets can become detached hands or an unintended extra pair of arms.
2. **Preserve the underlying physique independently.** Lock shoulder breadth, torso mass and thigh thickness from the designated body reference before adding machinery; bulky gauntlets alone do not establish a muscular character.
3. **Attach each shield to a specific emitter.** Require one outer-forearm emitter and one separate translucent energy-shield plane on each arm, with visible luminous edges and transparent centers. Keep both arms separated and angle the shield planes away from the face; a generic glow can render as armor illumination, one merged barrier or a background halo rather than two mounted shields.
4. **Reserve meaningful mechanical variation.** Vary forearm cross-section, actuator arrangement, knuckle blocks, shoulder load path and chest connections across designs while holding the requested palette and anatomy fixed. Keep luminous accent color separate from painted armor area so an orange-lit black suit does not become bright orange armor.
5. **Inspect the assembled silhouette and attachments.** Trace both arms to their fists and both shield planes to their forearm emitters, check all shield tips and hands remain inside the frame, and compare actuator and housing construction across the numbered batch. Enlarge ambiguous regions rather than treating transparent effects on a contact sheet as proof of two complete shields.
