# Reference-driven character design

## When to Use

Use for standalone character concepts and revisions from visual references. Use character-sheet-redesign for an explicit multi-view redesign sheet and reference-driven-image-generation for non-character subjects.

## Procedure

### Resolve prompt revisions before rendering

For same-series original specialists, separate the series reference's rendering/material language from the other references' loose face and wardrobe inspiration. Design a new face and garment construction rather than recoloring either source, and do not carry the previous batch's invented hairstyle or complexion into a newly supplied reference set. Translate tailoring corrections into physical endpoints: normal sleeves end at the wrists with hands visible; reduced strapping means hidden closures, integrated supply modules and no dangling webbing or thigh harness. Keep permitted long coat hems independent of sleeve length, because a long coat does not authorize oversized cuffs. When both short and long clothing are permitted across a batch, reserve distinct hem lengths and collar/chest/waist constructions before dispatching the independent calls. Keep white accents as local fabric when requested, not replacement white armor, and keep orange accents as sparse embedded light slots rather than broad glowing trim.

When asked only to modify a written instruction, return the revised prompt without generating images. When a subsequent message asks to generate according to that instruction, use the latest revised prompt as the complete active brief, retaining its compatible source references and explicit batch count; do not restore superseded ages, equipment or styling from earlier outputs. Do not let the word 'card' alone replace an explicitly retained full-body neutral-studio presentation with an unrelated action-card template.

Translate emphasis corrections into a visible mass hierarchy, not just extra adjectives. For a boxing-arm mecha specialist, prioritize the continuous shoulder drive -> upper-arm actuator -> elbow transmission -> forearm pressure/damping module -> wrist lock -> articulated striking fist; simplify torso and leg plating so the arm system remains dominant without sacrificing boxing mobility. Exclude physical shields, projected barriers, energy disks and shield-like forearm slabs individually when shields are forbidden, because large generic gauntlets can otherwise drift into defensive equipment. Keep a raised guard outside the face silhouette.

When the user rejects a geometric design language, replace its construction positively rather than merely prohibiting it: replace visible ring joints with concealed pivots under faceted housings and circular emitters with short recessed straight light slots. Retain compatible material and palette cues without feeding rejected topology back through reference descriptions, because a style reference can otherwise restore the removed shapes.

For a newly supplied mechanical-structure-only reference, extract its load paths, shell layering and fist articulation without inheriting its head, species, palette, decals or rendering finish. Preserve the active human identity, age, presentation and equipment exclusions; a robot reference does not replace a requested exposed human face.

For variants of a selected accepted image, use that selected image independently for every slot and bound changes to the requested components. When removing exposed arm connection rods, replace the open rod-and-brace architecture with overlapping closed forearm shells and internal actuation, not recolored external bars; preserve a plausible shoulder-to-elbow-to-wrist load path. Keep unaffected leg hardware and accepted face/body proportions unless the user also asks to change them. Reserve distinct replacement shell and fist constructions across variants, because changing only pose or lighting does not provide structural alternatives.

Carry an explicit age correction into every independent prompt through compatible face, hair and beard cues. For an approximately thirty-year-old design, avoid inherited gray hair, salt-and-pepper beards and elderly facial treatment; changing only the numeric age can leave an earlier veteran reference visually dominant. These age and role settings belong to the active brief, not every future character.

1. **Recover the active brief and source roles.** Inspect every current attachment and map face/identity, body proportions, wardrobe/armor, pose, material/style and background separately. When the user names the unit's role or corrects it, translate that role into visible mass, equipment, and action before reserving variants: a heavy infantry unit needs weighty planted charges, braces, drops, and close counterstrikes rather than interchangeable agile poses; a medic needs rescue/triage actions rather than a generic rifle stance. For named specialist roles, separate the reference's transferable material grammar from its old job-specific props before generating; reserve visible role-appropriate equipment and reject borrowed gear that changes the unit's function. Use `references/specialist-equipment.md` for medical versus engineering tool geometry, wearable machine arms, projected shields and attachment QA. If a named reference does not contain the requested equipment but another attachment does, preserve the explicit body/hair/style assignments, briefly disclose which attachment supplies the equipment, and proceed when the intended combination is clear; silently renumbering sources can apply the wrong traits. Keep the current source's actual held equipment and markings subject to its brief, and discard the previous batch's role, even when its armor and environment look similar. When one reference is explicitly for body type, lock its proportion relationships (torso size, arm reach, hip span, leg length, knee articulation and ground contacts) before interpreting later armor references; otherwise a full-bodied armor image can silently replace the intended anatomy with a conventional human silhouette. Translate relative limbs into visible landmarks in every prompt: if legs must exceed arms, end both fingertips at upper or mid-thigh above the kneecaps and place both feet well below a high pelvis; if knees must be mildly flexed, angle thighs outward to knee pivots and shins inward to planted feet, not merely say "bent" (which often renders straight legs). When a reference is a social-media or video screenshot, identify the actual artwork boundary first; treat usernames, music labels, player icons and other interface chrome as noise, not transferable typography or character markings. For a character posed on a vehicle, separate outfit evidence from vehicle hardware and decals. For a standing character sheet, keep only actual garment markings and infer obscured clothing conservatively so the machine does not migrate into the costume. For a mounted-rider card or key art, instead lock the rider AND distinctive vehicle as a coupled identity in every independent prompt: inventory helmet/visor, suit, vehicle silhouette, nose, side pods, central hub, hover mechanism and lights; reserve different camera directions as well as maneuvers, because a renamed action can still render the same three-quarter bike view. Distinguish genuine on-costume identifiers and emblems from screenshot chrome: preserve source markings when identity or outfit fidelity is required, interpreting a generic 'no text/logos' negative as no *invented* markings unless the user explicitly requests removal of all lettering; verify generated glyphs at native size because plausible small text often mutates. New attachments and explicit overrides replace only their assigned roles; an earlier batch's baldness, face covering, nose/mouth treatment, palette, weapon or pose must not leak into a new request. When the user selects numbered favorites from the last delivered batch and asks for new designs, resolve those numbers to the exact delivered files before generation. Use the favorites as an accepted envelope for fit, material balance, accent restraint and studio framing, not as topology to clone; retain compatible original constraints and reserve new collar/chest/shoulder/waist/joint connection graphs across the requested count. Otherwise a reference-conditioned batch can look like recolors of the favorites rather than new concepts. If a new message supersedes an unfinished batch, discard pending slots and build a fresh manifest from the new originals.
2. **Lock observable anatomy before aesthetics.** For an eye reference, record count, image-space side, approximate width relative to face, iris color and which opposite region is concealed. When only one eye is visible, specify an opaque full-orbit occluder on the correct image-space side and inspect the enlarged result for apertures or red glints leaking a second eye; textured openwork can look solid in a thumbnail. When adapting a profile eye to a frontal body, specify geometry rather than copying the source person's identity or eye color. Define the hairline and actual hair texture positively when a new haircut supersedes a bald or helmeted source. For explicit visor removal and ethnicity/hair changes, carry those overrides into every independent prompt: describe the requested adult facial appearance, uncovered eyes and face, and exact hair color separately from haircut geometry; exclude replacement goggles, masks and helmets so the original face covering cannot survive in another form. Verify uncovered faces and hair color on enlarged head crops, since dark visor bands and warm hair highlights are easy to miss on full-body thumbnails. If the face reference has closed eyelids, keep them closed rather than painting a default iris that cannot be seen. For a fully opaque helmet or visor, keep the face hidden even in an enlarged portrait; generic face-sculpting instructions in a style-conversion brief do not justify inventing an unmasked person. When one closed eye remains but the opposite orbit is replaced by organic cavities, name the image-space sides and require the entire missing orbit to have no eyelid, eyeball or pupil; inspect both orbits at native size and replace a candidate that restores a second human eye beneath decorative holes. State nose/mouth visibility from the *current* brief, not a prior prompt. Translate anatomical landmarks in the brief into explicit visible endpoints on both sides of the face: a mouth extension that must reach the ears needs a continuous line from each lip corner to each ear base, not a conventional wide smile or short cheek markings. Keep the redesigned facial proportions and expression distinct from the source's exact makeup or tooth pattern. Put skin effects on named exposed patches; distinguish them from armor cracks, red piping or all-over face paint. For a sculptural, masked or eyeless alien face, first inventory the source's dominant color area, outer contour, cheek seams, chin shape, hairline and actual openings; keep only the permitted occlusion/material cues, then reserve a genuinely different face construction before generation. A nearly literal pointed mask remains a copy even with altered armor, while an arbitrary high-contrast two-tone split can look pasted-on rather than attractive. Prefer a coherent shell with deliberate brow, cheek and jaw load paths; compare the first candidate against both the source and any rejected correction at thumbnail and native head-crop size, then change topology rather than only recoloring it. Specify absent eyes explicitly so neither invented eyeballs nor false LED eyes leak into a sealed face.
3. **Resolve count and reserve variations before each call.** Distinguish one character design shown in N images from N independent character designs: lock the former's anatomy, asymmetric parts and armor connections across views while varying composition or pose; for the latter, choose a different silhouette and armor/garment connection graph (head, shoulders, chest, waist, legs), not just a new adjective or hash. A set of similar renders is not proof of one consistent model, and different hashes are not proof of distinct designs. When an imposing creature reference is assigned to exterior silhouette but the user asks for a slender stealth variant, reserve narrower shoulder/hip mass, lower armor volume and quieter appendage count before varying panels; a narrow waist alone still produces a heavy warrior with large chest shells or a dominant tail. Check the first full-body thumbnail for that mass hierarchy before dispatching the batch. For identity-locked style conversions of one source character, do the opposite: hold the silhouette, outfit construction and palette fixed, and reserve distinct rendering treatments (plane angularity, brushwork, shading, material emphasis) before each call; changing armor topology would turn stylistic variations into unauthorized costume redesigns. For action-card batches with a locked identity, vary the POSE silhouette and camera scale independently of outfit topology: preassign contrasting actions such as a close guarded recoil, low crouch, side leap, braced parry and upward climb. Also reserve contrasting environment topology, not just new place names: a reference with a dominant vertical neon corridor can keep reproducing the same central tower behind every supposedly different action. Rebuild repeated slots from the original references with a physically different setting (for example, an enclosed low-ceiling depot with diagonal beams or a broad horizontal runoff tunnel), while retaining only the reference's lighting and atmosphere. Inspect the first numbered contact sheet for a shared arms-spread/three-quarter pose template; if several cards collapse to that template despite differently worded action prompts, replace those slots with explicit limb placement and a tighter or lower camera rather than merely changing the adjective or background color. Keep a designated body-type reference's support topology across the batch while varying the head outline, shoulder/thorax structure, pelvis and limb housings independently; name the limb count and planted foot contacts in every prompt so stylistic variation does not turn a biped into a cropped torso or an extra-legged machine. When a reference is exterior-silhouette-only but the requested subject is a quadrupedal crawler, do not inherit its upright pose or hanging stilt-like strands: reserve a horizontal trunk and exactly two shoulder-rooted forelegs plus two hip-rooted hindlegs, use a three-quarter side camera that exposes all four paths, and trace each to a distinct planted foot at native size before accepting the slot. Reserve different thorax/hip mass and fore-versus-hind leg proportions across the batch; recolored plates on one repeated four-legged body are not independent designs. For a correction asking the same crawler to feel giant and threatening, rebuild independently from the original references with monumental shoulder/hip volume, column-thick load-bearing legs, oversized claws, fine bolts as relative scale cues, a floor-level camera and heavy contact shadows; do not simply upscale a slender crawler. Reserve safe side margins before rendering and reject a close-up whose claw tips touch the frame, even if its apparent mass is stronger. A white studio shot without an external comparator can suggest scale through proportions and camera but cannot prove an exact physical height. With a faceless or masked source, reserve different exposed-skin footprints, eye-window placement, cowl boundaries and jaw outlines before generating; changing only the body armor produces a batch of near-identical heads. When the user asks for more independent designs after a similar-looking batch, reserve materially different body plans and support/locomotion topologies before generating (for example, a low gantry, tall lift frame, tracked or wheeled base, tripod, secondary manipulator pair, or asymmetric arm), and inspect the actual topology before naming a variant: a prompt or unique file hash cannot prove the model made the requested legs or appendages. When the user says only the character or robot may appear, remove held loads, action vignettes, detached detail tiles, functional props, diagram dividers and scenic context from EVERY slot; communicate the role through attached hardware and requested subject views instead, because a function-demo inset violates a subject-only image even if the main figure is correct. For a batch of different monster species, reserve different head geometry, torso architecture, limb topology, and locomotion before rendering; if the user excludes animal imagery, invent abstract alien body plans rather than defaulting to wolf, bird, insect, reptile, crab, or amphibian analogues. For a double-headed or shoulder-borne symbiote, map the host head, secondary head, shoulder load path and any required head-to-head tissue bridge before generating; in the full-size result trace the secondary skull through its neck/root to the host's occiput, verify both heads are distinct, the requested host head remains visible, and any requested open jaws have separate upper and lower rows of teeth. If the user replaces a human host head with a faceless alien shell, explicitly remove hair, realistic skin, nose and lips from EVERY prompt and check every head crop: a generic 'male' or prior face reference can restore a human portrait despite the new mask reference. Otherwise the model can turn a living connected head into a floating mouth, shoulder ornament, helmet, or extra creature. When the requested alien should be geometric rather than animal-like, inspect tapered muzzles and beaks separately because they can change its species read. Keep figure count, view, full-body boundary, backdrop, palette and ratio explicit in every independent prompt. Treat empty hands as a default only: if the current source or user requires a held object, map its gripping hand, silhouette, screen/face, accent colors and any cable's two attachment points before generation; require the same prop in all views where that hand is visible. For a named specialist role, compare an unarmed calibration against one with role-defining equipment when the references support it: a helmet optic and cloak alone may read as generic reconnaissance rather than a sniper, while a safely carried original precision rifle makes the role legible. If the user requests an unarmed character, express the role through attached optics and tactical loadout instead; never silently replace a depicted weapon with empty hands. Do not make a new cropped prop reference unless needed for fidelity: an extra crop of a multi-view source can prompt an unintended fourth panel even when the prompt says three views. For an asymmetric translucent drape, wing, cable, or side plate in a multi-view sheet, record its physical attachment side and count before generation, then translate screen side across views (a feature on front image-right appears on rear image-left). Inspect both full figures at native size and reject a mirrored extra piece or missing rear feature; a correct front and portrait can conceal an invented symmetric back. When an exact finger/claw count is requested, name the thumb and four fingers separately on EACH hand and pose both open palms away from dark clothing against a contrasting background; overlapping talons can hide a missing digit. Bound reference leakage: a clothing source's sword, tail, long hair, mask, caption and scenery do not become character attributes unless requested. For unarmed designs exclude held, holstered and armor-integrated weapons separately. When the brief requires a single long appendage such as a barbed tail, reserve lateral white space and specify one visible root, continuous shaft, requested dorsal barb row and a free terminal tip; a curled shape that disappears behind the body does not prove the endpoint exists. For multiple tails, resolve the requested count before batching, assign each tail its own visible pelvic root, path and free tip in a different part of the frame, and trace all paths at native size; overlapping loops, neck spurs and forked shafts can falsely read as the right count in a contact sheet. For a low, forward-leaning quadruped with long legs, anchor the head below the shoulder arch and the chest close to the ground while keeping the back roughly horizontal; 'long limbs' alone often turns the intended crouch into an upright stilt animal. If the tail REPLACES the legs, define its root at the waist and forbid a split pelvis, thighs, knees and feet; calibrate the first render against a loose trailing S/J silhouette longer than the torso before batching, since a tight ring or hidden tip can satisfy 'no legs' but miss 'long trailing tail'. In the contact sheet check each root-to-tip path, then zoom into longitudinal armor rails that could be mistaken for extra tails. For a named hairstyle such as slicked-back dreadlocks, specify where individual locks begin at the hairline, their backward direction and how they gather at the nape; otherwise scalp ridges or tentacles can falsely read as hair.
4. **Generate one standalone file per call with the allowed provider.** For a fresh identity-locked batch use original sources independently in every call; do not cascade generated candidates into later slots. For an originality-locked suit or wardrobe batch, first extract permitted material and engineering cues from each reference, then reserve a distinct collar, shoulder, chest, waist, joint and leg-seam connection graph per slot. Calibrate one full-body image before parallel generation: compare its armor topology against the source and check that micro accent lights stayed sparse. If image conditioning reproduces a source's cup-shaped chest shells, broad shoulder plates, color blocks or headwear despite explicit exclusions, switch to a fresh text-only prompt built from those distilled cues rather than feeding the full reference pixels again; name the forbidden component positively as a continuous new panel or flush joint so the original shape has no place to reappear. Verify the corrected sample at full-body and detail scale before batching, then inspect a numbered contact sheet for generic near-duplicate bodysuits, copied topology, enlarged accent trim and unintended exposure. A negative 'do not copy' clause alone rarely overcomes a dominant visual reference. For an explicitly iterative correction, edit the selected source and retain accepted identity/framing while naming the changed material or anatomy. If repeated tiny armor tiles read as reptile skin and the user wants less of that texture, replace the micro-scale construction with fewer broad interlocking plates and deliberate seams across every view; smoothing the old scales alone preserves the unwanted pattern. Treat painterly rendering and battle wear as separate controls: put broad brush-shaped light/shadow transitions on intact matte plates while explicitly excluding scratches, chips, peeled paint and grunge. Inspect the armor at thumbnail and native size after each pass, because a distress-removal edit can revert to glossy CGI even if it cleans the surfaces. Treat a request for a 'realistic character portrait' as a rendering/face-realism correction, not permission to turn a previously specified full-body 2:3 batch into head-and-shoulders crops; change the crop only when the user explicitly requests a close-up or bust. For live-action realism, specify a photographed adult human actor, skin pores and natural asymmetry, strand-level hair, physically wearable cloth/armor and photographic studio light; explicitly reject anime proportions, concept-art brushwork, plastic CGI and game-render surfaces. Calibrate one at native resolution against the rejected stylized batch before repeating the correction, because merely adding 'photorealistic' to a prompt can retain its original illustrated face. For the opposite conversion from a photo/CG source to dimensional painterly animation, lead with a positive instruction to draw by hand and place broad brush-shaped tonal planes on EVERY view, including both full bodies and the portrait; a generic 'painterly' adjective can leave photo-like bodies beside a painted close-up. Inspect one candidate at thumbnail size before continuing the batch, and replace a style failure rather than applying a flat stylization filter to it. If a photoreal human or mechanical source keeps overpowering the requested hand-painted treatment, generate an abstract material-and-brushwork swatch without any character, matching only the CURRENT source's skin/hair/fabric/armor materials that actually appear. Use the swatch for style only alongside the ORIGINAL image for identity, anatomy, costume and held props; inspect one three-view test at thumbnail and native size before resuming. Keep the swatch's shapes, panel layout, palette accents and background out of the character design; the style guide must not become a new subject reference. If the full facial reference keeps imposing its signature head silhouette despite explicit redesign prompts, make a verified crop of its material or lower-face region that excludes the whole head outline, and use that crop for selected independent replacements alongside the original clothing references; keep any observable nose/mouth constraints in the text. This preserves reference roles while reducing composition leakage.
5. **Verify according to the requested gate.** Decode actual files; check count, PNG format, unique hashes and exact ratio. If an image tool returns a near-ratio canvas despite an exact aspect-ratio request, compute the largest centered integer-pixel crop with the requested ratio, inspect that all required heads, hands, feet and margins survive, then save and decode the cropped PNG. For a 9:16 portrait, a 941×1672 render can be center-cropped to 936×1664 (2 px per side, 4 px top/bottom); a 1024×1536 render needs an 864×1536 crop, so reserve and inspect much wider hand clearance first. Verify `width * 16 == height * 9` on the saved file. For an exact 2:3 full-body portrait returned on a narrower, taller canvas, do not center-crop away the head or feet: uniformly scale to the target height, then pad both sides with the measured seamless-background color to reach the exact width (for example, 1024×1536), and verify `width * 3 == height * 2` after export. Never stretch the image or silently call a near-ratio output exact. Default to fast delivery without visual review unless the user asks for review, selection or correction; state what was not inspected. For a correction, compare native-size face/hand/detail crops and a numbered contact sheet to the original and rejected set, then regenerate failed slots. When a batch combines a full-body silhouette gate with tiny eye or hair requirements, use both a full-body contact sheet and an enlarged numbered head sheet; thumbnails cannot establish slit-pupil geometry or true backward lock direction. For an extreme-neck brief, place jaw, clavicles and shoulder tops before designing armor; keep one continuous anatomical neck exposed against clear negative space on both sides, with collar and hair below or behind it. Compare chin-to-clavicle length to that candidate's own head height on both full-body and enlarged head-shoulder sheets, then replace ordinary-neck candidates: numeric ratios in a prompt do not establish the rendered geometry. For a named body-type reference, compare each full-body candidate's head-to-shoulder scale, shoulder-to-hip width, torso-to-leg length, fingertip-to-knee endpoint, separate knee angles and planted feet against that reference before accepting the armor; accurate materials can disguise a return to ordinary human proportions. Check both legs on the numbered full-body sheet, then replace straight-kneed or knee-length-hand slots from the original references; a prompt claiming slight flex or a unique file hash does not establish the joint geometry. On the head sheet, reject a face buried by shoulder armor when the reference-defining eye or jaw becomes unreadable, even if the hunch itself is correct. Check frontal eye visibility and any appendage tip separately at native resolution before accepting a replacement, since fixing head yaw can hide or truncate the tail. For an endpoint requirement such as an ear-reaching grin, inspect each side at native size against the actual landmark; a nearby seam or ordinary smile is not a pass. Recheck hands and full-body framing after a face-only edit because generative edits can alter other regions despite a localized prompt. For any exact anatomical count, inspect each relevant region at native resolution on every output and replace a slot when the count is wrong or obscured; a batch contact sheet downscales away small digits. A different hash does not prove a different character or correctly colored eye. For a painterly style conversion, inspect both full-body views independently of the enlarged portrait at thumbnail and native size; a brushed portrait beside photo-like bodies or a stylization filter outlining the original image does not pass the style gate. For a required hand-held device, zoom into the gripping hand, identifying open stock/receiver/terminal geometry and any full cable path on each file. In a multi-view sheet, trace the same single prop from hand to complete tip in both front and rear: a cloak can hide a shortened rear device that looks intact in a contact sheet. Reject a dangling free cable end, generic substitute, truncated barrel, duplicate item or a prop retained only in the portrait; none establishes that the source-held item survived. Count standing figures separately from portrait panels so a side view or extra source inset cannot masquerade as the required three-view layout. For a cloaked helmet-optic specialist sheet, check the cape's attachment side and length in front/profile/back, confirm the colored points belong to the head-mounted optic rather than the face, and count any specified light geometry on enlarged front and detail crops (for a three-point triangle, one above and two below); contact-sheet thumbnails cannot establish tiny lens counts. Inspect the exact source lettering on the unobstructed front torso as well as a close-up before accepting a slot; a heavy cape can hide a correctly rendered mark entirely, while small glyphs can mutate punctuation. Regenerate that slot with the front cape opening explicitly exposing the marked plate when either fails. In a multi-view sheet, inspect the portrait's camera side separately from the full-body views and replace a slot that turns a requested frontal face or visor into a profile or rear view; a correct back/front pair can conceal this composition failure. For an opaque helmet repeated at different scales, compare its material-panel map relative to the horn roots and visor axis (dark sides versus light center), not just palette samples or stripe width; a three-quarter portrait can make a consistent palette read as a different helmet construction. If the user rejects the match, ask which material zone is wrong before another narrow recolor, and never move the head to solve a color-layout problem. Compare the enlarged head sheet against the actual face reference as well as within the batch: reject both signature-feature copies and near-duplicate head families, even when the armor differs.
6. **Deliver only the current batch.** Post each standalone image as a bare `MEDIA:/absolute/path.png` line; report what was mechanically verified and any visual limitation without claiming unperformed inspection. If the provider refuses a design for safety, do not retry the same blocked target with cosmetic wording changes; continue any other independently planned batch slots, then offer a genuinely different safe replacement for the blocked slot and wait for agreement before generating it. If the user declines replacement, deliver every completed image promptly, state the actual count and the missing slot, and do not describe the partial batch as complete.
