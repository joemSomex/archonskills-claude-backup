# Reference-Driven Image Generation

For armor refinements and realistic product conversions, see `references/armor.md`.

## When to Use

For pixel emoji, use `references/pixel-art-emoji-production.md` and `references/animation-stabilization.md`. Latest gaze/crop corrections win. Preserve exact Frame 1 and assigned style; clarify conflicting arms. Calibrate after style rejection. Preserve PNG order/timing; await GIF approval. Deliver standalone files directly, without unsolicited approval gates or contact sheets.

Assign each reference one role; resolve unambiguous numbering/content conflicts explicitly, asking only if unclear. Generate variants concurrently from originals within scope. For hands, pass base and pose each time; track side, palm, wrist and fingers. Preserve accepted motion and unrelated pixels. Composite registered expression donors through tight masks, preserving alpha and outside-mask pixels; insert a requested blink as an extra pose. Verify counts, formats, hashes and actual differences. Lead prompts with the edit and role boundaries; repetition buries the edit. For GIFs, sample a representative RGB still. For wrist shields, retain source framing and arm axis; vary hardware, not only effects. For this user's batches, skip unsolicited visual review, contact sheets, calibration and correction loops; verify source mapping, count, format, size and distinct hashes without claiming visual acceptance. For process audits, separate image-call latency from assistant review/round-trip time; compare accuracy only on matched prompts and references against stated criteria, not hashes or model labels. For held props, see `references/held-prop-item-sheets.md`; for animation, avatar frames and robots, use topical references. Clarify object-class conflicts.

For prompt edits, revise in chat without generating; execute when requested. Separate source geometry from later style references; exclude their mannequins, text and props. For black matte metal, specify bead-blasting and low sheen, not glossy plastic or bronze-heavy steampunk. Product renders imply no validated manufacturability.

For an effect corrected from physical vines to light-only vines, regenerate from the original role-separated references. Specify branching translucent filaments emitted from the visible palms, with particles following the curves and no solid bark, roots, stems, leaves, or opaque ropes. Inspect the palm connection and transparency before batching; a glow around a solid vine is still a physical vine.

## Procedure

For armor smoothing, use medium angular plates rather than tiny tiles or seamless shells; keep topology consistent across views.

For partial-mask characters, lock coverage, hair exit and local skin effects. When exposed metal becomes flesh, specify natural skin tone/pores on every exposed part and calibrate a face and limb; metallic references otherwise leak. For a garment-only person reference with new faces, crop out the source face, then calibrate a new face and outfit silhouette; whole-person references clone identity and costume topology despite redesign wording. A many-node compound-eye reference replaces the brow/ordinary-eye zone: require multiple distinct correctly colored facets, no eyebrows or conventional eye pair, and a visible lower face. For unarmed figures, exclude held, holstered, and armor-integrated blades separately.

For an unoccupied piloted mech, show driveability through empty seats, controls, access hatch, and boarding steps rather than a human scale figure. Exclude pilots, bystanders, silhouettes, window occupants, and reflections in every main view and cockpit inset; a broad “no people” instruction can retain source scale figures or invent seated operators.

1. **Recover and version the complete active brief.** When resuming an interrupted task, read the latest relevant session and continue only the request the user has now explicitly reactivated. Match established named image-workflow triggers despite capitalization-only differences when the user supplies the required image; otherwise a case change can turn an executable request into a mere quoted phrase. An assistant's own repetition of a trigger is not a new user request. If a prompt refers to numbered images from preceding messages, retrieve and inspect those exact originals before concluding that references are missing; a later single attachment may add to rather than replace the earlier reference set. Distinguish replacement language from additive language: a newly pasted full prompt replaces the prior brief, while phrases such as “include these as well,” “make it more,” or “also add” extend the current brief and retain every compatible earlier constraint. Treat a revocation such as “forget this” or “do not use that” as removal from the active brief: immediately clear the named variant preference or calibration and do not silently restore it in later generations. Do not carry forward attributes the user explicitly superseded, such as a border, light color, camera direction, pose, effect, or preferred variant. When a requested palette replaces a locked template palette, propagate the replacement through every color-dependent clause—key, rim, fill, background glow, reflections, highlights, atmosphere, and forbidden competing colors—because a partial substitution leaves contradictory constraints that can restore the old palette. If the user adds references or a batch-wide correction while generation calls are still running, abandon the stale role map and rebuild every not-yet-accepted output from the updated canonical brief; an output made without the newly assigned references is not part of the revised batch. When a repeated batch boilerplate count conflicts with a later task-specific count in the same prompt, follow the later specific count, verify that many independent outputs, and briefly state the interpretation at delivery; otherwise the batch can overshoot the actual request. When a user distributes many source images across consecutive messages and later assigns the same named workflow to each, enumerate the complete source set in message order, deduplicate repeated attachments, and map one independent job and output group to each distinct source; do not use only the final attached image or mistake an earlier image-description question for a generation request. Confirm the collected source count against the user's stated count before processing, because partial chat-window visibility can silently drop inputs. Reuse original reference files and accepted outputs only when they still match the active brief; do not repeat completed attachment-ingest side effects. When the user repeats a completed image-batch request without a fresh attachment, resolve the last original character sheet and treat it as a new batch, not a request to resend the previous files; use the delivered batch as a visual rejection baseline and reserve new expression/gesture combinations before generation.
2. **Classify and deduplicate references by visible content before prompting.** When asked only what attached images show, inspect and describe every distinct image in the current message, keeping their descriptions separate; deduplicate repeated copies. Do not start an unrelated generation workflow or return a generic processing-failure reply when the images are accessible. A prior processing failure is not a reason to ask for a resend. If an image-inspection tool reports that the picture is already attached to the conversation, use the visible pixels rather than treating that status as an error. For generation, inspect every original image at useful size before writing the first prompt, then assign its semantic role: identity, face, expression, product/design, background, pose, wardrobe, composition, lighting, or style. When a reference is explicitly expression-only, translate its eyelid state, brow angle, mouth asymmetry, cheek lift, and gaze into facial mechanics, while locking the separate identity sheet's hair, iris color, costume, and accessories; otherwise a distinctive expression model can leak its face and palette into the new character. On a revision, re-check the original identity reference rather than inheriting an earlier prompt or generated variant's description; an invented gender presentation, facial implant, or garment detail can otherwise propagate through the entire batch. Detect byte-identical or visibly identical duplicate attachments and include only one canonical copy unless the user explicitly assigns the copies different roles; duplicates consume reference slots and can overweight one source without adding information. If filenames, platform attachment notes, and visible content disagree, trust the visible content and build a canonical ordered list for the generator—except that an explicit user role assignment always overrides the default role map. If the user's numbered Discord references outnumber the locally surfaced image paths, read the triggering message's attachment manifest and retrieve missing originals, including AVIF files, before declaring references unavailable; the message mirror can omit formats while Discord still holds every attachment. Support split-source transfers without recombining roles: a face-only reference controls identity and facial traits, a separate wardrobe-only reference controls garment construction and materials, and neither may leak its background, pose, or the other reference's identity. A composition-only reference controls subject scale, crop, headroom, vertical placement, and shot size but must not leak identity, wardrobe, pose, scars, props, or lighting. Discord can present attachment paths in an order different from the visual upload sequence, so never map roles from list position alone. When a reference is a contact sheet, collage, turnaround, or character board, first determine whether the requested output is itself a sheet. For a standalone scene, extract identity/design traits and reject the reference's panel layout and repeated subjects; otherwise the generator can reproduce the board. For a requested turnaround, count the full-body views and the small angle/detail panels separately before prompting, then preserve their exact view order, count, positions, full-body extents, repeated model identity, backdrop, and any intentionally concealed face; do not expand a side-head close-up into an extra full-body figure, because the added figure displaces the source's rear or lower-detail panel. Inventory every repeated readable mark by panel and verify each location independently when visual QA is requested; a correct main-chest mark does not imply the small rear-thigh mark survived. If only a label is missing on an otherwise intact armor panel, restore its exact text with a localized deterministic overlay aligned to the plate, inspect the result and disclose that typography is newly typeset rather than source-pixel-identical. If the assigned identity sheet shows only armor or the back of a head, lock only visible wardrobe/anatomy and label any generated face as unconstrained rather than inventing a facial match; if the subject is deliberately faceless (such as a mech), preserve that absence and redirect face-focused variation directions to visible head-shell, armor, and material design instead of inventing human features. When another panel actually shows a face, isolate that panel for identity and use the remaining views only for wardrobe. For a shared scene with two characters, assign each person an immutable identity source, image-space side, relative height, costume source and palette before generating; keep a suit-sheet model's face out of both roles and assign later references only to background or style. If the user supplies new face photos to correct an already delivered duo, supersede only the corresponding identity anchors: rebuild the requested action scenes independently from both new portraits plus the original wardrobe and environment references, using prior renders only as rejection baselines. Crop each original face and each generated face at comparable apparent size, then check each actor's hairline, face outline, eyes, nose, mouth, and skin marks separately before accepting a scene; matching suit colors and gesture do not establish likeness. Preserve the height difference at a shared ground plane rather than by placing one person closer to the camera, because perspective can fake the requested stature while breaking continuity across shots. Explicit user figure labels override the skill's usual single-character reference numbering. When a person reference is explicitly loose rather than an identity lock, extract only permitted broad traits (such as age range, complexion, or expression) and specify different facial geometry, hair construction, wardrobe, setting, and pose; otherwise the generator can recreate the photographed person instead of an original variation. When translating stylized character art into a new photoreal person, isolate the permitted design motif from the illustration's identity, palette, rendering style, and exaggerated anatomy before prompting. State the new human facial anchors positively and bound any mechanical augmentation to named anatomy (for example, articulated plates covering only the lips and lower jaw while the nose, eyes, cheeks, forehead, and natural hair remain human); otherwise the model can inherit a full mask, glowing eye, or illustrated face instead of the requested original human-looking character. In batch QA, inspect both the shared motif and meaningful changes in identity, silhouette, prosthetic geometry, viewpoint, and background rather than treating different file hashes as different designs.
3. **Keep role boundaries explicit and convert lighting references into image-space instructions.** State which attributes each reference may contribute and which it must not contribute. For studio batches, distinguish views of ONE object from members of a TEAM. For turnarounds, inventory the component graph from full-frame and tight crops, including occluded roots and dorsal versus ventral parts; verify counts and mounts in hero, side and rear views. Lock palette and markings. For teams, vary role silhouettes while sharing art direction. Name each file's camera and backdrop. For new products inspired by references, avoid their exact silhouette and load-bearing layout; transfer only intended function, material and palette. If a batch still echoes the references, stop sending those images as generation inputs: translate their permitted cues into text, calibrate one structurally distinct candidate against the references and rejected batch, then generate independent views while retaining the requested marking, studio setup, and aspect ratio. Changing color or camera around the same wing-and-fan layout is not a redesign. Pose-only references must not leak identity, clothing, lighting, background, or color; product references must control the product design rather than the face or pose. For an empty-room request based on a reference with a person, map the person as forbidden content, preserve the room's actual fixtures and architecture, and positively reconstruct the vacated surface (such as a flat empty bed, pillow, and uninterrupted wall); a negative-only 'no people' prompt can retain a body-shaped mound, reflection, or person-like shadow. Inspect every generated room at useful size for those remnants. For a piloted-vehicle design whose scene must contain no people, preserve drivability through unoccupied seats, controls, hatches, and boarding steps; forbid pilots, scale figures, silhouettes, window occupants, and reflections in every panel. Show size through cabin and access hardware rather than human comparators, because a generic 'piloted' or 'large-scale' prompt tends to add a person even when the exterior brief excludes one. For every lighting/style reference, inspect the actual pixels and spell out the key-light color, image-left/right and above/below direction, front/side/back angle, lit facial/body planes, shadow side, rim-light direction, fill strength, contrast, and falloff. For low-key environment or architecture references, also quantify the approximate shadow coverage, highlight coverage, lighting ratio, number and placement of practical sources, black-point hue, midtone hue, and whether haze reveals narrow shafts or raises the whole room. For realistic atmospheric haze, define separate foreground, midground, and background visibility: foreground edges stay crisp, contrast and sharpness decay with optical depth, practicals illuminate only nearby vapor, shafts terminate as intensity falls, and wet reflections remain dimmer, shorter, and blurrier than their sources. Forbid a uniformly luminous fog veil and broad room-filling bloom, because a dark grade alone cannot hide physically implausible scatter. When the user wants concealment, require named elements to be only partially glimpsed where light skims them and keep the remaining architecture in negative space; do not brighten the scene merely to prove every requested detail exists. Enumerate salient forbidden motifs from every role-limited reference—such as colored eyes, robots, crowds, snow, or glowing symbols—and QA each one explicitly, because high-contrast reference details can leak even when the prompt assigns only architecture or framing. Separately identify colored background glows and state whether they are key light, rim light, or only weak ambient bounce; generic phrases such as “dramatic lighting” or “red-black atmosphere” let the model put the wrong color on the face or lift an environment that should remain hidden. When a design, contact-sheet, title-card, or hologram reference contains typography, coordinates, UI labels, logos, symbols, panel borders, people, or unrelated scene content, explicitly forbid those elements and inspect for pseudo-text or collage leakage—the model otherwise treats visible reference content as part of the requested style. When approved outputs appear alongside broad stock sheets, make the approved outputs the target for material, scale, depth, and finish; bound stock sheets to general craft cues only, because their watermarks, fantasy props, layout, and colored rank language can overwhelm the intended original emblem.
4. **Preserve genuinely locked prompt text and resolve template conflicts.** Load any required prompt template. Include its body word for word and contiguously only when the user actually locked that text and it does not contradict the current request; otherwise treat the template as a default, replace its conflicting figure roles and color-dependent clauses consistently, and keep its reusable prohibitions. Front-load non-negotiable pose, anatomy, count, gaze, and motion constraints, because long prompts can underweight an appended suffix. Specify head yaw separately from eyeball target: a quarter-turned torso with direct camera gaze is not the same as looking toward the frame's lower corner. Use a suffix only for low-risk variation notes. For a character-sheet template that explicitly requires empty relaxed hands, separate source-held props from worn costume parts: omit a held weapon or tool unless the user overrides the template, while retaining its sheath, strap, mount, or other worn fixture when visible. A source identity lock does not silently override an explicit pose-and-hands rule; resolve that conflict before prompting each variation.
5. **Choose generation versus deterministic editing before calling the model.** Use `image_generate` when the user wants a new rendition and some global variation is acceptable. If the user requires the original face, crop, framing, lighting, or unrelated pixels to remain identical while changing only one region, route to `pixel-preserving-image-compositing`; image-to-image generation can redraw the full frame despite preservation language.
6. **Use authorized references only in their assigned roles, and exclude revoked ones.** Before each remake, rebuild the active reference set from the latest brief: if the user says not to use a prior icon or image, remove that source from `image_url` and `reference_image_urls`, not merely from the written prompt, because its visible features can still leak into the result. Put an authorized identity reference in `image_url` when a generative edit base is appropriate and pass the other authorized originals in `reference_image_urls` in canonical semantic order. When references are assigned as art-language inspiration but the user forbids copying their design, first inspect them and translate only permitted material, finish, engineering function and production cues into text. Reserve a positively specified new silhouette and component connection graph that differ from the references and any just-rejected batch; if image conditioning keeps restoring the prohibited reference silhouette, generate from that distilled text without attaching the reference pixels. On a correction for shared copying, compare one internally generated sample with the references before producing the rest; repeat the same new design fingerprint in every view, because different camera angles can otherwise become different vehicles rather than views of one model. Never feed a generated output into a later generation unless the user explicitly selects it or requests iterative editing. For a monocular-character remake, bind that selected image to the one-eye layout only while reserving different helmet, shoulder, torso and leg silhouettes, because a strong reference otherwise clones one suit across the batch. When correction QA is requested, inspect labeled native-size head crops to count exactly one visible iris of the specified color and an uninterrupted opaque cover over the opposite eye; full-body thumbnails hide extra apertures. For batch-wide color correction, edit each numbered output from its OWN prior file; retain only originals still valid under the new palette, calibrate one and inspect it before repeating. Exclude a colored archetype reference when removing its accent, and explicitly recolor tiny joints, lamps, seams and reflections; a generic palette command leaves residue. Do not regenerate all variants from originals, which erases accepted differences.
7. **Generate one file per call, and stop batching while a shared visual property is unresolved.** Even when the locked prompt says to create several images, append a concise instruction such as “generate only image 1 as one standalone PNG in this call,” then make a fresh `image_generate` call for each output. This prevents collages, contact sheets, and repeated crops while preserving the required prompt verbatim. For achievement-badge batches driven by a description screenshot or workbook, read the exact earning action before choosing iconography. When the user supplies new style references for a remake, make a separate style-acceptance gate: compare the candidate's surface handling, bevel scale, shadow grouping, and sculptural depth against the NEW references and the previous rejected slate, not just its icon. A prompt saying 'painterly' can still produce the same smooth metallic-disc treatment; preserve the current icon constraints while correcting material and construction from the originals. Do not copy stock-sheet watermarks, fantasy props, or sheet layouts. Distinguish posting from earning approval, a top comment from general upvotes, posts-only approval from combined posts-and-comments approval, moderator pinning from popularity, and publishing a short story from earning votes on it; otherwise adjacent achievements collapse into one symbol family. For a story-publication badge, make a recognizable bound story plus a visible release cue such as a page departing the book or a completed volume entering a public frame, then check both at 48px; an open book alone can mean reading or drafting rather than publishing. For a merch-order badge, pair a recognizable merchandise product (such as a shirt with collar and sleeves) with an unmistakable transaction carrier (bag, cart, basket, order box with purchase cue, or order slip); a product alone means ownership and a plain parcel can mean delivery instead of placing an order. Reserve these carrier topologies before generation and replace a repeated cart or bag with another purchase mechanism, because different rim geometry does not make two copies of the same merch-in-cart icon distinct. For an achievement conditioned on another member's named rank, look for the actual rank insignia before designing; an invented stripe may communicate generic seniority but cannot identify the named rank. If no authoritative mark is available, use a clearly disclosed placeholder rather than presenting it as canon. If the rank source is role-limited to shape and the user forbids its color, generate blank source-bubble sockets, then crop the source by its alpha bounds, map opaque-pixel luminance to neutral metal while preserving relief and alpha, and composite the actual geometry into those sockets. This avoids approximate redraws of distinctive tiny notches and inherited tier colors; check that transparent source RGB does not introduce fringe. Depict the direction of the interaction with separate source and response shapes and a visible connection, then inspect each finished rank mark at thumbnail size on dark and light mattes; two unconnected speech bubbles only read as generic conversation, not a reply to that member's thread. Reserve five different constructions AND meaningful center motifs, then compare each candidate's outer frame and central symbol against the favored references, neighboring achievements, and other slots; strong badge references can make every variation inherit the same segmented rim and centered icon even when prompts demand different constructions. If a semantic reference pulls all variants into the same centered object-plus-symbol layout, retain its meaning and material but assign explicit image-space geometry to replacements (for example, a stack at lower-left with its reward symbol at upper-right, or a wide object at the bottom with several reward marks rising above it); change their object count, connection graph, and negative-space regions, not merely the ring bevel. Judge both parts of the earning action and the between-slot silhouettes on a small-size contact sheet before accepting. For a posts-only upvote badge, show one post artifact receiving several physically connected approval marks, not a comment bubble or pair of post-and-comment tokens. For a moderator-pinned post badge, show the post and a recognizable thumbtack physically piercing it: specify the round head, neck, tapered point, and contact at the post surface, then inspect them at thumbnail size; tiny rivets on a card do not read as pinning. Reserve substantially different object arrangements before generating five slots (single post and pin, openwork angled mounting, pinned post stack, two visible pins, oversized pin crossing a short post), and reject lookalikes even if the rim changes. Keep post faces free of pseudo-writing; a prompt for a post or publication card can still generate parallel strokes that violate the no-typography brief. Specify positive geometry rather than only forbidding old symbols: an unmarked blank slab, key-like shaft, faux writing lines, or generic pentagon can satisfy the negative list while failing to communicate the achievement. For a compound action, require both semantic components to read at website-thumbnail size: a blank speech window with only a tiny triangle outside it loses the upvote meaning, while a free-floating arrow loses the comment meaning. Put the vote motif visibly within or structurally fused to the speech form, size it against that form, and replace only the failed slot before normalizing the final files. Reject pseudo-text strokes or comment UI lines even if no literal letters appear, because they recreate generic social-interface aesthetics. For a recurring participation achievement that counts attempts regardless of outcome, distinguish it from a correctness award before reserving concepts: use cyclic cadence, repeated neutral response tiles, or joined day/week tracks, and forbid selected-answer, solved-socket, check, and X motifs. Compare against the previous achievement's accepted icons; a repeated capsule with a pointed tail can become a speech bubble and mislabel quiz participation as commenting, so reject and replace that slot. For multi-shot environment and website-player background layout, follow `references/website-background-layout.md`. When a requested viewpoint reverses the reference's camera geometry (for example, aerial-to-ground looking up), first calibrate with the original in `image_url`; if a contact sheet still collapses into near-identical centered canyons, regenerate only those slots with the original in `reference_image_urls` as architecture/palette/atmosphere-only and put the new camera geometry in text. Changing the reference role reduces composition copying while preserving the original visual world. For a strict direct-overhead map derived from street-level skyscraper references, assign those references to materials, rooftop motifs, and palette only, then require roof planes and one contiguous open central ground polygon before batching; dramatic tower facades and a split or narrow arena can survive repeated '90-degree' wording, so inspect the projected geometry and central traversable area on a calibration frame. When a source portrait is reassigned to an overhead rooftop LED, put the readable display on a shallow upward-facing panel with visible roof mounts and local pixel glow, not a vertical facade or floating face. If the user specifies an opaque theatrical mask rather than a human face, require a flat porcelain mask silhouette with dark apertures and no visible skin or photographic facial planes; inspect the screen at native resolution before batching because a white-tinted portrait can pass thumbnail review while violating the mask. If only the display content fails after the map passes, replace it inside the screen polygon and verify unchanged pixels outside rather than regenerating the whole arena. On a request for more maps, compare against the previous contact sheet and reserve different arena footprints and perimeter route graphs, not merely different surface lighting. When a high-angle city reference supplies a specific landmark such as a face projected on a tower, lock the landmark's physical carrier (facade-mounted LED panel, pixel/scanline texture, projected light confined to nearby haze) independently of the new street-level camera; otherwise the face can become a floating portrait or disappear when the angle changes. In each new shot, place visible pavement and a tower base at the lower frame, tilt up toward a distant screen at a distinct image-space position, and preserve the user's dark exposure by keeping the screen glow local rather than lifting the entire fog. Make replacement shots structurally different (open plaza with one dominant tower, diagonal ground-rooted viaduct, underpass aperture, or a close bridge-underside/truss view with the city far below), not just different facades or hashes. For near-black tower canyons, reserve a different dominant foreground topology and camera direction for each slot—wet canal surface, enclosed porthole, bridge deck, rooftop parapet, close structural underside, or downward shaft—because changing the named location alone can still yield the same upward-looking gap between towers. Inspect for unwanted readable signage when opening the view, because generators can fill new facades with ad copy. For the unseen opposite side of a referenced room, treat the image as a continuity anchor for palette, materials, scale, lighting, and architectural details rather than copying its visible wall: place the camera inside the room facing the requested opposite wall, build only plausible new features there, and exclude original-view fixtures that should now be behind the camera. For a reverse view of a room entrance, anchor one fully visible door in the room wall, then preserve the room's lived-in realism rather than reducing it to a bare door and blank wall: add plausible functional furnishings, wall controls and wear. Include a distinct exterior window on a coherent side wall only while the active brief requests it. When the user removes that window, replace its whole opening with continuous opaque wall and remove curtains, exterior light, glazing in the door if 'no window' is unqualified, and any window-shaped luminous panel; retain the furniture and substitute dim interior practicals so the correction does not make the room empty again. Keep the source-view bed and equipment completely behind the camera; explicitly reserve the foreground for continuous floor or entrance-side furnishings and inspect it at full size, because a bed rail can reappear despite a 'no bed' clause. Vary camera height, side, distance and foreground geometry across independent views; inspect for second openings, corridor viewpoints, stray bed rails, and artwork or door glazing that reads as a forbidden window. When every environment variant must show named objects, map each to a distinct foreground/midground/background region and define its recognizable parts and minimum legible frame share as an acceptance gate for each shot slot. For a crowded product-in-environment slate, first make a numbered source inventory and separate product-identity references from the room reference. Reserve the clearest, locally lit foreground/display space for the products the user prioritizes; lock paired colorways, logos, readable lettering, and garment front/back distinctions separately. Inspect every output against the full inventory at native size, including small marks on prioritized products: a plausible room can silently omit, merge, recolor, or invent products, while dark ambience and wide framing can make an otherwise present logo impossible to judge. If all products cannot be both visible and accurate in one frame, disclose the tradeoff rather than certifying inventory fidelity. For exact-count atmospheric props such as two burning barrels behind falling snow, require separately visible physical containers, rims, and flames at the requested depth; count the containers at final resolution rather than counting glows or reflections, because distant fire, haze, and snow can merge two props or make one flame look like several. For a fixed-capacity installation, specify the exact number of independent stations, each station's recognizable human-scale hardware and vacant or occupied state, then scan the entire background at final size for another human-shaped rig or figure; a distant repeated fixture can silently violate the count even when the foreground pair is correct. For a requested blend of two suit references, extract each source's construction motifs separately, assign which materials and components remain visible on the new unified suit, and vary armor topology and wearer action rather than only camera angle or room; a strong person reference can pull every image toward the same pose and design. When a base character sheet is assigned identity, style, readable markings and multi-view layout while another image supplies only a class archetype (such as heavier infantry), lock those assigned anchors but specify the desired change in outer-silhouette geometry: shoulder span, chest depth, side profile, hip/thigh and shin mass, plus a distinct plate connection graph for each variant. If a named redesign workflow assigns the base only to style and exact lettering, do NOT re-lock its identity, helmet, palette, armor topology or page layout; rebuild the whole costume head to boot and carry over only those assigned roles. For a rescue-robot correction to "robot only" and photoreal, remove earlier load vignettes, debris, vehicles, loose closeups, captions and scenery from the active brief; show only full front/profile/rear views of the same robot with empty integrated hands on a seamless studio backdrop. Specify PBR painted steel, hydraulic seals, wear and grounded studio lighting rather than concept-art wording; a worksite reference otherwise leaks cargo or illustration. Inspect one correction before batching; reserve different load-bearing chassis topologies instead of surface adjectives when outputs converge on the same biped silhouette. For fabric-led redesigns, set cloth/armor and main/accent color balance in every view before prompting. Make jacket, sleeves and trousers large uninterrupted fabric planes, keep only compact safety plates and one role-defining tool kit, and put inherited letters on the corresponding new cloth patch. Exclude duplicate pouches, packs, cables and decorative micro-panels: merely saying 'more cloth' leaves the plated base intact. On a simpler-design correction, calibrate one image at native size against the cluttered predecessor before batching. Exclude the archetype's face, unauthorized palette, weapon, logos and literal panel shapes. For two hand-held defensive props and a shoulder-mounted module, assign one prop and visible rear-grip contact to each hand, fix the module to one character-side shoulder with a load-bearing mount, then count the same parts in front, profile, rear and close-ups; side-view overlap must not silently erase one prop or duplicate the module. Check source lettering in its original panel location AND every newly designed prop surface; copied chest text on a shield is an invented mark, not preservation. For a correction that the redesign looks too close to the base, compare the rejected batch with the original at thumbnail size before prompting; if an image-edit base keeps reproducing the slim original with extra seams, move the original to design-only references, reserve visibly broader silhouettes and calibrate one fresh redraw before batching. Keep front/side/rear armor topology coherent and verify any exact source lettering separately; a stronger silhouette must not silently replace identity or markings. Keep warm reference lighting local so it does not overturn a requested cool, dim grade. For a secondary object in the background, reserve enough size and light for its specified geometry to read; if an action is required, show physical evidence such as an open panel, worker touching the mechanism, tool, or worklamp rather than relying on a static prop. If the user instead specifies a parked, untouched object in an unoccupied scene, remove every inherited worker/repair cue from the prompt, place the intact object on its own supports, and QA for silhouettes and apparent work-in-progress; cluttered tools and a service-bay reference can otherwise recreate the rejected action. Keep all anchors separated from shadow and clutter even in wide, doorway, or overhead angles; a batch can preserve the room while losing the chair, the car, or a distinctive wheel angle at thumbnail scale. Inspect every numbered contact-sheet panel for each anchor, action evidence, and meaningful camera variation; check ambiguous geometry at final resolution and regenerate failed slots from the original references. For a referenced vehicle on an elevated landing deck, lock its defining silhouette and repeated components separately from the architecture, require visible landing-gear contact plus a parapet or vertical drop, and QA those connections at final size; otherwise a recognizable vehicle can appear airborne or the platform can read as a ground hangar. When several architecture references are blended, give each a bounded contribution (overhang, ribbed mass, skyline, curved deck) and explicitly exclude unrelated figures or cars from material-only references; a model may import their narrative content along with the wanted surface. First inspect the scale reference and estimate the target object's visible bounding-box width as a percentage of the full frame. Distinguish the physical object, its base/platform, its projected content, and the portion actually visible after intentional edge cropping; a reference can make an object feel large by placing it close to camera and cropping it at the bottom/right rather than by shrinking the room. Then specify percentage, frame region, depth plane, intentional crop edges, required negative space, and forbidden overlaps or height lines. Convert metaphorical scale language into both a frame percentage and an output-pixel target: for “rice-grain-sized” figures, lock the exact figure count, side-by-side separation, depth plane, and approximate pixel height, then inspect every final-size image because a model may preserve the count while enlarging the figures. Convert Chinese crop labels into an explicit lower-frame landmark before generation and QA: `腰部以上` ends at the waist; `胯部以上` includes the complete pelvis and, when the user requests it, a short section of upper thighs, but must not drift to the knees; `大腿以上` ends at the requested upper- or mid-thigh level and must not include knees, calves, or shoes. If a user corrects a waist-only crop to hip-up with some thigh, update the landmark first and inspect it on every final-size image rather than repeating the previous waist crop. If the generator overshoots but the face, hands, and prop already pass, use a top-anchored deterministic crop only when it preserves every required grip and continuous prop path; then inspect the final-size file rather than certifying the uncropped source. For a strict hip-up or knee-up frame, reject a calibration image with the wrong anatomical endpoint before producing the remaining variants: repeating the same prompt only multiplies the failed crop. When a stance subject shows unwanted calves, first crop to the requested knee landmark; uniformly scale that crop to the target height and center-crop the width only if hair, both fists/hands, and all critical equipment remain intact. Inspect the resulting final-size bottom edge for crisp thigh contours; darkness or vignette does not count as an anatomical crop. A chain exiting the bottom and a blade permitted to leave the outer edge do not relax the anatomical crop; recompose the weapon around the torso rather than shrinking the person to fit the whole axe. Translate camera-angle requests into visible geometry: place the camera above or below eye level, state its tilt angle, specify chin and gaze relationship, and forbid eye-level substitution, fisheye distortion, or exaggerated hand/prop perspective. When a close-up reference shows only a tower's high structure but the request is a ground-shot vista, transfer its architecture without inheriting the crop or cloud-height camera: anchor a visible ground-connected base, put the distinctive crossarm at a measured fraction of tower height well above the skyline, and center the tower's axis if requested. When the user instead wants the *whole base-to-tip silhouette visible through cloud*, frame the entire tower as a ground-level extreme long shot (approximately 70-75% of frame height, with sky above the tip), put translucent foreground cloud across both the upper shaft/tip and the high-attached lateral deck, and retain faint but traceable tip and deck outlines; do not replace this with a sharply clear summit above clouds, an opaque cloud that erases the summit, or an edge crop. Keep the deck high above the skyline and visibly connected to the shaft even where a cloud softens its edges. When the user shortens a lateral deck, constrain its *total outer-tip-to-outer-tip width* as a fraction of the frame (for a restrained wing, roughly 18-22%) and check both left and right extensions separately; otherwise the model can preserve the high attachment but render a city-spanning bridge or lose one wing in cloud. When an auxiliary reference controls only the summit, describe its distinctive terminal geometry as part of the single main shaft (for example, adjacent unequal-height chamfered fins), and reject an inherited second tower or unrelated background; reference-role labels alone do not prevent whole-scene leakage. Inspect one calibration frame before the batch; a tower line merely cut by the frame or clouds only behind the architecture are not foreground cloud occlusion. At final-size QA, trace the tip through translucent cloud and both shortened deck arms into the central shaft before accepting each view. For an environment requested *on a high-rise roof*, anchor the camera on a visible near roof deck with parapet and service equipment, then require many other rooftops at staggered depths behind it; a skyline alone does not establish the rooftop location or the city's roof density. If the source is night but the requested shot is golden hour, transfer its architecture without its lighting: assign a low directional warm sun to selected exposed roof planes, keep overhangs and inter-building gaps cool and dark, and reject both inherited neon-night exposure and a uniform orange wash. A quarter-turn can make a centered person look offset, so inspect the subject axis separately from asymmetric effects in the contact sheet. When the user says the object looks too small *relative to the room*, do not merely enlarge the object: compare whether the reference uses tighter FOV, fewer lateral bays, closer placement, or foreground cropping, and change only the mechanisms actually present in the reference. During QA, estimate both the visible object percentage and the room's apparent width. If an output overshoots unintentionally or clips on the wrong edge, edit the accepted composition proportionally, shift it inward where needed, and remeasure before delivery.
10. **Express constrained anatomy and silhouette pieces as positive geometry, then run visual QA.** State where each limb ends and what it does rather than relying on negatives such as “not both hands in pockets.” For example: “right hand inside the right trouser pocket; left elbow above the shoulder; left hand touching the hair.” For count-sensitive hand poses, enumerate all five fingers and their states (for a finger gun: index extended, thumb raised, middle/ring/little curled), then inspect the rendered hand at high resolution; foreshortening can make a malformed hand look plausible at thumbnail size. For a hand near the face, compare middle-finger length with palm length and total hand size with the face at final resolution; a correct five-finger count can still produce implausible claw-length digits. For a forward-reaching claw paired with a lowered free hand, place the claw wrist above and beyond the support plane with curled fingertips separated from the ground, and place the other hand below beside the body without ground contact; inspect fingertip shadows and contact points at full size, because a low crouch often turns both requested gestures into planted support hands. Distinguish a raised, foot-planted bent knee from the opposite knee touching the ground, and trace both legs from hip to their separate contact points before accepting. For any single tail, trace one root through every bend to a complete tip with safe margins. If it replaces legs, require an open S/J longer than the torso; reject tight coils, branches, and visible or skirt-hidden legs. A cropped arc can conceal a missing tip. For hand-to-device contact, name the touching fingertips, palm-to-face spacing, thumb placement, and device edge being touched, then forbid nearby fallback gestures such as covering the ear, holding a phone, saluting, or resting the whole palm on the head. For two-hand poses, specify character-side laterality, image-space location, palm orientation, overlap order, wrist continuity, and whether each hand must be fully inside frame. For fighting poses, distinguish an active strike from a ready guard with positive geometry: a strike has one shoulder driving forward, an extending elbow, a fist path, and deliberate foreshortening; a guard keeps both elbows visibly bent, both fists close to the torso or jaw, fist faces angled away from the lens, and no arm on the camera axis. When the user retracts a punch request, remove every stale extension, lens-aim, centered-fist, and foreshortening clause rather than adding only “do not punch.” Interpret an unqualified request for “both hands holding a gun” as two hands on one firearm, not dual-wielding: lock one firearm, one muzzle, each hand's gripping role, the gun's image-space path, and muzzle direction, then inspect a tight hand-and-weapon crop to trace both wrists into the same weapon. For two armed characters in one scene, front-load the complete totals—two people, four human arms, four hands, two separate guns, and two muzzles—then assign both hands, one gun, one muzzle direction, expression, gaze, and image-space side to each character. Require visible contact geometry for relationships such as back-to-back while keeping both faces and weapons unobscured; inspect the whole image first, then tight mouth and hand/weapon crops to catch identity leakage, fused props, hidden support hands, or a muzzle aimed in the wrong direction. For hands stacked on a centered staff or scepter, lock exactly two hands, ten fingers, one vertical prop, which hand is above, where the prop top sits relative to the hands, and the shaft's path to the lower frame; this prevents duplicate props, fused gloves, and ambiguous grip geometry. For a two-ended chained weapon, lock the totals and topology separately—two handles, two blades, two tips, one continuous chain, two arms, and two hands—then assign each hand independently as gripping or empty. When only one hand holds one end, place the empty hand at its specified body location and keep it visibly clear of the chain and free weapon end; suspend the second end from the chain in reserved lateral negative space. Never carry a prior two-hand grip into a revised one-hand brief. When the user requires a normal forward grip, lock the blade to the thumb/index end of the handle, the chain to the pommel below the little finger, and the initial chain path downward along the handle axis; reject reverse/ice-pick grips, a chain leaving the guard or blade end, parallel duplicate chains, and chains crossing the torso when a vertical drop is requested. When the chain must route behind the body, specify its visible entry point at the foreground silhouette and exit point near the rear handle; require links to reach both occlusion boundaries so the hidden middle reads as one continuous weighted chain rather than two dangling fragments. Trace held handle→chain start→visible sag→body-entry occlusion→body-exit occlusion→free or rear-held handle end-to-end in tight crops, and reject gaps, branches, duplicate chains, fused grips, body intersections, an unintended hand touching the free end, or an old blade tip left outside the cleanup region. Lock shot size before arranging the weapon: in a hip-up portrait, route the chain and free blade beside the torso/hip rather than widening to the thighs or shrinking the character to fit the full prop. For a long straight-bladed weapon with two hands on one handle, map the image-space order before generation: raised/rear hand on the handle's tailward section, near/front hand just behind the guard, then a single guard and one continuous blade pointing away from the hands. Lock the handle centerline through both grips, guard center, blade centerline, and tip to one straight axis; trace the visible segments on both sides of each occluding hand at final size and reject gaps, kinks, duplicated guards, shortened blades, or a hand gripping the blade. When the user permits only the pommel or blade tip to leave frame, crop at that terminal end rather than through a gripping hand; mathematical aspect-ratio compliance alone cannot validate the grip or continuous weapon. For a long-handled hammer or similar T-headed weapon, lock one continuous shaft from the head’s central core through every gripping hand to the tail, plus one transverse head axis perpendicular to the shaft. Reject a floating head, a short disconnected stub below the head, a second shaft, a hand-held shaft misaligned with the head, or an approximate diagonal junction; when exact image-space geometry is requested, verify both head ends share the same pixel height and the shaft is vertical. For a gripped scythe, specify one straight shaft axis and trace blade/head connector → each grip in order → butt, including required collars, root blades and spike. For an exact mechanical-appendage count, front-load the total count, enumerate each path by origin, frame region, and endpoint, and explicitly forbid extra limbs, support-like arcs, disconnected fragments, and partial tips; a multi-limbed identity board otherwise encourages the model to restore the full rig. If anatomy passes but another property fails, use the accepted output as an iterative edit base and explicitly lock the successful hand, face, wink/gaze, and pose while correcting only the failed property. Inspect identity, expression intensity, product geometry and marks, background, pose direction, shoulder position, neck extension, spinal alignment, every visible hand, full-height subject sharpness, framing, motion direction, lighting, crop, unwanted text, and collage artifacts. For age-regressed portraits, lock inherited identity cues separately from the requested younger facial geometry: retain eye color, brow/eye spacing, freckles, and hair while specifying early-adolescent cheek fullness, shorter softer jaw, narrower shoulders, modest age-appropriate clothing, and a child-height camera. QA apparent age at final size rather than trusting the age label; smoothing an adult face often still reads as an adult. When removing scars or jewelry, inspect the exact scar site plus both ears and the neckline in useful crops, because a faint mark or small stud can survive a global negative prompt. For requested facial geometry such as wider-set eyes or smaller pupils, state observable proportions and keep them biologically plausible, then inspect a frontal eye crop at final size; a prompt may preserve ordinary spacing while producing different hashes or a stronger expression. For a single ponytail request, specify the gathered hair's attachment and visible path over or behind one shoulder, explicitly exclude twin pigtails, and inspect its continuity at full size; a front-facing crop can hide whether the hair was actually tied. Describe a generatively recreated source photo as a likeness rather than claiming pixel-exact reuse. For multi-character panel compositions, explicitly count panels and subjects, verify left-to-right identity order, equal panel geometry, aligned edges, reserved empty bands as a measured frame percentage, frame color, and role-specific motifs. Build an internal labeled contact sheet for batch comparison, then inspect small critical regions—faces, hands, props, or the final panel—with targeted crops before accepting. Regenerate only failed outputs from the original references.
