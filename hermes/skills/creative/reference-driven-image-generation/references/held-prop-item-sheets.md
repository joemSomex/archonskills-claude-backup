# Held-prop item sheets from character artwork

Use this recipe for standalone weapon or equipment design sheets, not pixel-identical extraction from a character scene.

1. Inspect the whole uploaded source and identify the object by the user's anatomical hand designation, checking image-side mirroring before cropping. Crop the actual prop with enough context to retain the complete shaft, grip, emitter and tip while excluding adjacent hands, armor and effects as much as possible. Give the source and crop distinct roles: the former resolves which prop, the latter clarifies its shape. If geometry is occluded, describe the reconstructed portion rather than claiming a literal extraction.
2. Inventory the reference prop's silhouette and materials: shaft length, grip rings, emitter housing, blade or photon configuration, metal color and glow. When compositing a front from one reference with handle positions from another, name the orientation and transfer *only* the assigned geometry from each: count the handles, locate each mounting point relative to the receiver, and specify stem shape and grip projection direction. Explicitly exclude the handle reference's unrelated barrel and the front reference's superseded handle; otherwise the model may keep the old U-bridge despite moving it. Inspect one result for handle topology; if the front and body are accepted but a grip is wrong, refine just the grips while locking the accepted geometry and palette. For item sheets, request a single white-background sheet per generation with one complete full-length hero view, supporting orthographic or detail views, and no character, shield or scene. Specify internal consistency across views and margins around both ends. Treat each sheet as a separate deliverable, even though it contains several views.
3. Assign a different *macro* construction to each requested design while retaining identifying family features. Distinguish the emitter/head, shaft section and guard/profile; surface etching, angle changes and glow alone do not establish unique variants. Generate independently from the same source and crop rather than iterating from a previous sheet. If a generation is safety-filtered, retry once with concise fictional-design language without dropping the visual gates.
4. Inspect each native-size sheet for the entire tip, butt, connected shaft, coherent grip/head across views, white background and absence of character parts. Compare all candidates in a numbered contact sheet at thumbnail size, rerender near-duplicates, and save only accepted separate PNGs. Check actual decoded PNG formats, dimensions, file count and unique hashes programmatically; aspect-ratio requests may not match returned files. A hash check proves distinct bytes, not distinct designs.
5. Deliver individual PNG attachments and state when they are reference-based reconstructions rather than literal source-pixel cutouts. Do not present the contact sheet as a requested standalone variant.
