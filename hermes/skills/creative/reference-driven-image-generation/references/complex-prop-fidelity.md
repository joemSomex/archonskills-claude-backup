# Complex Prop Fidelity

Use this procedure when an image contains a mechanically specific fan, hammer, bow, firearm, staff, or other prop whose identity depends on geometry and connections.

## Inspect Before Prompting

Open the prop reference at useful resolution and write a compact morphology spec:

- overall silhouette and open/closed angle;
- exact or approximate repeated-part count;
- shape, overlap, and direction of repeated parts;
- material hierarchy and surface patterns;
- intrinsic light color and where it runs;
- connection graph from the hand contact point through handles, pivots, sockets, shafts, heads, strings, or limbs;
- edge treatment and termination points;
- likely false substitutes, such as a segmented fan becoming a wing or blade array, or a hammer head floating above its shaft.

Describe what is visibly present. Do not infer a continuous membrane when the reference uses overlapping rigid panels, or separate blades when it uses a filled fan leaf.

## Prompt Positive Structure and Forbidden Lookalikes

State the must-have structure first, in load-path order, then add a short forbidden-lookalike list. For a segmented mechanical fan, specify panel count, overlap, tip curvature, rib shape, pivot, handle, light strip, and surface pattern; forbid paper-fan, wing, peacock-tail, shield, and disconnected-blade substitutions. For a hammer, trace head -> neck/socket -> shaft -> grips -> pommel and forbid gaps or axis jumps.

State the exact visible prop count and which hand owns each prop. Require every grip to contact the intended handle rather than an adjacent decorative part. When the source depicts a matched pair as one item, specify two separate mirrored objects in every turnaround view, not just the hero view. Count the objects again in the side and rear panels at full size; a sheet can show a correct pair in front but silently double it in back. Exclude a panel with the wrong count from delivery rather than letting a distinct hash or valid file format make it seem accepted.

For a requested new size or weapon class derived from a reference (for example, a short blade redesigned as a long sword), separate the design fingerprint from the geometry being changed before prompting. Carry forward specific materials, wrap pattern, collar/guard architecture, emitter placement, and pommel treatment; positively specify the new blade-to-grip proportion, curvature, edge, terminal tip, and continuous blade-to-collar-to-handle connection. For a long blade, reserve enough canvas to show tip and pommel with margins and inspect both after aspect-ratio normalization. Reject a stretched short blade, an unchanged dagger, multiple prongs, or a generic replacement sword: copying the original silhouette wholesale defeats the requested transformation, while changing every detail loses its lineage. Vary camera, placement, and bounded design details across independent generations without sacrificing those shared identifiers. When converting a long energy prop to a clustered rotary silhouette, lock its recognizable rear housing, power modules, optic, cable/clamp rhythm, palette, and two distinct handhold regions; assign the requested change to the forward cluster, rings, and protective shroud only. Distinguish variations by visible cluster topology or shroud silhouette, not file hashes or lighting alone, and compare all views for the same connection graph. If an image request is safety-rejected, stop that rejected target and report the blocker rather than retrying the same content through cosmetic rewording or omitted details. Offer a substantively different composition for the user to approve when appropriate, such as replacing an aiming pose with a lowered ready stance. After approval, propagate the changed pose into every prompt: keep the prop below the shoulder and point it toward empty ground; use any earlier aiming photo only for still-compatible lower-body stance, not the rejected upper-body pose. Retain independent identity, environment and prop-design references. Do not promise that the alternative will be accepted. For a partly completed batch, preserve the completed outputs and report the exact shortfall.

## Rigid Firearms and Annotated Axis Corrections

For a directional card of a long or blade-like firearm, first isolate one complete ORIGINAL design-board view and identify the front and rear by visible hardware, not by the apparent pointed silhouette alone: muzzle shape and vented shroud at one end, trigger loop, hollow grip and butt at the other. Place the muzzle and butt at separate image-space coordinates, then trace the continuous rear -> receiver -> shroud -> single muzzle path on one calibration image before making the batch. If the user locks the muzzle to a corner, vary camera thickness, lighting and setting without reversing that path; pull the whole camera back to fit both terminals above the reserved lower-third zone rather than truncating the shroud. Check each native-size output for a single un-forked tip, intact trigger/grip topology, straight shaft, contact shadow and no bright scenery in the text zone. Start from the original item and environment references; use a uniformly rotated crop of an ORIGINAL view only as a pose-only guide if text direction repeatedly fails, because an extra design-board crop can carry studio backing and glossy product rendering into every card. Inspect and reject any guide backing, cutout halo or copied typography in the generated result.

When a user marks a firearm with a rejected path and a desired straight path, find the exact unmarked base first (compare likely prior exports after uniform resizing, then inspect face, armor, and prop details at native scale). Use the marked image only as a geometry guide; never import its colored strokes into the deliverable.

1. Read the annotation as two distinct paths: the rejected route and the desired centerline. Measure three image-space landmarks on the desired line—rear receiver/barrel root, intermediate barrel ring, and muzzle-center—and express them as frame percentages. The muzzle target may move away from an earlier centered, lens-facing composition; the latest marked direction overrides it.
2. Rebuild the whole rigid load path, not only the front ring: receiver -> barrel root -> parallel barrels through each clamp -> muzzle face. Keep the three centers collinear and every long tube straight and parallel in perspective. A frontal muzzle can hide a receiver-to-barrel kink, so inspect the side contours and clamp centers as well as the circular holes. Reposition hand/forearm contacts around the new receiver rather than bending the gun to preserve an old grip.
3. Generate one calibration image and trace the axis at full size before creating the requested number of independent variants. Compare the candidate's receiver, midpoint, and muzzle with the desired guide, then inspect muzzle edge clearance, hand contact, reference-specific hardware, and absence of annotation colors. Do not certify a gun as straight merely because its front barrel segment is straight; reject a diagonal-to-vertical turn at the receiver or barrel junction.
4. If the edit must preserve unrelated pixels exactly, use a local prop/contact composite from the untouched base and verify protected regions. Otherwise describe a full-frame image-to-image correction as a new rendition, not pixel-exact weapon-only repair.

## Protect Color Semantics

Separate three layers:

1. base materials;
2. intrinsic emitters built into clothing or props;
3. environmental key, rim, and background light.

Ambient orange or red may create limited edge reflection, but it must not recolor black/gray armor or turn reference-white/reference-pink emitters into the ambient color.

## Compose for Full Extent

Reserve negative space around the prop before generation. Put exact constraints on high/low, left/right, and face-clear zones. When a pose reference obstructs the face, preserve the arm path but minimally translate or rotate the prop outside the facial silhouette. Define a measurable face-clear corridor: keep the full forehead, both eyes, nose, mouth, chin, cheeks, and jaw visible, place the nearest weapon/hand/forearm edge outside that silhouette, and require a visible background gap. A weapon that merely avoids the eyes but crosses the cheek, mouth, chin, or hairline still fails an unobscured-face request.

Decide whether the brief requires the full prop visible or intentional edge exit. For full visibility, require a safe margin around long shafts, hammer heads, fan tips, bow limbs, and other extremities; if exact-ratio normalization would clip them, regenerate with a smaller subject and explicit margins rather than stretching or accepting cropped geometry. For a long rigid prop in a card with reserved lower text space, measure the source's head-to-shaft length, head width, and terminal size before generation; specify a smaller whole-prop bounding box above the text zone, not a shorter handle. If an upright composition keeps entering the text zone, rotate a crop of an ORIGINAL view uniformly as a pose-only guide while retaining the whole original as the design authority; this can move the long axis across the upper card without changing proportions. When repeated text constraints still shorten the shaft or fill the blank zone, build a temporary composition guide before further generation: isolate one complete canonical source view to RGBA, inspect its alpha mask for missing tips or included page-background wedges, and place its full bounding box above the reserved lower third. If the entire measured alpha box fits, translate the cutout at 1:1; otherwise scale the *whole* item uniformly once and record the factor, never just shorten its shaft or blade. On a pale design board, a grayscale threshold and largest connected component can isolate dark metal, but the ground shadow may touch the pommel and survive that filter: constrain the terminal alpha with a tapered spatial mask, then inspect against a saturated dark test plate before generating or compositing the batch. On a painted board with dark brush marks behind a curved blade, seed segmentation from the item's dark metal and intrinsic-color accents inside a spatial silhouette corridor; inspect both terminal tips, outer colored edge, negative-space holes, and grip against a contrasting matte. Reject a broad color-threshold cleanup that punches holes in similar-toned silver or severs the arc; trim only verified exterior page texture and recheck the measured tip-to-tip alpha extent before compositing. Supply this guide as a composition-only image base while also supplying both ORIGINAL references with separate design/environment roles; generate each variant independently from its own guide, never from another generated card. The guide is a positional scaffold, not proof of a finished painted card: inspect whether generation redrew the prop's actual surfaces and preserved the main blade, counterblade, connector, full shaft and butt tip. If it merely copies the source CG cutout or still violates structure, mark that style or topology gate unmet; for a literal no-deformation request, prefer a verified uniform-scale composite and disclose the source-pixel preservation instead of claiming repainting. Compare the generated component ratios and straight load path at native size against the original: avoiding nonuniform postprocessing does not prove the generator preserved geometry. For an intentionally oversized prop, preserve its source proportions and let only the named end or ends cross the named canvas edge; never shorten, thicken, flatten, or compress the prop merely to fit it inside a portrait frame. After normalization, verify that the central defining structure remains readable and that each cropped path reaches the frame boundary continuously rather than disappearing inside the image.

## Flexible and Segmented Weapons

For a whip, chain, cable, or segmented flexible weapon, lock one continuous topology before describing its curve: handle -> proximal connector -> repeated segments or links -> taper -> terminal tip. Specify the repeated segment shape, joint color, taper direction, and exactly where the terminal path leaves or ends. Reserve negative space for the requested large arc; a small loop near the wrist is not equivalent to a broad outward curve.

Assign each hand independently. If only one hand holds the handle, keep the other hand visibly empty at its requested body location and clear of the flexible path. If a second hand guides the body, name the safe contact segment and forbid contact with blades or joints. Trace the entire visible path through every hand and occlusion to the exit edge; reject branches, duplicate handles, a second weapon, floating segments, unexplained gaps, or a tail that closes inside the frame when it was required to leave it.

## Bow and Arrow Contact Geometry

Inspect a close crop of the central riser before deciding whether an arrow exists: a short horizontal arrow rest or guide projecting from the hub is not a complete projectile. For a bow-only reference, preserve the upper and lower hooked tips, one taut continuous string attached at both ends, central hub/grip, and short rest; do not invent a full arrow merely because the weapon is called a bow and arrow. If a complete arrow is visible or explicitly requested, treat the drawn assembly as a load-bearing connection graph: upper limb -> upper tip -> continuous string -> nock -> continuous shaft -> arrow rest/guard -> single arrowhead, plus lower string -> lower tip -> lower limb. When the arrow or arrowhead must sit on the bow-body guard, place the shaft through the bow window and require the arrowhead or front shaft underside to make visible physical contact with the top groove of the rest above the grip. Require a contact shadow and an unbroken common axis; reject an arrowhead floating below the rest, dropping toward the lower frame, sitting outside the bow window, bending away from the shaft, or piercing the riser. Inspect this small contact region at full resolution, because a plausible overall aiming pose can hide a detached or unsupported arrowhead.

### Released shots and photon streaks

For a released solid-light arrow, keep tail, shaft, arrowhead symmetry axis, apex and trail core on one shared launch line through the original arrow rest; a straight shaft alone does not prevent a sideways arrowhead. Treat colored trajectory annotations as geometry guides and remove them from the output. Keep the detached projectile ahead of the rest with a visible separation, and distinguish its physical shaft from residual light extending backward. Let the latest request decide whether pulse rings exist; do not retain or reintroduce them after explicit removal. These are prompt constraints, not proof of successful geometry without authorized visual review.

Separate the shot state before prompting: a released shot requires an open drawing-hand follow-through and a forward-returned string, not an arrow still pinched at the cheek. Keep the shooting string attached to the original limb tips; distinguish original compound-bow control cables from invented duplicate shooting strings. Render requested vibration as one continuous translucent motion-blur band rather than multiple crisp parallel lines, because discrete afterimages can read as extra physical strings. Prohibit stray string segments across the face or hair. When the user specifies the string in front of the bow-holding forearm, state the camera-near occlusion explicitly and inspect that crossing during an authorized correction audit; a plausible overall bow pose can still hide a string behind the arm.

Anchor photon emission to the actual arrow rest immediately above the gripping hand, not the lower limb. If the user intentionally wants the visible trail to begin at the chest, extend one straight trail backward from that same rest toward the chest rather than lowering the firing point. For a pure light-streak request, remove the entire geometric arrowhead, fins and solid shaft, not just their color; retain only the requested glow, pulse rings and particles. Treat crescent shockwaves as optional effect components, not mandatory features of a photon shot.

For an isolated effect asset, assign the scene reference to VFX appearance only and exclude character, bow, environment and source annotations. Use the requested white background rather than silently substituting transparency. For a local crescent reversal, specify both opening direction and convex-spine/horn positions while locking its bounding area and beam trajectory; mirroring the entire image reverses unrelated rings and the shot direction. For crescent removal, distinguish the large shockwave from small elliptical pulse rings so deleting one does not erase the other.

## Preserve Accepted Contact While Resizing One Part

When the user accepts a support or grip relationship but asks to resize one component, lock the accepted axis, height, contact point, support surface, and connected hardware before changing dimensions. For an arrowhead-size correction, scale only the arrowhead length and width, keep its rear socket coaxial with the same shaft, and keep its underside on the same arrow-rest groove with the same contact shadow. Reject any resize that lifts the arrowhead off the rest, shifts it below the rest, changes the shaft axis, enlarges the rest, or redraws unrelated bow, hand, face, or framing details.

## Long Railguns and Electromagnetic Rifles

Assign the weapon by the user's explicit image number, even when another reference depicts a competing gun; pose-only references supply body mechanics, never prop design. Treat a reference-specific railgun as a complete ordered architecture rather than a generic long gun: muzzle frame -> continuous exposed energy rail/coil -> repeated bridge clamps -> vented outer shroud -> receiver/energy chamber -> top optic -> trigger grip -> rear stock, with a distinct underside support grip. For paired blue rails, verify two continuous parallel emitters and their bridge contacts at native size; a single blue streak or conventional barrel is a substitution, not fidelity. Preserve the source length-to-height ratio; in a portrait frame, let the muzzle cross the named side edge rather than shortening the barrel into a conventional rifle. Assign the firing hand to the rear pistol grip and the support hand to the underside foregrip or handguard; reject hands gripping the energy rail, optic, muzzle, or decorative braces. Verify the intrinsic rail color separately from warm environmental reflection so orange ambience does not replace a reference-yellow emitter or recolor black/gray armor. When the requested finish is matte black with sparse colored indicators, assign a few separate recessed light locations on the receiver and rail clamps, and require each to remain small but discernible at final size. Check the standalone images, not just thumbnails: near-black surfaces can swallow tiny accents even when the prompt names them. Regenerate failed variants from the original reference with explicit indicator positions instead of brightening the entire rail, flooding the environment with that color, or abandoning the matte finish.

## Verify Standalone, Not Only as Thumbnails

Honor the review mode in SKILL.md: for this user's direct-delivery batches, do not introduce unsolicited contact sheets or image-by-image inspections. When visual review is requested, use a contact sheet to compare variants, then inspect each relevant standalone image at useful resolution. Trace:

- total prop count;
- repeated-part count and rhythm;
- silhouette against the source;
- surface pattern and light placement;
- handle/pivot/socket/shaft continuity;
- each hand-to-prop contact;
- face occlusion;
- edge clipping;
- extra pivots, handles, heads, strings, or floating pieces.

Reject and regenerate only the failing output from the original references. Do not certify strict reference fidelity when the defining geometry is merely similar.
