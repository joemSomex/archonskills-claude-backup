# Reference-matched pixel-art emoji production

Use for a character-sheet identity plus one or more pixel-art emoji style references, especially when the output must be a batch of independent transparent PNGs at an exact size.

## 1. Read the originals, not their preview matte

Inspect each original at native size. Assign the character sheet to identity/outfit only and the emoji images to rendering/framing only; enumerate distinctive hair, face, garment, and hardware anchors separately. When a new original character sheet arrives, reset the identity and palette instead of carrying the prior emoji character forward. For scarred faces, zoom into the original and inventory each separate healed mark by orientation, image-space side, start/end landmarks, color, and length. State those as positive geometry in every generation prompt (for example, an under-eye line crossing the nose AND a descending cheek line); keep hair and hands off the marks, and choose near-frontal angles when a three-quarter turn would conceal one. Check each final 480-pixel face crop for the correct count and placement: one plausible slash is not a substitute for two distinct scars, while an invented V-shaped branch or blood/tattoo effect also changes identity. Preserve a short diagonal branch only when the original close-up actually shows it; do not infer branches from intersecting hair or shadows. Distinguish an under-eye scar crossing the nose from eyewear: require a skin-embedded line without lens rims, bridge hardware or temple arms when the identity has no glasses. Treat face-only framing as active only when the current brief requests it; for an unconstrained crying emoji, a large-head waist-up chibi can preserve gloves, cloak, and outfit while keeping tears legible. Check each style image with Pillow: `Image.open(path).mode`, `size`, `getchannel('A').getextrema()` when RGBA, and a corner pixel. A black viewer backdrop can simply be transparent alpha. Describe observable cluster density, irregular stepped contours, tonal range, highlight rhythm, facial proportions, and edge treatment. Do not infer an exact source grid or small palette merely from the word 'pixel'.

Specify observable geometry rather than merely 'masculine', inspect one corrected sample before revising the remainder, and check every final image again: prompts forbidding lashes do not prove their absence. Keep the requested gesture unless explicitly changed, and preserve the pixel-cluster rendering rather than overcorrecting into smooth realistic portraits.

### Lock a supplied iris HEX without staining surrounding skin

After generation, inspect native-size eyes and define a separate tight ROI for each visible iris in each frame; do not reuse eye coordinates after a new render. Within each ROI, combine hue/saturation/value selection with the verified iris connected component. Exclude dark pupils, pale glints, eyelids, scars and skin explicitly. Treat candidate HSV thresholds as source-specific starting points, not universal eye masks.

For a requested base such as `#ffb831`, map only verified colored iris pixels to `round(V * [255,184,49])`, where `V` is that source pixel's maximum RGB channel divided by 255. This locks base hue/saturation while retaining brightness shading; preserve excluded pupils and highlights literally. Verify zero changed pixels outside the union of iris masks and inspect both eyes in EVERY exported PNG. Do not claim every iris pixel literally equals the HEX when darker shaded gold remains, and do not equate a prompt mentioning the HEX with a verified color lock. Use one mask per iris rather than a global gold selector, which can recolor skin or armor. If GIF delivery follows, check decoded iris crops separately from whole-frame palette error. Reserve a dedicated gold subpalette when ordinary quantization turns amber into skin-brown: a useful split is 191 general colors plus 64 verified iris colors, with transparent index 0 separate and the supplied HEX explicitly included. Map unique opaque RGB values to nearest palette entries 1-255 using signed integer squared distances in bounded NumPy batches, then expand the indices back to pixels; this avoids both unsigned overflow and accidental transparent-index selection. Verify per-iris RGB error, intact pupils/glints, and no dark source contours mapped to bright colors before accepting the GIF.

## 3. Obtain real transparency without damaging the sprite

Prevent cutout artifacts at generation time: submit the user's transparency requirement directly on the FIRST generation attempt without adding a forced white/chroma background, and inspect the actual mode and alpha extrema before any editing. Do not call it the 'same prompt' if the agent added a flat-background instruction: that changes the generation path and may create artifacts absent from a direct ChatGPT run. The available `image_generate` route using provider `openai-codex` and `gpt-image-2-medium` returned opaque RGB PNGs at 1254×1254 for five direct prompts explicitly requesting transparent 480×480 RGBA. Treat this as a tested limitation of that route, not proof ChatGPT's own UI cannot make transparent PNGs. Do not repeat prompt-only transparent requests on that route and claim native alpha; use a confirmed alpha-capable ChatGPT/OpenAI path or disclose the need for conversion. If the user requests raw unedited files, send them honestly labeled as raw and do not assert transparency or target dimensions. If the generator yields opaque RGB, use a single truly uniform flat backdrop with no shadows, checkerboards, fades, or halos; choose a chroma color absent from the subject (such as vivid magenta for dark clothing and warm skin) when white eye/hair highlights would be confused with a white matte. A tested fast fallback for this dark-armored amber-eyed character used `#FF00FF`: at source resolution, classify pixels with `R>145, B>145, G<155, R-G>95, B-G>95` as matte, dilate that binary matte by one source pixel, assign zero alpha there, then resize RGBA to 480×480 with Lanczos. Test one sample on dark and light mattes before the batch, verify each file's corner/matte fraction, and reject non-uniform backgrounds; thresholds are source-specific, not universal. Never call an RGB checkerboard transparent. When extraction is needed, test it on one image before applying the same method to the batch; reject an extraction with opaque matte specks. Keep this gate quick because the user values turnaround speed as well as correctness. For a magenta backdrop with no pink subject accents, use a source-tuned global magenta mask and a small dilation, then inspect wispy hair and cloak edges on both mattes: border-connected masking can strand vivid magenta pockets inside black hair gaps and leave pink streaks even when corner pixels are transparent. Start a dark-haired character with `R>125, B>125, G<165, R-G>80, B-G>80` and a 5x5 dilation at source resolution. If dark magenta strands remain between black hair or cloak edges, widen the color gate to about `R>90, B>90, G<165, R-G>50, B-G>50` and the dilation to 9x9, then inspect the face, tears, hair tips, and costume on both mattes; the broader global mask removes enclosed background pockets that a border-connected mask leaves behind. Protect any legitimate magenta/pink costume marks before using either gate. For pink/red hearts, irises, hair, or armor lights, first choose a chroma hue absent from the character and effects rather than keying against their own color. Conversely, when the armor has green emitters, use a non-green matte so extraction does not erase its signature lights. A flat vivid green `#00FF00` backdrop preserved solid red and pink hearts in a dark-haired emoji batch: after sampling each source corner, a workable starting mask is `G>90, G-R>50, G-B>50` with a 9x9 dilation at source resolution. After premultiplied resizing, clear only demonstrably green fringe adjacent to zero-alpha pixels (7x7 neighborhood, `G>45, G-R>20, G-B>20`), then inspect every detached heart and hair edge on both mattes. If green clothing, leaves, or effects make that key unsafe, use another absent hue or protect those regions; thresholds are source-specific. If a magenta backdrop is already baked in WITH pink subject accents, instead classify near-magenta candidates with a source-tuned red/blue balance check (a workable starting gate is `R>145, B>145, G<155, R-G>95, B-G>95, abs(R-B)<35`), then use 8-connected components and remove only labels touching the canvas border before a one-pixel dilation. A global magenta threshold erases enclosed pink heart eyes and detached hearts; white/pale outlines and component boundaries protect their interiors. Check the original's corner pixels for every slot before batching conversion, since a generator may render one image with a baked checkerboard even when the other slots use magenta; regenerate that slot with an explicitly uniform flat backdrop rather than silently treating its opaque checkerboard as transparent. For a clean flat backdrop, remove border-connected near-white, near-neutral pixels with connected-component labeling rather than a global white threshold: global thresholding erases white eye glints and pale skin. When a rendered checkerboard uses different gray levels across files, sample each file's corner tones and adapt the neutral-background threshold per file; a white-only threshold leaves darker tiles opaque. Preserve isolated gray hair highlights instead of deleting every enclosed neutral component; those highlights can share the matte's hue. For a high-resolution white source, the tested initial discriminator was `min(R,G,B)>190` and `max(R,G,B)-min(R,G,B)<32`; these are starting values to inspect, not universal constants. Use `cv2.connectedComponents(..., connectivity=8)` on the candidate mask, collect labels touching the four borders, and set only those labels to alpha zero. If hair traps white holes, clear verified enclosed near-white matte components in peripheral hair gaps and between the legs while protecting the central face and costume highlights. Border-connected removal alone leaves opaque checkerboard islands trapped inside a connected character silhouette. Sample both checkerboard tones; for dark clothing and warm skin, `min(R,G,B)>175` with channel spread below 35 is a useful candidate gate, not an automatic deletion rule. Inspect candidate components spatially at native size before clearing them, since silver buckles, boot laces and cool hair highlights can share those values. Recheck both light and dark mattes after cleanup; a clean outer silhouette and valid alpha extrema do not prove its interior gaps are transparent. Preserve isolated colored droplets outside the main silhouette. Make RGBA BEFORE resizing, decontaminate fringe RGB against the chosen matte color, and resize with alpha-aware (premultiplied) filtering so hidden backdrop colors cannot bleed into the outline; never substitute a white RGB preview for actual transparency. Automatically flag opaque pixels matching the backdrop color outside protected interior highlight zones, including enclosed hair gaps and detached icon edges, then resolve them before final downscaling. If tiny dark-magenta flecks remain after premultiplied resize, apply final edge-only cleanup at target size: dilate the zero-alpha mask by a 7x7 neighborhood, then clear adjacent nonzero-alpha pixels matching `R>45, B>45, R-G>20, B-G>20, B>R-40`. If native-size inspection still shows purple wisps along dark hair or cloak, widen only that edge test to `R>25, B>20, R-G>8, B-G>8, B>R-65` and rerun the batch; low-value contaminated edge pixels fall below the first gate. Do not apply the wider test to interior pixels: preserve red irises, blush, mouth shading, and detached symbols. Recheck hair-tip continuity, any red X or other colored glyph, both light/dark mattes, and alpha extrema after each change.

### Recover fine details and remove unwanted cool hair streaks

When cutout cleanup removes details, rebuild from the untouched generated source before changing poses: recover hair tips, exposed fingertips, sleeve edges, hardware and detached reaction marks. Start with the source-tuned chroma mask without dilation, then decontaminate verified edge RGB rather than enlarging the removal mask; dilation can shave fine foreground details as well as matte. For green-matte extraction on a subject with no green identity colors, compute channel differences in signed integers and test dark spill too. A source-tuned starting gate is `(G-R>30) & (G-B>30)` without an absolute G floor; a brightness floor can strand dark matte pixels. Neutralize confirmed green excess only in retained pixels immediately adjacent to the removed matte, preserving interior skin, red irises and cloth. After export, assert that no opaque pixels satisfy the residual-spill gate, then inspect the actual final PNG on light and dark mattes; the assertion detects chroma, not missing hair tips or damaged anatomy. Segment the full sheet and assign detached components to their corresponding character using row and centroid distance, because nominal cell crops can sever tips and effect marks. Apply one scale factor across the sequence and reserve margin for the full character-plus-effects extent. Do not claim to have restored a detail already absent from the source; redraw that region instead.

For a requested neutral-black hair correction, inspect saturated dark edge streaks as well as bright highlights: a gate limited to small blue/red differences misses vivid low-value blue fringe. On a region confirmed to contain neutral hair, identify blue-biased opaque pixels with signed-channel arithmetic such as `B>R`, replace their RGB with luminance, and preserve alpha. Use a spatial hair mask whenever legitimate blue identity colors exist. Do not use `B>G` alone as a blue detector: warm red laugh marks can satisfy it and become gray. Protect skin, costume accents, eyes and detached red indicators; compare their colors and the entire alpha mask before and after correction. Inspect native-size edges and dark/light mattes before declaring the streaks removed.

For a local closed-mouth endpoint correction, preserve the accepted frame and edit only the mouth region. Prefer literal transfer of the rendered smile and surrounding skin from an approved neighboring frame when its face angle and placement match; a procedural curve over inpainted skin can look like a pencil mark rather than the established shaded pixel rendering. Register the donor if needed, keep the entire smile in the fully opaque interior of a tight mask, and feather only the outer skin boundary. Exclude the chin and jaw from the transfer even when they fall inside a convenient rectangular crop, because their positions differ between poses. If no matching donor exists, generate an aligned mouth repair candidate and composite only the permitted region. Verify no old mouth opening or outline remains, require byte-identical alpha and zero changed pixels outside the repair mask, and inspect the actual delivered file at native scale after the final adjustment. Keep other frames unchanged unless the user also requests a sequence-wide correction.

For a finger-to-mouth overlap repair, inspect the circled region on the clean original rather than editing the annotated screenshot. Crop the hand and mouth generously with cheek, eye, glove and scarf landmarks; generate a repair candidate that separates rounded fingertips from the mouth behind them without changing the hand's pose or adding digits. Resize the candidate uniformly back to the source crop, register unchanged landmarks using SIFT matches and `cv2.estimateAffinePartial2D(..., method=cv2.RANSAC)`, then transfer only the defective fingertip/mouth union through a tight inward-feathered polygon. Reject mouth-edge fragments, tooth-like finger bumps, extra fingers and doubled contours at native resolution. Preserve the original alpha and every pixel outside the polygon; deliver the corrected standalone PNG for review without silently rebuilding an already accepted GIF.

### Local blink and direct-gaze revisions

For a requested blink in one existing pose and viewer-facing gaze in another, preserve the accepted head angles and edit the eye regions only. Crop with brows, nose scar and surrounding hair as registration anchors; generate a repair donor independently for each target, resize uniformly to the original crop and align unchanged landmarks with SIFT plus RANSAC partial affine estimation. Transfer through separate tight eye masks with feathering confined to their outer skin boundaries. For closed eyes, remove the entire former sclera/iris aperture rather than merely lowering a lid; inspect for stray iris slivers and duplicate lash lines. For direct gaze on a turned head, adjust iris/pupil positions within the existing apertures instead of turning the entire head toward the camera. Reapply the iris color lock only to visible irises after compositing. Require byte-identical alpha and zero differences outside the eye masks; leave unrelated frames untouched and deliver the requested full sequence using the latest approved version of every slot.

## 4. Match the requested pixel density and size

For dense shaded emoji references near the requested final dimensions, isolate at full generated resolution, then resize uniformly to the exact square target with a high-quality resampler such as Pillow `Image.Resampling.LANCZOS`; inspect whether the source's clustered details survive. Even a square generation request can return a near-square raster, so inspect actual width and height and pad the shorter axis with transparent pixels before resizing; independently resizing both axes distorts the character. When cutting a cyan holographic sprite from magenta, a workable initial mask is `R>145, B>145, G<155, R-G>95, B-G>95`, dilated by one source pixel. The apparent solid backdrop may vary slightly in RGB, so sample multiple border locations rather than requiring exact `#FF00FF`. Set removed pixels' RGB to zero and premultiply the remaining RGB by alpha before Lanczos scaling; unpremultiply afterward to avoid pink fringes. For deliberately coarse source sprites, use nearest-neighbor and an integer scale instead. Do not blindly quantize dense examples to a tiny fixed palette or impose a 64-pixel grid: that destroys their layered shading and produces a different style.

## 5. Gate every delivered file

Verify each file opens as PNG/RGBA at the requested exact dimensions and alpha extrema include both 0 and 255. For a batch, verify exact count and distinct SHA-256 digests, then compare a labeled contact sheet for meaningful different emotions and poses. Composite each final RGBA over a dark and a light matte and inspect fringe/white wedges, hair gaps, buckles, eyes, tear droplets, and cut-off silhouettes. Run a separate style pass at the delivered 480-pixel size and a nearest-neighbor zoom of representative face, hair, and garment crops side by side with the style references: require visible intentional square-pixel clusters in the interior shading, not just jagged outer edges. If the interior has continuous brush gradients, wispy smooth hair strands, or uniformly antialiased linework, fail the style gate even when identity, pose, transparency, and file checks pass; correct a sample from the original references before accepting the rest. A reduced contact sheet can disguise smooth anime illustration as pixel art, so do not certify a batch's style from that sheet alone. When style is the user's primary objection, narrow the next deliverable to one style-focused preview instead of generating another motion batch. Judge thin hair-strand clusters, outline thickness, stepped skin shadows, matte versus glossy fabric, saturation, light direction and head-to-torso ratio separately. Once a preview is approved, compare every subsequent native-size frame directly against it and the original style examples: supplying approved art to the generator does not prove that its outputs match. Do not describe a batch as corrected when the defining style mismatch remains, even if timing, alpha and anatomy checks pass. Inspect each image at native resolution for extraction artifacts too: near-white antialiased pixels along hair and detached icon edges can survive a border-connected mask as tiny opaque flecks, and Lanczos resizing can spread them. Remove or decontaminate only those exterior fringe pixels while protecting intentional eye and hair highlights; recheck both mattes after correction. Deliver only the standalone accepted PNGs; a contact sheet is QA, not a replacement attachment.

## 6. Multi-frame looping sprite sheets

### Build from an exact supplied opening frame

When the user designates an existing PNG as Frame 1, retain its original file and pixels rather than regenerating a near-match, resizing it unnecessarily, or substituting a prior approved preview. Inspect that exact image's existing arm and hand positions before planning later poses. If its raised anatomical arm conflicts with the previous action's assigned arm, ask which arm should move; do not silently mirror the image, transfer the gesture, or freeze the intended action. Deliver the unchanged opening frame alongside numbered new pose PNGs when frame review is requested, and wait for approval before GIF assembly. A source image establishes the target, not evidence that unreviewed generated neighbors already conform to it.

### Restyle an existing animation without changing the performance

1. Open the actual attached GIF, extract every decoded RGBA frame in order, and record frame count, individual delays, loop flag and total duration before generation. A replied-to older attachment can differ from the latest output; use the attachment as authority.
2. Assign the source GIF all identity, outfit, eyewear placement, colors, expression, anatomical arm, gaze and pose-path authority. Assign style references rendering only, except explicit overrides for half-body proportions or lower crop. Preserve the source action while adapting that crop; a hoodie reference must not replace armor, and sunglasses above the forehead in a style example must not move source eyewear off the eyes.
3. Supply a numbered source-pose sheet and its individual frames as visual anchors. Restyle every original frame one-to-one; do not generate a new performance, reverse its order, add intermediates, or substitute timing-only copies for separately requested restyled outputs. When several outputs are requested, vary rendering execution within the target style rather than the gesture. Before rendering, define one shared lighting specification: direction, exposure, saturation, shadow depth and highlight strength. For this user's detailed chibi standard, require fine crisp pixel clusters, individually defined hair strands, warm skin midtones, restrained rosy cheeks, dark controlled outlines and stepped shading; keep clothing darker than the bright face and metal highlights small and sharp. Reject broad glossy gradients, bloom, crushed shadows, oversized pixel blocks and painted interiors even when the outer silhouette is stepped. Let shading vary only with changed surface orientation, not independent per-frame lighting or exposure.
4. Keep the same anatomical gesture arm through shoulder, elbow, wrist and fingers; audit the unused arm and any exposed breastplate for obsolete limb pixels. Inspect complete hands, elbows, necks and hair-to-shoulder contours at native resolution before encoding. Redraw incompatible anatomy rather than hiding it under rectangular donor patches.
5. Honor the current delivery gate. For a PNG-only review, export all restyled frames numbered in source order at the original dimensions, record each original frame delay and the total duration in a timing manifest, and state that PNGs do not themselves carry GIF playback delays; do not encode a GIF yet. When GIF assembly is authorized, encode the exact original per-frame delays and loop flag. Reopen each GIF and compare the complete delay vector, count, total duration and transparency to the source; numerical checks establish timing, not pose fidelity. Compare each source/restyled pose side by side and disclose remaining rendering or motion differences rather than claiming exact preservation from matching counts alone.

### Selective hair-color corrections across a sequence

Treat an explicit hair-only correction as a masked color transform, not image regeneration. Reuse existing geometry and alpha, inspect semantic hair masks on every frame, and apply one shared continuous tonal mapping to the entire sequence. Do not normalize exposure or build a separate histogram curve per frame, because independently fitted curves create color flicker. For palette-anchor requests, interpolate through the anchors while retaining tonal order, highlights and strand clusters; for a supplied color reference, sample unambiguous hair regions excluding skin, eyes, eyebrows and armor.

Inspect disconnected hair masses behind raised arms as well as the largest component: a largest-component-only mask can leave long hair tips uncorrected. Conversely, brown shoulder-skin shadows may satisfy the same hue threshold as hair. Validate mask overlays at native resolution before checking pixel differences. Zero changes outside a mask proves edit containment, not that the mask contains only hair. Require identical alpha, canvas and silhouette, unchanged protected skin/eyes/outfit pixels, and visual confirmation that no hair islands remain in the old hue.

### Authorized lower-torso crop without redrawing

When the user explicitly permits cutting off the lower torso to match a shorter half-body reference, prefer a lossless crop over regeneration. Decode the exact attached GIF and record its frame order, delays, loop flag and dimensions. Choose ONE horizontal crop line from the stationary torso after inspecting every moving hand and elbow; apply it identically to every frame, then use at most one shared integer translation to recenter the retained artwork on the original transparent canvas. Do not independently fit, scale or center silhouettes, because moving hands must not change the camera or body size. Compare hair-inclusive head height to the retained jaw-to-crop distance against the current references; cropping changes visible head-to-torso ratio without altering anatomy. Preserve every retained RGB/alpha pixel, and confirm the crop does not sever the action's essential fingertips or contact point.

If the decoded sequence has at most 255 unique opaque RGB colors, build a direct color-to-index lookup with transparency at index 0 rather than re-quantizing; this preserves the accepted art exactly. Reopen the GIF and verify identical delays, frame count and loop flag, exact retained opaque RGB and alpha after the known translation, common torso baseline, and safe bounds. Report the crop as a crop, not a style redraw. If the crop would remove an essential low gesture, obtain permission for a different framing treatment instead of silently cropping it away.

### Timing-only changes

When only delay changes are authorized, prefer changing Graphic Control Extension delay fields in the accepted GIF rather than decoding and re-quantizing its palette. Parse GIF blocks, skip global/local color tables and length-prefixed sub-blocks, and write each frame delay in 10 ms units; do not search-and-replace raw byte patterns that might occur inside compressed image data. Reopen old and new files and require equal frame counts, loop flags and pixel-identical decoded RGBA frames before delivery.

Plan animation continuity separately from static variation diversity. For multiple loop variations, vary gaze direction, blink depth, head-tilt amplitude and the timing implied by anticipation/hold/settle poses; retain the same identity, crop, palette and pixel density across variations. Within one loop, move smoothly through a single reaction instead of changing among unrelated smiles, skepticism, anger and surprise. If the requested file count exceeds the listed key beats, distribute drawn anticipation, peak and settling in-betweens across those beats; do not silently reduce the output count. Keep requested blinks while locking iris hue and underlying eye construction on open-eye frames.

Track each moving hand by anatomical side, elbow location, forearm connection and contact point in every frame. For a mouth-covering laugh, raise the same hand from chest to chin to lower mouth and lower it again while the other stays near the chest; reject a hand switch at the peak even when each isolated pose looks plausible. Transfer circled reaction marks from an expression reference as detached pixel effects, not the annotation circles or the reference's vector rendering. Preserve those effects during alpha cleanup and reserve margin for their peak extent.

For crisp pixel-art loops, redraw in-betweens from the original character, style and approved gesture references; do not use optical-flow warping, crossfades or averaged outlines, because they introduce ghost edges and inconsistent pixel clusters. Sharpening a blended frame does not restore the missing clean drawing. Inspect native-size face, fingers and hair details as well as the small contact sheet before approving consistency.

Measure the lower edge of the largest character component, not the combined bounds of steam or other detached effects, when aligning the torso baseline. Translate the complete frame by the measured offset and verify no foreground was clipped; keep intended head/shoulder bob above that stable cutoff. On cyclic motion, use a near-opening in-between rather than a duplicated endpoint unless explicitly requested, because consecutive identical endpoints add an unintended pause. When adding frames without a requested speed change, preserve total loop duration and divide the delays across the increased frame count; encode delays in GIF-compatible 10 ms units and verify the wraparound as well as the internal transitions.

When the user asks for a sheet rather than independent emoji files, change the deliverable gate: output one PNG with the exact grid, cell size, and reading order, not individual attachments.
