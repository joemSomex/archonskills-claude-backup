# Armor refinement

1. Separate character identity from pose and weapon authority, even when one attachment supplies both pose and weapon. When the user instead requests a newly designed weapon, use the pose attachment for body mechanics only and design the new prop to fit the existing shoulder, elbow and hand relationships; do not import its original gun or inherit an older weapon assignment. Keep the requested stance and grip fixed across variations; vary environment arrangement, camera and light rather than inventing actions. Preserve the character reference's panel construction when applying the pose, because copying the pose image's tiled armor silently reverses accepted refinements.
2. Tune topology and finish independently. Reduce small-square segmentation with fewer medium-large angular panels, broad chest shields, two or three abdomen lames, substantial shoulder overlaps and long limb guards. Preserve bevels, articulation, layered edges and directional brushwork; fewer plates must not become rounded blank shells. For a heavier-armored batch, first assign each variation a different massing archetype and load-bearing component graph (for example, fortress shoulders, exposed exosuit frame, low siege prow, asymmetric shield pauldron, or dorsal protection). Specify how torso width, shoulder height, limb volume, and armor layering change relative to the source; do not present minor panel, color, or lighting changes as extreme armor variations. Keep the source helmet identity, palette, and art language separate from a second image's armor-construction inspiration, or its colors and helmet can leak into the redesign. For helmet-only smoothing, consolidate crown/brow/cheek patchwork while retaining silhouette, opaque visor and sealed faceplate, leaving body topology unchanged.
3. Reduce bright fasteners, chipped-paint dots and scratch stipple without deleting permitted physical lettering, broad edge highlights or environmental lamps. Use restrained continuous gray-blue edge light and faint broad scuffs to retain painterly volume without white pinpoints.
4. Carry accepted local deletions into later replacements and regenerations. Changing armor or character must not restore removed weapon lettering, red accents or raised side modules. Preserve receiver, stock, magazine, separate foregrip and hand contacts independently of identity.
5. Describe generative percentage reductions as approximate. Removing half of a bright-component detector's candidates proves only half of those candidates were edited, not half of perceptual specks. For localized deterministic edits, protect lettering and unrelated content and verify unchanged pixels outside edit masks.
