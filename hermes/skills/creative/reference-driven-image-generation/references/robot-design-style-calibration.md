# Hard-surface redesign and material-style calibration

## Futuristic unarmed aerial product batches

1. Assign each source a bounded role (such as aerodynamic shell versus mechanical assembly). List the recognizable silhouettes and luminous motifs that must not transfer before prompting; otherwise a white recolor can reproduce a dark reference's body plan.
2. Reserve a different chassis massing, propulsion placement, and sensor arrangement per concept before generating. Make the propulsors, cooling, hinges, and optical sightlines physically legible; high-density panel lines added to a repeated frame do not create distinct designs.
3. Calibrate one complete single-machine studio image. If a requested decades-ahead platform reads as a contemporary consumer drone, change the primary shell masses and connections first, then add purposeful microdetails; more vents or glow alone cannot fix the silhouette.
4. Bind white to the broad outer shells, dark gray to joints/ducts/optics, and trace orange to named short status-light locations. Exclude any competing colored glow from the references. Use broad blunt multi-aperture camera housings rather than protruding narrow tubes so surveillance optics do not read as weapons.
5. Compare a labeled contact sheet midway and at the end for distinct silhouettes, complete uncropped single machines, clean gray studio backgrounds, color balance, and absence of copied source motifs. Count unique output files separately from visual diversity.

## Localized edits to annotated drone designs

1. Treat a red circle or arrow on a source image as a location marker, not part of the intended design. Identify the complete assembly inside it and the fixed interfaces it joins before prompting; for a shortened nose, redraw the housing, optics, light strips and tip together rather than simply removing an antenna or clipping the front edge.
2. When the user prohibits cutouts or compositing, generate each variant independently from the original annotated source with an explicit instruction to reconstruct the edited assembly and remove the markup. Do not crop, rescale or paste an old tip to simulate a structural change.
3. Compare the original and candidates side by side at a similar camera angle. Measure the changed assembly against stable landmarks such as wing roots and duct rings rather than against the image border; reject a result if only the framing changed, the marker remains, or propulsion and tail structures were lost. Deliver each accepted variant as its own image rather than a contact-sheet substitute.

## Armor segmentation and smoothing revisions

1. Separate panel topology, surface speckling, helmet curvature and rendering style before prompting. Fewer panels changes construction; matte highlights, reduced scratches or blur alone do not remove small-square segmentation. When retaining the source art style, explicitly preserve its brush-shaped shading, charcoal palette, restrained bevel highlights and textured studio backdrop while changing panel scale; smoother construction must not become glossy photoreal hardware or a blank rounded suit. Keep sparse purposeful wear distinct from random white dots, and restrict a smooth-helmet request to the helmet rather than silently smoothing all body armor.
2. Resolve the current design authority first: use the newly attached identity sheet when supplied; otherwise recover the source that governs the requested revision. Treat a rejected smoother rendition only as a comparison baseline, not as the next batch's identity source. Preserve the sealed helmet, visor coverage, silhouette, body proportions, palette, fabric joints, utility equipment and physical markings unless the user explicitly changes them.
3. For a middle-ground revision, specify medium-scale angular plates with crisp bevels and a few meaningful seams. Consolidate chest and back into several substantial panels, shoulders into two or three purposeful overlapping segments, and limb shells into long faceted sections. Exclude both dense checkerboard/scales and rounded monolithic shells; otherwise the model can swing between the two rejected extremes.
4. Reserve genuinely different construction plans for structural variants: chevron breastplate, interlocking trapezoids, broad articulated lames, central plate with modular flanks, or elongated diagonal facets. Lighting and texture may vary for finish studies, but do not count those alone as different armor constructions.
5. Apply each construction consistently to every supplied full-body view and portrait inset. Keep the source's exact rear/front/bust arrangement and framing, and repeat the same chest, shoulder and helmet seams in corresponding views; one convincing bust does not establish a consistent turnaround.
6. Generate every variant independently from the original sheet. Follow the user's delivery mode: skip unsolicited calibration renders, contact sheets and image-by-image visual checks when they opted out, while retaining file/count/format checks. If visual review is requested, compare actual plate scale and seam placement rather than assuming prompt counts were followed.

## Robot-only photoreal redesign

When replacing a rescue-action concept sheet with robot-only designs, remove the prior load, vehicles, rubble, tools, worksite, action vignette, loose-part closeups, labels, and held props from each prompt. Show only complete machine views on a seamless studio background. Reserve distinct chassis and locomotion topologies before generation; new file hashes and surface panels do not make distinct concepts. For a multi-design giant-mech batch, assign each slot a different load-bearing graph - head location, torso shell, shoulder support, arm terminal, leg root/joint count, and foot shape - then compare those at thumbnail scale before accepting it as a new design. For a four-legged platform, assign four separate chassis-corner roots and deliberately choose a slightly elevated three-quarter camera, compact enough hull, and outward-splayed leg positions to leave background gaps between hip, knee, shin and each planted foot. Trace all four complete root-to-foot chains at full size; a fourth pad peeking from under the belly is not evidence of a visible load-bearing leg. Regenerate that slot with the hidden rear leg moved outside the hull silhouette rather than accepting a plausible three-legged render. Reserve a distinct upper-body equipment/weapon topology for each concept as well as a distinct chassis and gait; changing only panel texture or camera angle does not make the requested equipment configurations different. Crop any required on-armor marking at useful size to check exact spelling and punctuation, one occurrence, and placement on the machine rather than on the background. Specify PBR material and lighting independently of scene references so their illustration or background style does not leak into the robot.

## Photoreal source to painterly animated character

1. Assign the source to identity/shape and the desired painting language to style/material; do not let a highly rendered source silently control both.
2. Calibrate one sheet before batching when a photoreal mechanical source dominates the requested animated finish. Examine the full-body armor and portrait at native size: a painted background around nearly unchanged glossy hardware is not a style conversion.
3. If an explicit painterly prompt still reproduces glossy hardware, create an **abstract character-free material guide** from the source palette and surface vocabulary (for example, matte fractured charcoal plates and painted crimson cables). Keep its backdrop light and neutral for a studio sheet, with accent-color swatches sparse and source-accurate; otherwise a dark/textured style board can hijack the output background or spread an accent across the costume. Pass it as the primary style image and the ORIGINAL source separately as an identity reference. Prompt their roles explicitly, requesting brush-shaped armor/cable shading across the body, not merely a new background. Do not use a prior generated character sheet as the identity source.
4. If the source carries a transparent protective pane that must remain intact, isolate the painterly style guide to the **opaque armor** rather than showing shattered, cracked or brush-fragmented translucent swatches: those marks can transfer as apparent glass damage. Render each pane as one continuous lightly tinted see-through surface inside its complete rim, with no branching white streaks, holes or internal segmentation. Inspect every pane and its hand grip at native size before batching; a thumbnail can disguise cracks as reflections or conceal a clipped edge.
5. Once one sample actually reads as matte sculptural animation and retains the source's distinctive helmet, markings, cables and proportions, generate the remaining independent variations from the same original and style guide. For a front-only sealed robot source, check that the requested back view is genuinely dorsal (rear helmet shell, scapula/spine armor and boot heels rather than a second front-facing chest/visor); independently count each held prop and each single-sided appendage in both views, reversing viewer-left/right when the anatomical side remains fixed. For a nonhuman turret walker, the left view must show the rear pack and antenna/cable routing, not forward-facing muzzle or launcher apertures; inventory the front aperture arrays and shield as separate structures and count each at native size in the front and detail views. Trace every chassis-corner leg from root to planted foot through the translucent field, since a blue pane can conceal a missing load path. For a source-held long device, inventory its stock/open spaces, receiver, barrel and distal tip before generation, then trace the hand-to-tip path in every full-body view at native size. Compare rear-view tip height against the source: a plausible front grip can hide a rear barrel shortened to the thigh when the source extends to the ankle. Regenerate the failed view from the original and style guide rather than using another variation as input. A front-correct sheet can silently duplicate a drape on the back or omit a held panel. Reject a shared defect before committing to a full batch; technical uniqueness alone is not visual variation.
6. Measure actual output pixels rather than assuming the image tool returned its requested aspect ratio. Keep every accepted sheet at a common canvas size: when one image is a single row short and the bottom is blank studio, pad with its matching background row rather than stretching the machine; inspect the added seam. For a mathematically exact 16:9 requirement, test `width * 9 == height * 16`. If the canvas is only a few pixels off, crop verified empty margins to a nearby `16*k` by `9*k` canvas, or pad with matching studio background when cropping would risk the subject. Resize uniformly only after the ratio is exact; resizing an approximate-ratio image directly to divisible width and height would stretch the subject. Describe merely approximate 16:9 outputs as such.
