# Style guide for messages the user sends (provisional)

Based on one real message sample. Ask the user for 3 to 5 more samples and update this file.

- Plain words. Short lines. Bullets for lists of facts.
- Lowercase openings and casual contractions are fine in Lark ("i suggest", "so we dont get marked as spam"), but keep meaning exact.
- State what you know, then what you suggest, then what you need.
- Give one clear ask and a time if there is one.
- Define any term that could be read two ways. Say "the 15-part episode cuts", not "ep 1".
- No slang, no jargon, no filler, no long intros.
- Do not sound AI-written: no "I hope this message finds you well", no stacked adjectives, no summary paragraph repeating the bullets.
- Do not use em dashes. Use commas or periods.

Example shape for a check-in question:
"quick check before i start: for the forum redesign, do you want a design mockup, or changes made on the live site? i'll also ask joem what the devs need."

## Notes from the user's real work docs
- Weekly reports and meeting notes are written in fragments. That is fine for private notes, but anything sent to Jason must be full plain sentences with owners and dates.
- Keep informal phrases out of anything a manager or the CEO might read (for example jokes or 'learn through YT university'). Say what you will do and when.
- Report status words should be consistent: Done, In progress, Pending, Waiting on [name].

## Approved examples (written or approved by the user)
Asking a colleague for information:
"Do you have a file or list or documentation of your platforms, content types, and posting days?"

Sharing a summary with the COO before it goes to a group chat:
"hi Jason, here's the social media summary. is there any additional information that i should know or edit before i forward this to the community group chat? if anything needs to change or missed let me know. thank you!"
(then the summary in short bullets, one plain question about the least certain fact, and a source line such as "source: checked the TikTok and IG pages on [date].")

Pattern: ask if something already exists before asking for work. Ask about missing information or changes to the content, not the wording. End with thanks.

## Meeting notes style (approved by the user)
- Header shape: "Sept 28, 2026 | Meeting w Jason, Joem & Edsel"
- Section labels in lowercase, short bullet lines starting with a dash.
- Use "dev bros" for Joem and Edsel together. Use their own terms as written.
- Keep the user's phrasing where it is clear. Fix only what is vague. No formal headings like "Direction and Decisions", no bold, no em dashes, no closing summary.
- Sign own tasks as "i" or "Sen", and name the owner for every task.

- Layout for short notes: header line, then one flat bullet list. No section headers unless the meeting is long. Follow with a short group-chat clarification for anything the user was unsure about, with no apology.

## Clarification questions (user's own wording, use as the model)
1. catchup posts for the global side: what do you mean by catchup posts, and what do you expect from it?
2. tiktok and ig posting: my understanding is Silvia edits the cuts and i post them on global, can confirm? also do we have a target date?

Tone: short, plain, no extra polish. "can confirm?" and "do we have a target date?" instead of formal phrasing. Only state an understanding the user actually had at the time.

## The user's own chat style (from real Lark and Telegram screenshots; the user is Johnsenn, the light-blue bubbles)
- All lowercase, no full stops at the end of lines, short messages. Splits a second thought into a second short message.
- Light shorthand in casual chat with Jason: "u", "ur", "ill", "alr", "gotchu", "yeppers", "hahaha", ":P".
- Opens a nudge with a greeting and the name: "good morning jason, nudging you for those above and the reddit banner", then adds a second question in the same message ("also for episode 07, is there anything u want me to do/prep?").
- Offers help before being asked: "ill go QA the website and the game to help the devbros".
- Owns a mistake in one plain line: "yeah i assumed, shouldve asked why". No long apology.
- Uses one small face emoji at the end of a softer or playful line, not in every message.
- Asks direct one-line questions: "jason who will make the dailies and weeklies?"
- Rule for drafts to Jason on Lark: match this voice (lowercase, short, light shorthand). Keep meaning exact and never use slang inside explanations or facts, because the COO has misunderstood slang before. Notes for the group chat follow the meeting-notes style above.
