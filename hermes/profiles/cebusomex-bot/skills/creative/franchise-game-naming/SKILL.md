# Franchise game naming

## When to Use

Use this skill when naming a new game, expansion, mode, or related product inside an established story universe; when comparing subtitles across sibling products; or when the user asks for title varieties, collision research, ranking, or a final naming recommendation.

## Procedure

1. **Map the product family first.** Read canonical product documents and list the master franchise, sibling titles, in-world vocabulary, and working labels. Keep product branding separate from canon: a sibling subtitle is not automatically an in-world procedure.
2. **Generate across distinct naming lanes.** Cover direct sibling constructions, canon-plus-conflict phrases, identity/theme phrases, institutional incident language, universe-scale events, one-word subtitles, and competitive framing only when the story supports it.
3. **Review semantics before searching.** Score canon fit, gameplay signal, sibling differentiation, memorability/pronunciation/logo use, and preliminary search differentiation. For important naming decisions, run independent canon and marketability reviews, then reconcile their reasoning rather than averaging rankings blindly.
4. **Search every serious candidate systematically.** Run exact-phrase, major-storefront, complete franchise-title, and broader brand/company queries. Batch the work and retain structured query/title/URL/description results so the longlist is reproducible.
5. **Classify collision risk.** Green means no obvious collision in preliminary searches; yellow means crowded or confusing; red means an exact or strongly established same-market collision. Exact indie-game titles still count as collisions.
6. **Verify evidence.** Open or extract the real result URL before citing it; never guess a storefront or creator slug from the title. Use the grounded-citations workflow and its rendered Sources format.
7. **Deliver a decision, not a dump.** Provide one lead, strategic alternatives, a grouped longlist, rejection reasons, and the limits of the search. Explain when deeper research changes an earlier recommendation.
8. **Wait for explicit approval.** Do not rename repositories, update canon, or persist a title as approved memory until the owner approves it.

## Always-on rules

- Do not force every sibling product into the same suffix pattern; the master franchise can establish the relationship while distinct subtitles reduce confusion.
- Do not use another fighting-game or entertainment brand in the title merely because it is a genre reference.
- Optimize simultaneously for lore, combat/product signal, pronunciation, searchability, logo treatment and announcer use.
- Treat web and storefront screening as preliminary differentiation, never trademark clearance.
- Preserve exact owner-approved canon and label generated title lore as a proposal.
