# Title research reference

## Product-family inventory

Record these before ideation:

```text
Master franchise:
Existing product titles:
Product being named:
Genre and audience:
Canonical vocabulary:
Working labels that are not canon:
Forbidden or misleading associations:
```

## Naming lanes

Generate and deduplicate candidates from at least these lanes:

1. **Direct sibling:** repeats a proven construction such as `[concept] Protocol`.
2. **Canon + pressure:** combines a signature noun with threshold, break, fracture, breach, fault, rupture, divide, escalation or state.
3. **Identity/theme:** foregrounds personhood, memory, classification, inheritance, copies or unresolved status.
4. **Institutional incident:** sounds like an alert, condition, measure, index, directive or event.
5. **Universe-scale event:** frames the conflict as a war, convergence, crisis, crucible or major incident.
6. **One-word premium:** concise and logo-friendly, but usually more crowded.
7. **Competitive/tournament:** use only if the story genuinely contains organized competition.

## Weighted score

Use a 100-point rubric:

- Canon fit: **25**
- Gameplay/genre signal: **20**
- Product-family fit without sibling confusion: **20**
- Memorability, pronunciation and logo/announcer use: **20**
- Preliminary differentiation: **15**

Scores structure judgment; they do not replace explicit reasoning or owner taste.

## Reproducible search set

Run for every serious subtitle:

```text
"<subtitle>" "video game" OR game
site:store.steampowered.com/app "<subtitle>"
site:itch.io "<subtitle>" game
"<franchise>: <subtitle>"
"<subtitle>" trademark OR brand OR company
```

Expand to Epic, PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo and mobile stores for finalists. Save query results as JSON with the query, result title, URL and description. Programmatically deduplicate and count candidates rather than counting by eye.

## Collision classification

- **Green:** no obvious exact game/store collision found. Say `no obvious collision found in preliminary searches`, never `available` or `clear`.
- **Yellow:** the root term is crowded, a close entertainment title dominates search, pronunciation is difficult, or the name misstates the genre.
- **Red:** an exact title is already used by a game, or a highly established title creates likely confusion.

A generic root-word collision can lower differentiation without automatically killing a distinctive full title. An exact subtitle collision in games should normally remove the candidate from the final shortlist.

## Evidence discipline

- Verify each URL before registering or citing it.
- Never construct a guessed URL from a search-result title.
- Keep local canon claims tied to exact source files and external collision claims tied to storefront or publisher pages.
- Render the citation helper's required Sources block instead of manually approximating its format.

## Final report structure

1. Decision summary and proposed product-family lockup.
2. Explanation of why the lead wins.
3. Evaluation criteria and search scope.
4. Ranked 10–15 finalists with score, risk and concise rationale.
5. Grouped longlist by naming lane.
6. Explicit rejection list with evidence.
7. Strategic winners: overall, action-forward, philosophical, institutional and direct sibling.
8. Suggested official title, shorthand, slug/project code and optional taglines.
9. Formal clearance checklist and clear statement that approval is still pending.

## Clearance boundary

Before final legal adoption, check relevant trademark registries, game storefronts, company/product names, domains, social handles and target-language meanings. Qualified counsel—not a web search—determines registrability and infringement risk.
